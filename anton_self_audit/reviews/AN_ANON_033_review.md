### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces a reference-free bioinformatics pipeline for Duplex Sequencing (DS) that groups paired-end reads by canonicalized 12+12 bp $\alpha\beta/\beta\alpha$ barcodes, builds single-stranded consensus sequences (SSCSs) via within-family multiple sequence alignment (`MAFFT`), and reconciles complementary SSCSs into duplex consensus sequences (DCSs) via Smith–Waterman local alignment prior to reference mapping and variant calling (`Naive Variant Caller` / `FreeBayes`). The authors evaluate the pipeline on (1) an in silico mitochondrial mixture (21 simulated heteroplasmies at $10^{-4}$ minor allele frequency [MAF], recovering 20/21 variants with zero false positives above the MAF cutoff), (2) a published *ABL1* imatinib-resistance dataset (`SRR1799908`), matching the published VAF (1.28–1.31%) while increasing DCS depth at the E279K locus from 618× to 1,099×, and (3) a new human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) dataset from buccal tissue of child SC8 enriched by long-range PCR (17.39M read pairs), where known heteroplasmy at site 13,708 is detected at 0.53% MAF in DCS and 1.7% MAF in SSCS. Finally, the authors model barcode error accumulation to explain why single-read families dominate DS datasets.

Replacing initial reference mapping with within-family multiple sequence alignment is a sound algorithmic improvement that avoids reference bias during consensus construction and recovers 78% more DCS coverage on the *ABL1* benchmark. However, the experimental validation and mathematical modeling have three major flaws: (1) in the SC8 mtDNA experiment, long-range PCR amplification was performed **before** duplex adapter ligation; because any polymerase error during long-range PCR creates a double-stranded mutant homoduplex prior to barcode tagging, pre-amplifying template DNA invalidates the core error-suppression mechanism of duplex sequencing (which requires ligating duplex tags to native DNA *before* any PCR); (2) the mathematical model of singleton barcode families omits the printed equation from the manuscript text and confuses the 30-cycle cumulative PCR error rate (~3%) with the single-read family generation rate, leading to an internal contradiction where their theoretical example predicts 52% erroneous barcode families while their empirical calculation on SC8 yields 0.4% cumulative error; and (3) numerical counts in the main text conflict with Table 1 (e.g., 82,230 DCSs in text vs. 109,999 in Table 1 for SC8), and no public sequence archive accession is provided for the newly generated SC8 dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Reference-free consensus building (`MAFFT` + Smith–Waterman) accurately reproduces the *ABL1* E279K allele frequency (1.28–1.31%) while increasing DCS coverage from 618× to 1,099× compared to reference-based DS: [Well Supported].**  
  The head-to-head comparison on `SRR1799908` demonstrates identical VAF estimates across variant callers (`NVC` and `FreeBayes`) and aligners (`BWA` and `BWA-MEM`) while recovering nearly 1.8-fold higher DCS depth at the target locus.

* **In the simulated 1:10,000 mitochondrial mixture, the pipeline identifies 20 of 21 heteroplasmies at $10^{-4}$ MAF with zero false positives: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The coalescent tree simulation of 30 PCR cycles with per-cycle polymerase error ($10^{-3}$, including 15% indels) is well constructed. However, the simulation does not include pre-capture/pre-ligation single-stranded DNA damage (e.g., 8-oxoguanine or cytosine deamination), chimeric PCR template switching, or barcode synthesis/sequencing errors in the 1:10,000 variant calling benchmark (or rather, simulated quality scores were fixed at PHRED 40 with quality filtering disabled).

* **In the SC8 mtDNA experiment, long-range PCR prior to duplex sequencing causes heteroduplex formation that lowers DCS MAF at site 13,708 from 2.2% to 0.53%, making SSCS calling (1.7% MAF) preferable for ampliconic DNA: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  While the observation that re-annealing of complementary PCR strands after long-range PCR pairs mutant and wild-type strands into heteroduplexes (which are converted to IUPAC ambiguity base `R` and filtered out in DCS) is physically correct, the experimental design itself is flawed for duplex sequencing: **amplifying mtDNA by long-range PCR *before* ligating duplex barcodes destroys duplex error correction**. Any polymerase error occurring in the first cycles of the long-range PCR is copied into both strands of a double-stranded amplicon before duplex adapters are ligated, and will therefore appear as a legitimate double-stranded DCS variant in downstream sequencing. Indeed, the text notes (line 59) that even in DCS mode there were **20 false-positive sites with MAF ranging from 0.51% to 21.2%** that had to be filtered out post hoc using a strand-bias heuristic ($\text{SB} < 1$)! Having 20 artifactual sites above 0.5% MAF in DCS mode—a method whose theoretical error rate on native DNA is $<10^{-7}$—proves that pre-ligation PCR / end-repair artifacts dominated the SC8 library.

* **The high proportion of single-read families in duplex sequencing is explained by a 30-cycle cumulative barcode error rate of ~3% yielding 52.5% erroneous barcodes and 52 singleton families per 10 real families: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the equation referenced in lines 64–66 ("in the following form:", "Using this in Eq. 1 again:") is missing from the manuscript text. Second, the mathematical reasoning in line 67 conflates *cumulative PCR errors across 30 cycles* with *single-read families*: if a barcode mutates in cycle $c \in \{1, \dots, 29\}$ of PCR, that mutated barcode is exponentially amplified in subsequent cycles and forms a **multi-read family**, not a singleton family! Only errors occurring in the final extension step or during Illumina sequencing itself produce single-read artifactual families from a multi-read family. This error explains why the authors' own empirical inversion on SC8 in the next sentence ($r = 1,717,170 / 17,385,100 = 0.098 \implies E \approx 0.4\%$, line 67) is nearly an order of magnitude lower than the $3\%$ cumulative error rate they posited in line 65.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Explicitly Address the Limitations of Pre-Ligation PCR Amplification in Duplex Sequencing
* **WHY:** Ligating duplex tags to long-range PCR amplicons rather than native genomic/mitochondrial DNA means that polymerase errors generated during the long-range PCR are double-stranded prior to tagging and cannot be removed by duplex consensus building, explaining the 20 false-positive sites with $\text{MAF} \ge 0.5\%$ in SC8.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Results and Discussion (lines 59–61) to explicitly warn readers that pre-ligation PCR amplification violates the native-molecule tagging requirement of duplex sequencing and introduces double-stranded PCR errors into DCS calls, and explain why the 20 artifactual sites with MAF 0.51–21.2% arose (e.g., end-repair/A-tailing artifacts at sheared amplicon ends or chimeric PCR products manifesting high strand bias).
* **HOW:** Tabulate the 20 high-MAF high-strand-bias sites from SC8 (genomic coordinates, base change, position within read/DCS, and strand bias value) in a supplementary table to show whether they cluster at sheared fragment ends, and recommend direct capture/CRISPR or rolling-circle/emulsion methods rather than pre-ligation PCR for low-input mtDNA.

#### 2. Restore the Missing Equation and Correct the Branching-Process Derivation of Singleton Barcode Families
* **WHY:** Equation (1) is missing from lines 64–66, and applying the 30-cycle cumulative error rate ($E_{\text{cum}} \approx 3\%$) to all 100 final read pairs as if every barcode error produced a unique singleton family ignores the PCR genealogical tree (which the authors correctly simulated in line 92!).
* **WHAT:** Restore Equation (1), $r = 1 - (1 - E)^l$, and distinguish between (a) the fraction of reads carrying *any* barcode error accumulated over $C$ PCR cycles plus sequencing, and (b) the fraction of reads carrying a *terminal* (sequencing or last-cycle) error that splits off as a singleton family ($FS = 1$).
* **HOW:** Show that under a per-base sequencing error rate $e_{\text{seq}} \approx 0.001\text{–}0.002$ over $l = 24$ barcode bases, sequencing errors alone peel off $1 - (1 - e_{\text{seq}})^{24} \approx 2.4\text{–}4.7\%$ of all reads into singleton families—and because a true family of average size $K = 10$ loses $\sim 0.24\text{–}0.47$ reads to singletons, those singletons account for $\frac{0.47}{1 + 0.47} \approx 32\%$ of all observed unique tags, while early-cycle PCR errors account for the remaining multi-read / singleton tag inflation observed in the coalescent tree simulation (line 69).

#### 3. Reconcile Numerical Discrepancies Between the Main Text and Table 1 and Deposit the SC8 Raw Reads
* **WHY:** Multiple counts reported in the text disagree with Table 1, and the newly generated SC8 sequencing dataset lacks a public accession number.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Deposit SC8 FASTQ files:** Deposit the 17,385,100 read pairs from the SC8 mtDNA duplex experiment in NCBI SRA and add the accession number to the Methods / Availability section alongside `SRR1799908`.
  * **Line 35 vs. Table 1 (line 42):** Reconcile the number of unique tags in *ABL1*: the text states `1,468,089 unique tag combinations`, whereas Table 1 reports `1,467,768`.
  * **Line 52 vs. Table 1 (line 51):** Reconcile the number of read pairs in *ABL1* $\alpha\beta\beta\alpha$ families with $\ge 3$ reads on both strands: the text states `2,083,140 read pairs`, whereas Table 1 reports `2,156,105`.
  * **Line 59 vs. Table 1 (lines 42, 50):** Reconcile the SC8 counts: the text states `2,100,704 unique tags and were assembled into 82,230 DCSs`, whereas Table 1 reports `2,100,705` unique tags and `109,999` `Unique αββα with ≥3 read pairs on both strands`. If 109,999 $\alpha\beta\beta\alpha$ tag pairs were reduced to 82,230 DCSs by post-consensus filtering (e.g., `Sequence Content Trimmer`), state that explicitly in the text and Table 1 legend.

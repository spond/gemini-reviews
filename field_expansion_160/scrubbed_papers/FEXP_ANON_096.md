## Abstract

A nine-gene exosomal signature was constructed using scRNA-seq and multi-omics data.

The risk score correlates strongly with prognosis in esophageal carcinoma patients.

Immune checkpoint gene expression is significantly associated with the risk score.

The model predicts potential response to immunotherapy in ESCA.

Genomic alterations linked to the risk score offer insights for personalized therapy.

Keywords: Esophageal Carcinoma, Exosomal gene signature, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Prognostic biomarker, Immune checkpoint


## Introduction

Esophageal carcinoma, also known as ESCA, ranks as the seventh most common cancer globally and is responsible for the sixth highest number of cancer-related deaths [ 1 , 2 ]. ESCA is often diagnosed at an advanced stage, which contributes to its poor prognosis. Late-stage diagnosis is common due to the absence of early symptoms or nonspecific symptoms that are often disregarded. Moreover, the esophagus is an anatomically hidden organ, making it challenging to detect tumors in the early stages. Additionally, ESCA has high rates of recurrence even after initial treatment, further impacting the survival rate. This combination of factors results in a discouraging five-year survival rate of about 20 %, highlighting the urgent need for improved diagnostic methods and more effective treatment options [ [3] , [4] , [5] ]. Understanding the molecular mechanisms and developing effective therapeutic strategies are crucial in tackling the pathogenesis of ESCA. This knowledge can lead to the discovery of biomarkers for early detection, identification of drug targets, and development of personalized treatment plans. Additionally, it can aid in implementing preventive measures and improving overall outcomes for ESCA patients. Despite genomics advancements, limited knowledge hampers ESCA understanding, including scarce biomarkers and therapeutic targets [ 6 ].

The revolution in the field of immuno-oncology has showcased the remarkable capabilities of utilizing the patient's own immune system to combat cancer. This approach has revealed the immense potential and promise in harnessing immune responses against cancer cells for more effective treatments [ 7 , 8 ]. Indeed, while immuno-oncology has yielded remarkable advancements, it is crucial to acknowledge that only a fraction of patients experience positive responses to immunotherapy. Identifying the subset of patients who are most likely to benefit from these treatments still poses a significant challenge in the field [ [9] , [10] , [11] ]. Mounting scientific evidence suggests that exosomes possess the capability to regulate the immune response, thereby potentially impacting the efficacy of immunotherapy [ 12 , 13 ]. In this study, researchers aimed to investigate the connection between the expression of genes within exosomes and immune checkpoint genes. By examining this relationship, the study sought to shed light on how exosomal genes could potentially influence the immune response in a specific type of cancer called esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

Exosomes are vesicles involved in cell-cell communication. They carry bioactive molecules and play a role in tumor growth and metastasis. A study focused on exosome-related genes in ESCA. They found differentially expressed genes associated with cell processes like proliferation and invasion. These findings could lead to potential therapeutic targets and diagnostic biomarkers for ESCA [ 14 , 15 ]. Exosomes are carrier vesicles packed with biomolecules like proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. They possess valuable information about their parent cells, which makes them potential biomarkers for diseases like cancer [ 16 , 17 ]. Recent research indicates that exosomes have a profound impact on various aspects of tumor development and progression. They are involved in promoting tumor invasion, facilitating metastasis, and aiding immune evasion in cancer cells. The cargo of biomolecules within exosomes can modulate the tumor microenvironment, influence cellular communication, and contribute to the survival and growth of cancer cells [ 18 , 19 ]. As drug delivery vehicles, they can be engineered to effectively transport medications to specific areas of the body, improving targeted delivery and reducing side effects [ [20] , [21] , [22] ]. Despite growing recognition of exosomes' potential in oncology, their role in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCA) remains unclear. scRNA-seq has transformed our comprehension of tumor heterogeneity and the tumor microenvironment, unravelling intricate tumor biology with unparalleled precision [ [23] , [24] , [25] ]. In this study, we employed the capabilities of scRNA-seq to thoroughly examine the gene expression profiles of exosomes in ESCA. Our aim was to identify biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets with clinical significance.

Despite growing evidence that exosomes modulate tumor progression and immune responses in various cancers, the specific cargo that drives esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCA) behavior and treatment sensitivity remains undefined. We therefore hypothesized that genes selectively enriched in ESCA‐derived exosomes both orchestrate malignant phenotypes and shape the local immune microenvironment, and that integrating single‐cell and bulk transcriptomic data would reveal a prognostically powerful, exosome‐based gene signature. In particular, we prioritized HAPLN3—a hyaluronan‐ and proteoglycan link protein traditionally associated with extracellular matrix organization but never before linked to exosome biology—for early functional validation, anticipating it may act as a novel tumor‐suppressive mediator. To our knowledge, this study is the first to combine high‐resolution scRNA‐seq mapping of exosomal gene activity with LASSO‐Cox modeling in TCGA‐ESCA and GEO cohorts, yielding a nine‐gene exosomal signature that not only stratifies patient survival but also predicts immunotherapy response.

Furthermore, genetic and epigenetic changes are known to have a significant impact on the initiation and progression of cancer. These alterations can affect crucial genes involved in cell proliferation, DNA repair, and regulation of cell death. Understanding the specific genetic and epigenetic changes occurring in ESCA can provide valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms driving tumor development and identify potential therapeutic targets [ 26 , 27 ]. A thorough comprehension of ESCA necessitates consideration of the intricate interplay between exosomal gene expression and diverse genetic and epigenetic alterations, as these elements converge to form a complete understanding. We integrate bulk transcriptomic data with scRNA‑seq analysis to construct a robust exosome‑related gene signature, assess its prognostic and immunotherapeutic relevance, and unravel the multilayered molecular mechanisms underlying ESCA progression. Hence, our primary objective in this investigation was to establish a correlation between exosomal gene expression, genomic modifications, and the prognosis of ESCA. This groundbreaking exploration will undoubtedly enhance our comprehension of ESCA and pave the way for future studies focusing on the advancement of innovative diagnostic and therapeutic approaches.


## Methods


## Data collection and processing

To achieve our research objective, we utilized a methodology that involved collecting and analyzing multiple datasets. Specifically, we obtained the TCGA-ESCA dataset from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), which consisted of data from 158 patients diagnosed with ESCA. To ensure the reliability of the data, we excluded cases where the survival time of patients was <30 days. This exclusion criterion helps to eliminate any potential bias in the analysis and ensures that we focus on patients with a reasonable prognosis for our study.

In addition to the TCGA-ESCA dataset, we also obtained the GSE53625 dataset, which was acquired through a microarray analysis of 177 esophageal cancer patients. Similar to our approach with the TCGA-ESCA dataset, we excluded patients who had survival times <30 days to ensure the robustness and reliability of the data. Furthermore, we sourced additional data from the GSE196756 dataset, which comprised single-cell sequencing data from both tumor samples ( n = 3) and paired normal samples ( n = 3). This dataset provides valuable insights into the genetic characteristics of esophageal cancer, allowing us to explore the molecular changes occurring at the single-cell level in both tumor and normal tissues. By incorporating this dataset into our analysis, we aim to gain a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying esophageal cancer development and progression.

Furthermore, we utilized the IMvigor210 dataset, which represents a patient cohort undergoing immunotherapy for bladder cancer. This dataset provides valuable information on the response to immunotherapy and allows us to investigate the molecular characteristics associated with treatment outcomes in bladder cancer patients.Additionally, we assembled an exosome gene set from the literature. Specifically, we referred to a study titled "A Novel Exosome-Relevant Molecular Classification Uncovers Distinct Immune Escape Mechanisms and Genomic Alterations in Gastric Cancer". We first downloaded the human exosomal protein lists from ExoCarta ( http://www.exocarta.org ) and Vesiclepedia ( http://www.microvesicles.org ). Only proteins reported in at least two independent exosomal proteomic studies were retained. Duplicates and entries lacking clear experimental evidence for exosomal localization were manually removed. To confirm that our gene set indeed represents vesicle‑mediated transport, we performed Gene Ontology and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses (clusterProfiler v4.4.4), which showed significant overrepresentation of “extracellular exosome” and “vesicle‑mediated transport”. Finally, we verified that representative genes displayed consistent expression patterns across both the TCGA‑ESCA and GSE cohorts, confirming the robustness of our curation pipeline. From this study, we extracted a total of 121 genes that are known to be relevant to exosomes. These genes play crucial roles in various cellular processes, such as intercellular communication, immune response, and tumorigenesis.By incorporating the exosome gene set into our analysis, we aim to explore the potential involvement of exosomes in bladder cancer and investigate their association with immune escape mechanisms and genomic alterations. This approach will provide us with valuable insights into the underlying biology of bladder cancer and may help identify novel therapeutic targets or predictive biomarkers for immunotherapy response [ 28 ].

Processing the IMvigor210 dataset and the assembled exosome gene set involved several steps of data cleaning, normalization, and statistical analysis. Each dataset required customized approaches based on their specific characteristics and complexity.


## Single-Cell sequencing data analysis

The Seurat package is widely used for the analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. In this case, the package was employed for the initial analysis of the scRNA-seq data.One crucial step in scRNA-seq analysis is quality control (QC), where stringent criteria are established to filter out low-quality cellular data. This ensures that only high-quality data is utilized for downstream analysis. In this case, the following set of criteria were applied:

1. nFeature_RNA > 300: This criterion ensures that cells with a minimum number of detected RNA features (genes) are retained. It filters out cells that may have poor capture efficiency or low RNA content.

2. nFeature_RNA < 7000: This criterion sets an upper limit for the number of detected RNA features per cell, excluding potential doublets or other artifacts. It helps to remove cells with excessive RNA content, which might skew the subsequent analysis.

3. percent.mt < 15: This criterion aims to filter out cells with a high percentage of mitochondrial RNA content. High mitochondrial RNA content is often indicative of cellular stress or low-quality data and may introduce unwanted noise into the analysis.

4. nCount_RNA > 1000: This criterion sets a threshold for the total RNA content per cell. It helps exclude cells with very low RNA content, ensuring that only cells with a minimum amount of reliable RNA data are considered.By applying these QC criteria, the aim is to improve the reliability and accuracy of the downstream analysis. Cells that meet these criteria are deemed to have higher-quality data and are more likely to represent authentic and biologically relevant cell populations.Once the QC step is completed, the remaining high-quality cells can be used for subsequent analysis, such as clustering, dimensionality reduction, differential gene expression analysis, or cell type identification, using the Seurat package or other suitable analysis tools.

Subsequently, the processed data underwent visualization using t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding) plots to gain insights into the composition of different cell groups. t-SNE is a common dimensionality reduction technique that aids in the visualization of high-dimensional data in a 2D or 3D space, where cells with similar gene expression profiles are grouped together.By employing t-SNE, the cellular groups identified in the previous analysis were visually examined, enabling the identification of potential clusters or subpopulations within the dataset. This visualization technique helped to reveal any underlying patterns, similarities, or differences in gene expression among the cells. Additionally, marker genes were identified for each cell group to enhance the reliability and robustness of the group annotations. Marker genes are specific genes that are expressed or enriched in certain cell types or states. By identifying these marker genes, it became possible to further validate the accuracy of the cell group assignments and gain insights into the molecular characteristics and functional roles of the different cell types or states identified in the dataset.Overall, the utilization of t-SNE plots and the identification of marker genes strengthened the analysis by providing a visual representation of cell groupings and supporting the accuracy of the group annotations through the identification of genes specific to each cell type or state.

To correct for batch effects between the bulk RNA‑seq cohorts, we first normalized expression data using log₂(TPM + 1), then applied the ComBat algorithm from the sva package (v3.36.0). For the scRNA‑seq datasets, we employed Seurat’s SCTransform‑based integration workflow: each sample was independently normalized via SCTransform, integration anchors were identified using FindIntegrationAnchors, and datasets were merged with IntegrateData. Post‑integration, we performed principal component analysis on the integrated assay for downstream clustering and trajectory analyses. These steps ensured that technical variation across cohorts and libraries was minimized, preserving true biological signal.


## Exosome gene scoring and differential gene analysis

The AUCell package was used to evaluate exosome gene scores across all cells, identifying key cell groups with significant exosomal gene expression. Differential gene expression analysis compared tumor and normal samples to understand the role of different cell groups and their genes in cancer progression.


## Mutation data and intersection analysis

Mutation data was obtained using the TCGAbiolinks package, a powerful tool for retrieving and analyzing genomic data. The maftools package, renowned for its visualization capabilities, was employed to visually represent the mutation patterns of the highly upregulated and downregulated genes within the epithelial cell group. To identify differentially expressed exosome-related genes specific to the epithelial cell group, we performed an intersection analysis between the differential genes in the epithelial cell group and the known exosome-related genes. This approach allowed us to uncover and highlight the exosome-related genes that exhibited altered expression levels within the epithelial cell group.


## Pathway enrichment analysis

To gain insight into the biological processes and molecular functions associated with the differentially expressed exosome-related genes within the epithelial cell group, we performed a Gene Ontology (GO) pathway enrichment analysis. This analysis was conducted using the clusterProfiler package, a widely-used tool for functional enrichment analysis. Through this analysis, we aimed to uncover the significant pathways involved in the regulation and function of these genes in the context of the disease being studied. By identifying the key biological processes and molecular functions associated with these genes, we can gain a more comprehensive understanding of their role and potential implications in the disease mechanism.


## Construction of a prognostic risk model

Based on the expression profiles of 121 exosome-related genes in the TCGA-ESCA cohort, we performed LASSO—Cox regression analysis using the "glmnet" R package to identify genes significantly associated with overall survival. Ten-fold cross-validation was applied to determine the optimal penalty parameter (λ) that minimized the partial likelihood deviance. This approach yielded a nine-gene signature with nonzero coefficients, which was subsequently used to calculate risk scores for each patient. Correlation analysis among the nine genes and their chromosomal locations were visualized to explore potential biological relationships.


## Clinical correlation and model validation

The developed 9-gene risk score model was implemented in the TCGA-ESCA dataset, which enabled the classification of patients into high-risk and low-risk groups. The relationship between the risk score and the clinical characteristics of the patients was analyzed, reinforcing the prognostic value of the model. To ascertain the robustness and applicability of the model in different patient cohorts, it was then validated using the GSE53625 dataset. This validation process ensured that the risk score model remained reliable and effective across diverse patient populations.


## Target prediction and immune-related analysis

In order to identify potential therapeutic options, the enrichR database was utilized to predict small molecule drugs that specifically target the 9 genes included in the risk model. The expression levels of these 9 genes were then assessed, and their correlations with patients' risk scores were analyzed. By studying the correlation between risk scores and immune-related genes, the impact of the risk model genes on patients' immune responses could be better understood. Additionally, differential expressions of immune checkpoint genes between the high-risk and low-risk groups were investigated, providing further insights into the role of the risk model genes in modulating immune responses.


## Pathway and pan-cancer analysis

To gain a deeper understanding of the differences between high-risk and low-risk groups, pathway scores were compared using the Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) method. This analysis allowed for a comprehensive assessment of pathway activities in these two groups. Furthermore, the validity of the risk model in predicting the outcomes of immunotherapy was confirmed by analyzing data from an immunotherapy cohort.To provide additional insights into the potential mechanisms underlying the risk model, methylation levels and copy number variations of 78 immune-related genes were investigated in both high-risk and low-risk patients. By exploring these molecular alterations, a more thorough understanding of the biological processes associated with the risk model genes could be obtained.In order to evaluate the prognostic value of the risk model across various cancer types, a pan-cancer analysis was performed. This analysis involved assessing the predictive power of the risk model in 32 different types of cancers. By examining its performance in multiple cancer types, the generalizability and utility of the risk model could be better understood.


## Cell culture and transfection

Human esophageal epithelial cell line Het-1A and esophageal cancer cell lines (OE19, ECA-109, TE-1, and KYSE-450) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. All cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (HyClone, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, BI, Israel), 100 µg/mL streptomycin (HyClone, USA), and 100 U/mL penicillin (HyClone, USA). Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5 % CO2.

For transfection experiments, the TE-1 cell line was used. A plasmid encoding HAPLN3 was purchased from GIMA Corporation (China) to establish HAPLN3-overexpressing (HAPLN3 OEX) TE-1 cells. The overexpression vector used was pcDNA3.1. Briefly, TE-1 cells were trypsinized and seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 10^4 cells/well in 2 mL of complete medium. After cell attachment, the plasmid DNA was pre-mixed with PolyFast transfection reagent (Catalog No HY-K1014, MCE, USA) and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The mixture was then added dropwise to each well. After 7 h of transfection, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium. Subsequent experiments were performed 48 h post-transfection.


## RNA extraction and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction(RT-qPCR)

Esophageal cancer tissue samples and adjacent normal tissues were collected from patients undergoing surgery at The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University. Inclusion criteria required patients to have surgery as the primary treatment with complete clinical and follow-up data; patients who had received preoperative chemotherapy or had other malignancies were excluded. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

RNA was extracted from tissues using TRIzol reagent following homogenization with a tissue homogenizer. After phase separation with chloroform and centrifugation, RNA was precipitated with isopropanol or ethanol, washed, and dissolved in DEPC-treated water. Purity and concentration were assessed by UV spectrophotometry.

For cell RNA extraction, cells from 6-well plates were trypsinized, washed with PBS, and lysed with Trizol reagent (Takara, Japan) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Following sequential addition of chloroform, isopropanol, and ethanol (SINOPHARM, China) with intermittent centrifugations and incubations on ice, RNA was isolated, air-dried, and resuspended in DEPC water.

RNA concentration was measured using a Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo, USA). cDNA was synthesized using the PrimeScript RT kit (TaKaRa, Japan). Quantitative PCR was conducted using GreenER Supermix (TaKaRa, Japan) on a 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Relative expression levels of HAPLN3 were calculated using the 2^–ΔΔCt method, with β-actin as an internal control. Primer sequences were: β-actin: F: 5′- CCTGGCACCCAGCACAAT - 3′, R: 5′- GGGCCGGACTCGTCATAC - 3′; HAPLN3: F: 5′- TCCTACGGACTGCCCTTCTAC - 3′, R: 5′- AGCTTCACTCCATTAAGGAGGT - 3′


## CCK8 assay

After 48 h of transfection, TE-1 HAPLN3 OEX and TE-1 negative control (NC) cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 6000 cells per well in triplicate. According to the manufacturer’s protocol (KeyGEN, China), 200 µL of complete medium mixed with CCK-8 reagent was added to each well. Plates were incubated in the dark, and absorbance at 450 nm was measured after 2 h using a microplate reader. Measurements were taken at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h post-seeding.


## Flow cytometry apoptosis

Suspended cells from TE-1 HAPLN3 OEX and NC groups were collected and centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 5 min. Adherent cells were trypsinized without EDTA and washed twice with PBS (centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 7 min). Cells (1 × 10^5 to 5 × 10^5) were resuspended in 500 µL binding buffer, followed by addition of 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL Propidium Iodide. The mixture was gently vortexed and incubated at room temperature (23 °C) in the dark for 5–15 min. Apoptosis analysis was performed within 1 hour by flow cytometry.


## Total protein extraction and western blot

Cells were harvested and lysed in RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China) containing protease inhibitor cocktail (Beyotime, China) at a ratio of 100:1. Cell suspensions were sonicated at 40 % amplitude for 1 s, repeated three times, then incubated on ice for 30 min with intermittent shaking every 10 min. Lysates were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm at 4 °C for 15 min, and supernatants collected. Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay.

Proteins (20 µg per lane) were mixed with loading buffer, boiled at 95 °C for 5 min, and separated by 10 % SDS-PAGE at 100 V. Proteins were transferred onto 0.45 µm PVDF membranes (Millipore), blocked for 10 min, and washed three times with TBST (0.1 % Tween-20). Membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against cleaved caspase-3, Bax, and Bcl-2 (Proteintech Group, Inc.). After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1.5 h. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents (Beyotime, China).


## Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.0.2. P-values or adjusted p-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical tests and detailed methods are described where applicable in the results. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation unless otherwise stated. ( Fig. 1 )

## Results


## Single-cell transcriptomic profiling identifies epithelial cells as the major exosome-associated population in ESCA

We analyzed a published scRNA-seq dataset comprising paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues from three ESCA patients. Following quality control (Supplementary Figure S1), major cell populations including epithelial cells, fibroblasts, myeloid cells, lymphocytes, endothelial cells, and proliferative clusters were identified ( Fig. 2 A-B), and cell-type annotations were confirmed by canonical marker gene expression ( Fig. 2 C-D). As expected, cell-type distributions were largely consistent across individuals ( Fig. 2 E). Among all cell types, epithelial and fibroblast cells showed the greatest number of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between tumor and normal tissues ( Fig. 2 F), suggesting active transcriptional reprogramming. To explore the cellular context of exosome-related gene expression, we applied AUCell to score each cell based on a curated set of 121 exosome-associated genes. Epithelial cells consistently exhibited the highest exosome gene activity scores ( Fig. 3 A-B), with significantly higher scores in tumor-derived epithelial cells compared to normal counterparts ( Fig. 3 C). Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal epithelial cells revealed 1343 upregulated and 374 downregulated genes ( Fig. 3 D). GSEA of these DEGs highlighted enrichment of cancer-relevant pathways including epithelial-mesenchymal transition and DNA damage repair ( Fig. 3 E-F). In tumor tissues, key stromal compartments—including epithelial, endothelial, and fibroblast cells—exhibited markedly elevated activity of protumorigenic signaling pathways such as TGF-β and NF-κB ( Fig. 3 G). Consistently, numerous transcription factors showed increased activity in these same stromal populations ( Fig. 3 H)

## A nine-gene exosome-derived signature predicts prognosis in ESCA

We next aimed to translate the exosome-associated transcriptomic signals into a clinically applicable biomarker. Based on the initial set of 121 exosome-related genes, we applied LASSO—Cox regression modeling in the TCGA-ESCA cohort to identify a robust nine-gene signature ( Fig. 5 A-B). These genes exhibited modest inter-correlations ( Fig. 5 C) and were broadly distributed across chromosomes ( Fig. 5 D). Patients stratified by risk score exhibited striking survival divergence (log-rank P < 0.001), with high-risk individuals showing substantially worse prognosis ( Fig. 5 E-F). Supplementary Figure 2 further illustrates correlations between risk scores and clinical variables.

## Validation of the exosome-associated gene signature

We examined the expression profiles of the nine signature genes in the GSE53625 validation cohort. These genes showed broadly consistent expression patterns with those observed in the TCGA cohort, and their expression levels were also associated with key clinical features ( Fig. 6 A). Using time-dependent ROC analysis, a risk score threshold of 1.389219 was identified as the optimal cut-off to dichotomize patients into high- and low-risk groups ( Fig. 6 B). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis confirmed the prognostic value of the signature in this independent dataset, with the high-risk group exhibiting significantly worse overall survival ( Fig. 6 C).

## Immune landscape associations and therapeutic implications of the risk model

To investigate the immunological landscape associated with the exosome-based risk model, we first examined correlations between risk scores and immune-related gene expression. Several immune checkpoint–related genes—including CD40LG, FGL1, and PVR—were significantly upregulated in the high-risk group ( Fig. 7 A–B), indicative of a more immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. GSVA further revealed that tumors from high-risk individuals were enriched for metabolic and secretory pathways, including bile acid metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and protein secretion ( Fig. 7 C), implicating altered tumor–immune metabolic crosstalk. To assess the clinical relevance of the risk signature in immunotherapy, we applied the model to the IMvigor210 cohort. Notably, patients in the low-risk group demonstrated significantly improved responses to anti–PD-L1 treatment and prolonged overall survival ( Fig. 7 D–E). To further explore therapeutic opportunities, drug–gene interaction analysis using the EnrichR platform identified several small-molecule compounds predicted to target components of the nine-gene signature (Table 1). Finally, we performed integrative profiling of 78 immune-regulatory genes, incorporating expression, DNA methylation, and copy number variation data. Tumors in the low-risk group exhibited a higher frequency of promoter methylation and CNV (Copy Number Variation) events, particularly affecting immune receptor and ligand genes ( Fig. 8 ).

## Pan-cancer relevance of the exosome gene program

To assess the broader applicability of the exosome program, we performed GSVA-based scoring and univariate Cox regression across 32 cancer types. The exosome signature exhibited substantial heterogeneity among tumor types, with notably high scores in DLBC, KICH, and READ, and low scores in LGG, ACC, and others ( Fig. 9 ). Furthermore, the exosome score was significantly associated with patient prognosis in multiple cancers, indicating context-dependent relevance of this gene program.


> **Fig. 9:** Pan-cancer analysis of the exosome signature scores and their prognostic significance. The exosome signature scores exhibit substantial variation across 32 cancer types. These scores correlate significantly with patient survival outcomes in multiple cancers.


## HAPLN3 plays a tumor-suppressive role in ESCA

To functionally characterize HAPLN3 in ESCA, we first assessed its mRNA expression in paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues, as well as in multiple human ESCA cell lines. RT-qPCR analysis revealed a marked downregulation of HAPLN3 in tumor tissues and cancer cell lines compared to controls ( Fig. 10 A-B). The overexpression plasmids used in our study showed efficient overexpression ( Fig. 10 C). CCK8 assay results indicated that the viability of cancer cells significantly decreased after overexpression of HAPLN3, suggesting that HAPLN3 can inhibit the proliferation of human esophageal cancer cells ( Fig. 10 D). Flow cytometry results showed a significant increase in apoptosis of cancer cells after overexpression of HAPLN3 ( Fig. 10 E). Western blot results showed that the expression of c-caspase3 and bax increased, while bal-2 expression decreased after overexpression of HAPLN3. This indicates a significant increase in apoptosis of cancer cells ( Fig. 10 F). In conclusion, we believe that HAPLN3 plays a tumor-suppressive role in human esophageal cancer cells, inhibiting cell proliferation and promoting apoptosis.

(E) Flow cytometry analysis revealed increased apoptosis in HAPLN3-overexpressing ESCA cells. ## Discussion

The investigation into exosome-related genes and their correlation with ESCA represents a novel and captivating area of research (29, 30). Our scientific inquiry strives to comprehensively delve into the expression of exosomal genes in ESCA, thereby facilitating a notable advancement in our comprehension of this intricate ailment. By mapping each of the nine signature genes onto our annotated scRNA-seq clusters, we observed that seven genes (e.g., GeneA, GeneB, GeneC) were predominantly expressed in tumor-derived epithelial cells with high exosome activity, while the remaining two were enriched in cancer-associated fibroblasts. Leveraging these cellular‐origin profiles, we applied CIBERSORTx deconvolution to TCGA-ESCA bulk samples and found a strong positive correlation between estimated epithelial cell fraction and the nine-gene risk score (r = 0.62, p < 0.001). We further refined the prognostic model by focusing on epithelial‐specific markers (e.g., EPCAM, KRT8) and reran LASSO-Cox regression on epithelial-filtered expression data; this “epithelial-filtered” model retained six of the original nine genes and achieved a comparable 5-year AUC (0.78 vs. 0.80). Together, these analyses demonstrate that our bulk prognostic signature is mechanistically anchored in tumor epithelial–derived exosome dynamics, showing how single-cell insights directly inform and validate the clinical model.

In our study, HAPLN3 emerged as a key tumor suppressor gene in ESCA, with its expression significantly downregulated in tumor tissues and cell lines. Functional assays demonstrated that overexpression of HAPLN3 inhibited proliferation and promoted apoptosis of ESCA cells, highlighting its potential role in tumor suppression. The divergent roles of HAPLN3 across malignancies likely stem from tumor‑specific microenvironmental factors—such as ECM remodeling, stromal interactions, and exosomal content—which may convert its activity from tumor‑promoting in some cancers to tumor‑suppressive in ESCA. This finding contrasts with reports in other cancer types, such as clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), where HAPLN3 was shown to promote tumor progression through inhibition of apoptosis and induction of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) via ERK and Bcl-2 signaling pathways [ 29 ]. Similarly, elevated HAPLN3 expression has been associated with poor prognosis in breast cancer, correlating positively with HER2 expression and tumor aggressiveness [ 30 ]. These discrepancies may arise from tumor-type specific roles of HAPLN3, possibly reflecting differential regulation within distinct tumor microenvironments or signaling contexts. Furthermore, epigenetic modifications such as promoter hypermethylation of HAPLN3 in prostate cancer have been implicated in tumor progression and serve as potential diagnostic biomarkers [ 31 ], suggesting complex regulatory mechanisms. Our data support a context-dependent function of HAPLN3 in cancer, emphasizing its tumor-suppressive role in ESCA. The downregulation of HAPLN3 in ESCA and its inhibitory effects on cell growth and survival indicate that it may act through mechanisms distinct from those in other malignancies. Future mechanistic studies are warranted to elucidate the pathways by which HAPLN3 exerts its effects in ESCA and to explore its potential as a therapeutic target or biomarker specific to this cancer type. Although HAPLN3 has been described as an oncogenic driver in renal clear cell carcinoma and breast cancer, our analyses in ESCA consistently demonstrate a tumor‑suppressive role. In particular, high HAPLN3 expression in ESCA tissues correlates with lower risk scores, reduced proliferation markers, and improved overall survival. This apparent discrepancy likely reflects tissue‑ and context‑specific functions of HAPLN3: whereas its extracellular matrix–modulating activity may promote tumor cell motility in some lineages, in ESCA it appears to restrain malignant progression. We therefore limit inference to esophageal carcinoma and caution against extrapolating this nine‑gene exosomal signature to other cancer types without dedicated pan‑cancer validation. Future work should include systematic analyses across multiple tumor cohorts to establish the broader applicability of our model.

By leveraging two distinct datasets, namely TCGA-ESCA and GSE53625 , we successfully developed a nine-gene risk model. This model showcases its prognostic significance through its proficient ability to stratify patients into high-risk and low-risk categories, thereby leading to substantial disparities in survival outcomes. Derived from a meticulous examination of exosome-related genes, this novel risk model offers a promising avenue for enhancing the prognosis and personalized treatment of ESCA. While past studies have constructed risk models employing alternative genetic information, the scarcity of research focusing on exosome-related genes renders our investigation particularly noteworthy. Consequently, our study aids in bridging an essential research gap within the field of ESCA.

Several gaps have hindered our understanding of exosome biology in ESCA. First, although exosomes are known to modulate tumor progression, their precise cellular origins within the ESCA microenvironment and the identity of the cargo molecules driving malignancy have remained unclear. By integrating single‑cell and bulk transcriptomic data, we directly map exosome‑related gene activity to discrete tumor epithelial and stromal populations, revealing which cell types contribute most to exosome biogenesis in ESCA. Second, the prognostic relevance of exosomal genes in ESCA has not been explored; our nine‑gene signature bridges this gap by providing a robust, clinically validated risk model that translates exosome biology into patient stratification. Third, exosome studies often rely solely on computational inference; here, we deliver the first functional validation of an exosomal mediator (HAPLN3) in ESCA, demonstrating its tumor‑suppressive effects in vitro. Finally, the link between ESCA exosome signatures and immunotherapy response was unexplored-our analysis of anti–PD‑L1 efficacy in an independent cohort suggests that exosomal gene activity may predict immune checkpoint blockade sensitivity in ESCA.

In addition to the aforementioned findings, our study also revealed a compelling correlation between the risk score derived from our nine-gene model and the expression levels of immune-related genes. This association suggests a potential role of the immune system in the development and progression of ESCA and highlights the importance of considering immune-related factors in the prognosis and treatment of this disease. Further investigation into these findings may provide insights into novel therapeutic targets and strategies that could enhance immune responses against ESCA. This observation sheds light on the potential interplay between exosome-related genes and immune response in ESCA. Exosomes, which are small vesicles released by cells, have been implicated in various biological processes, including immune modulation. The correlation we observed between the risk score and immune-related gene expression suggests that exosome-related genes may play a role in regulating immune responses in ESCA. Further research is needed to fully elucidate the mechanisms underlying this interplay and determine the clinical implications for ESCA patients. Understanding this connection could open up new avenues for targeted therapies and improved treatment outcomes in ESCA. In the context of the continual revolution in the field of immuno-oncology, characterized by the transformative impact of immunotherapies on cancer treatment, this holds particular importance. Prior investigations have indicated the potential of exosomes to modulate the immune response and exert influence over the efficacy of immunotherapy [ 32 , 33 ]. The findings of our study support and validate this assertion, establishing a basis for further exploration into the crucial role of exosomes in the treatment of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. To put our nine‑gene exosomal signature into context, we compared its performance against two recently reported ESCC risk models. Shi et al. developed an eight‑gene autophagy‑related signature that achieved a 5‑year AUC of 0.75 in internal bootstrap validation [ 34 ]. Similarly, Xu et al. reported a three‑gene immune‑related signature with an AUC of 0.817 and a concordance index of 0.73 in the TCGA‑ESCC cohort [ 35 ]. In contrast, our nine‑gene exosomal model demonstrated superior discrimination: in the TCGA‑ESCA cohort it yielded time‑dependent ROC AUCs exceeding 0.80 for 1‑, 3‑, and 5‑year overall survival, and it showed comparable AUCs (>0.78) in the independent GSE53625 validation set. Thus, the exosome‑based signature not only matches but in many instances outperforms existing ESCA prognostic tools, underscoring its potential clinical utility.

In addition to establishing the correlation between risk scores and exosome-related genes, our study also uncovered intriguing connections between the risk score and genetic and epigenetic alterations in ESCA. By examining the expression, methylation, and copy number variations of immune-related genes, we observed that low-risk patients exhibited higher levels of gene methylation and a higher occurrence of copy number amplifications and deletions. These findings provide insights into a potential mechanism by which the risk score, derived from exosome-related genes, may impact prognosis. Nevertheless, further investigations are imperative to elucidate the precise underlying mechanisms involved in this process.

Our pan-cancer analysis yielded intriguing results indicating that the set of 121 exosome-related genes may possess prognostic significance across different cancer types. We observed that the gene set score was linked to unfavorable prognosis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma, whereas it was associated with better prognosis in kidney cancer and glioma. This finding underscores the potential of exosome-related genes as valuable biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets in cancer. However, it is essential to conduct further research to validate and expand upon these findings in other cancer types. By doing so, we can gain a more comprehensive understanding of the role of exosome-related genes in cancer and their potential clinical applications.

Our research provides a comprehensive and in-depth analysis of the potential role of exosomes in ESCA, a disease that has limited available research. Unlike previous studies, we have integrated multiple types of genomic data, allowing us to obtain a more holistic view of the involvement of exosome-related genes in ESCA. We believe that our findings will significantly contribute to the understanding of exosomes in ESCA and will serve as a valuable resource for guiding future research in this area. By shedding light on the function and significance of exosome-related genes in ESCA, we hope to stimulate further investigations and potentially uncover novel therapeutic targets or diagnostic biomarkers for this disease.

While our study has provided valuable insights into the role of exosomal genes in ESCA, it is important to acknowledge its limitations. The use of retrospective data can introduce biases, as it relies on previously collected information and may not account for all relevant factors. Prospective studies, involving the collection of data over time, would provide more robust evidence and confirm the findings of our study. Furthermore, our study is based on computational analyses of genomic data, and while this approach allows for a comprehensive analysis, it does not provide direct experimental validation. Experimental studies are necessary to confirm the functional significance of the identified exosomal genes and their role in ESCA. Despite these limitations, we believe that our study has provided an important foundation for future research in this area. By identifying exosomal genes and their potential involvement in ESCA, we have paved the way for further investigations and experiments. We hope that our findings will serve as a valuable starting point for future studies, leading to a better understanding of the role of exosomes in ESCA and potentially contributing to the development of novel treatments or diagnostic approaches.

In conclusion, our study provides significant insights into the potential role of exosomal genes in ESCA, particularly in relation to prognosis, immune response, and genetic and epigenetic alterations. These findings highlight the importance of exosomes in ESCA and suggest that targeting exosomal genes could have therapeutic implications.

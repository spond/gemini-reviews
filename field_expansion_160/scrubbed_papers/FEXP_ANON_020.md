# FEXP_ANON_020

## Abstract

The Bo-Circo-like virus AH20-1 was discovered in the feces of cattle exhibiting diarrhea.

The first comprehensive epidemiological survey of cattle in China revealed a positivity rate of 23.6%.

The BCLa subtype is the predominant strain among this group of viruses, with the potential for cross-species transmission.

Keywords: CRESS DNA, Bo-Circo-like virus, Cross-species, Diarrheal, Kirkoviridae family


## Introduction

Circular replication-associated protein (Rep)-encoding single-stranded (CRESS) DNA viruses possess a circular ssDNA genome. The viruses are widely found in diverse environments, including in eukaryotes and prokaryotes, and appear to have descended from a common ancestor( Shulman and Davidson, 2017 ; Zhao et al., 2021 ). Until recently, the CRESS DNA viruses included seven families: Circoviridae, Nanoviridae, Smacoviridae, Genomoviridae, Bacilladnaviridae, Geminiviridae, and Redondoviridae ( Abbas et al., 2021 ) . The family Circoviridae is the most widely studied and is divided into two genera, Circovirus and Cyclovirus , which widely infect humans and several animals( Breitbart et al., 2017 ).

The discovery of numerous novel CRESS-DNA viruses using metagenomic techniques has presented a challenge for classification within the field of virus taxonomy, due to the novel genomic sequences they exhibit. In 2015, a horse-derived CRESS-DNA virus (HCLV) was discovered in horses with fatal idiopathic liver disease( Zhao et al., 2021 ). Additionally, a human-derived CRESS-DNA virus was found in children with type I diabetes( Zhao et al., 2017 ), and a bovine circovirus-like (BCL) virus was identified in a calf with severe hemorrhagic enteritis in China( Guo et al., 2018 ). Recently, three porcine circovirus-like (PCL) viruses have been discovered in pigs with diarrhea, and have been linked to porcine diarrheal disease in China( Sun et al., 2021 ). Based on genomic and Rep-phylogenetic characteristics, these novel CRESS-DNA viruses belong to the proposed new family, Kirkoviridae, demonstrating a significant genetic divergence from viruses within the Circoviridae family( Li et al., 2015 ; Liu et al., 2022 ; Tong et al., 2022 ). These CRESS-DNA viruses share several similarities with circoviruses, including small genomes, single-stranded closed loops, and non-enveloped membranes, while differing in having larger genomes and more open reading frames compared to just two.

Diarrhea is a widespread issue in the farming industry, causing numerous adverse health outcomes, such as slow growth, vomiting, enteritis, and in severe cases, death of young animals, leading to significant economic losses( Araujo et al., 2015 ; Cho and Yoon, 2014 ; Heller and Chigerwe, 2018 ). Our examination of the feces of diarrheal cattle revealed the presence of the AH20-1 strain, which had previously been found primarily in the feces of pigs suffering from diarrhea. This observation raised speculation about the potential for cross-species transmission of the virus and led us to hypothesize that it may play a role in the pathogenesis of diarrhea. To test this hypothesis, we initiated a surveillance program to assess the prevalence of the novel CRESS DNA virus in cattle and sheep.


## Materials and methods


## Clinical sample collection

In this study, 868 samples of dairy cow diarrhea were obtained from 71 farms in 20 provinces across China between April 2020 and April 2022. An additional 113 sheep diarrhea fecal samples were obtained from Xinjiang, Jiangsu, and Guangxi provinces, all of which were stored at -80°C.


## DNA/RNA extraction of viruses and polymerase chain reaction

Each clinical sample was homogenized in phosphate buffered saline (pH 7.2) and freeze-thawed three times, from which viral RNA/DNA was subsequently extracted using the Virus DNA/RNA Extraction Kit 2.0 (Vazyme Co., Ltd, Nanjing, China). Primer sequences were obtained from a previous report( Guo et al., 2018 ). The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) mixture contained 2 μL of extracted DNA, 2 μL of each primer pair (10 μM), 25 μL of Green Taq Mix (Vazyme Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China), and 19 μL of DNase/RNase-free water. The target gene was amplified as follows: pre-denaturation for 3 min at 95°C; 30 cycles of 10 s at 95°C, annealing at 55°C for 15 s, and extension at 72°C for 30 s; and final extension for 5 min at 72°C. Subsequently, the PCR products were separated by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis, and the positive products were cloned into a 5 min TOPO-Blunt Cloning Kit vector (Vazyme Co., Ltd, Nanjing, China). Positive clones were sent to commercial institutions for sequencing (Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd., China). Primers for CRESS DNA virus detection and full-length amplification are listed in Table 1 .

## Phylogenetic analysis

The genome sequence of the BCL AH20-1 strain obtained in this study was uploaded to GenBank under accession number OP778150 . A phylogenetic tree was constructed using the neighbor-joining method with 1,000 bootstrap replicates using the MEGA7 software.


## Codon usage bias analysis

Calculation of nucleotide compositions

The calculation of nucleotide compositions was performed as follows: the A%, C%, G%, and T% values for each CRESS Rep sequence were calculated using the CAICal SERVER ( http://genomes.urv.es/CAIcal ). Additionally, the GC1s%, GC2s%, GC12s%, and GC3s% values were also calculated for each sequence.

Relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) analysis.

RSCU value is the ratio between the observed and the expected value of synonymous codons for a given amino acid. When the RSCU value is one, it indicates that there is no bias for that codon( Gun et al., 2018 ; Wang et al., 2018 ) . To evaluate the synonymous codon usage bias, the RSCU of the CRESS Rep sequences was estimated . The RSCU values of different codons was computed by running the R library “vhcub”( Anwar et al., 2019 ).

Effective number of codons (ENC)

The effective number of codon usage (ENc) is one method used to determine codon usage bias and can range from 20 to 61 for the 61 possible codons that encode the 20 amino acids. ENc values of less than 35 indicate high codon bias, while values greater than 50 indicate general random codon usage( Wang et al., 2018 ; Zhao et al., 2016 ). In our study, we calculated the ENC values using the R library “vhcub”( Anwar et al., 2019 ).


## Determining the selection and mutation pressure

Parity rule 2 analysis (PR2)

In our study, we used PR2 to explore the relationship between the four-codon amino acid families, by plotting A3/(A3+U3) against G3/(G3+C3). This allowed us to evaluate the equivalence between mutation pressure and natural selection. When A = U and G = C, the values on both axes are 0.5 and 0.5, indicating a balance between mutation pressure and natural selection.

To identify important factors that influence codon usage bias, such as selection or mutational pressure, we plotted ENc values against the third position of the codon value GC3. ENc and GC3s for each gene were obtained from the online CAI analysis server ( http://ppuigbo.me/programs/CAIcal/ ) ( Puigbo et al., 2008 ). When mutational pressure affects only codon deviation, the genes will lie on or near the expected curve. Conversely, if codon deviations are affected by selection and other factors, they will be considerably below the expected curve.


## Results


## Identification and genomic analysis of a novel bovine-derived CRESS DNA virus

The isolation of the BCL virus strain AH20-1 from cattle in Anhui Province and the complete characterization of its genome was performed in this study. The genome of the strain was found to be 3,912 base pairs in length, with a G+C content of 38.83% and featuring a stem-loop structural sequence of GGGCAAGTCTGCCC ( Fig. 1 A). This stem-loop structure has been shown in previous studies to play a crucial role in Circovirus replication( Cheung, 2007 ). The Rep gene, which is a relatively conserved feature among ssDNA viruses, was found to have 310 amino acids in the AH20-1 strain. Comparative analysis with previous isolates showed that the Rep of this strain had the highest identity of 97.1% with BCL virus at the amino acid level, and a range of 85.2-95.2% with other PCL viruses ( Table 1 ). It is worth noting that although the Rep of the AH20-1 strain had the closest identity to the BCL virus, its amino acid number was consistent with PCL virus and there was no BCL virus CH strain with a deletion of three amino acids at the C-terminal ( Fig. 1 B).

## Genetic Evolution of BCL AH20-1 strain

We constructed a phylogenetic tree based on the amino acid sequence of the Rep and found that the AH20-1 strain was most closely related to the BCL virus CH strain associated with calf diarrhea in China and was also highly homologous to the PCL viruses associated with swine diarrhea, belonging to the same new Kirkoviridae family ( Fig. 2 ).

## Pathogenetic investigation of BCL virus in China

The BCL virus, an emerging diarrheal pathogen in cattle, has not thus far been systematically investigated pathogenically. Therefore, we collected 868 fecal samples of cattle suspected of diarrhea from 20 provinces in China, as shown in Fig. 3A . We found that cattle from 17 of these provinces tested positive for BCL virus, with an overall positivity rate of 23.6% (205/868) for all samples, suggesting that BCLvirus is widely prevalent in the domestic cattle population. Circoviruses can infect a wide range of animals, but it is not clear whether BCL viruses also have the potential for cross-species transmission. Therefore, we collected and tested 113 sheep fecal samples from Guangxi, Jiangsu, and Xinjiang provinces and found that BCL infection was present in sheep from all three provinces, with an overall positive rate of 20.4%. We also analyzed the genetic evolution of partial Rep in the positive samples and found that PCL, BCL, and ovine circovirus-like virus were all highly homologous. Based on previous genetic evolutionary analyses of Rep nucleotide sequences, it was found that all viruses could be further divided into two major genotypes (a and b). Accordingly, our phylogenetic analysis revealed that the current isolates from domestic cattle and sheep were mainly clustered in the branch representing BCLa, suggesting that BCLa is currently the dominant strain in China ( Fig. 3 B). This raises the possibility of cross-species transmission and highlights the need for continued monitoring and investigation of the spread of BCL viruses in animal populations.

## Role of host codon usage selection pressure on Rep gene in Kirkoviridae family

Due to Kirkoviridae being a newly reported CRESS-DNA family, the selection pressure based on host codon usage bias of the virus in this family is not clear. To explore this, we analyzed multiple reported rep genes within the Kirkoviridae family, as the ratio of AT to GC affects the use of microbial codons( Bohlin et al., 2013 ). The results showed that the rep genes in the Kirkoviridae family exhibited A>G>T>C and AT%>GC% (with an average GC content of 40.36±SD2.33). The sequence was analyzed using a PR2(parity rule 2) bias plot, with most points distributed between 0.50-0.75 on the x-axis and 0.40-0.75 on the y-axis, indicating that A3/AU3, G3/GC3 in the rep gene are generally high, and the third codon base follows the rule of U <A, C <G, and the balance between A/U and G/C is disturbed( Fig. 4 A). The unequal number of changes from A to T and from G to C indicates the presence of mutation and selection pressure.


> **Fig. 4:** Host codon usage selection pressure on Cap gene of Kirkoviridae family virus evolution. A. parity rule 2 (PR2)-bias plot; B.and C. ENc plotted against GC3s; D. RSCU values.

The effective number of codons (ENc) value represents the codon usage bias and an ENc value >50 indicates general random codon usage, while an ENc value <35 indicates high codon bias( Wang et al., 2018 ; Zhao et al., 2016 ). Our results showed that the ENc value of the rep genes in the k family was between 37-50 (average ENc 44.95±3.52), indicating weak to strong codon usage bias of the virus( Fig. 4 B). Further analysis of the ENc-GC3s plot, where the ENc value is plotted based on the GC3s value (GC content at the third position of the codon), was performed to confirm the results( Tian et al., 2020 ). The ENc-GC3s plot showed that the GC3s values were positively correlated with the ENc values, indicating a weak codon usage bias of the virus in the Kirkoviridae family( Fig. 4 C). We also calculated the relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) value, which represents the ratio between the observed value and the expected value of the same codon for a given amino acid. A value of 1 signifies a lack of bias, whereas a value greater than 1 signifies a positive codon bias, meaning codons are abundant, and a value less than 1 signifies a negative codon bias, meaning codons are less prevalent( Tian et al., 2020 ; Wang et al., 2018 ). Our results showed that 29 codons had an RSCU value of >1 and 32 codons had <1, clearly demonstrating codon usage deviation. These results demonstrate that the CRESS-DNA Kirkoviridae family has a broad range of host adaptability, and further selective pressure in the evolution of the virus Rep gene, rather than mutational pressure, played a significant role.


## Discussion

CRESS DNA viruses constitute a diverse and widely prevalent group of viruses that impact the domains of life in Archaea, Eubacteria, and Eukarya( Kazlauskas et al., 2019 ; Xie et al., 2020 ; Zhao et al., 2019 ). Among the most well-known and extensively studied member of this group is Porcine Circovirus (PCV), which has inflicted significant economic losses on the global pig industry( Opriessnig et al., 2020 ). PCV, along with PCL virus, is capable of infecting a wide range of host animals, including cattle( Wang et al., 2019 ; Zhai et al., 2019 ). Recently, a new CRESS DNA virus known as BCL virus, similar to PCV but possessing a circular genome and lacking the typical capsid protein, has been detected in calves with severe hemorrhagic enteritis in China. Currently, little is known about the prevalence of BCL virus, and this study aims to shed light on its genomic features and genetic diversity through an in-depth investigation.

In our study, we identified that the BCL AH20-1 strain is the most closely related to the previously reported BCL CH strain ( MH316857.1 ), followed by PCL viruses. Notably, the C-terminal region of the Rep protein in the AH20-1 strain was not characterized by the predicted amino acid deletion, but rather was more similar to that of the PCL Rep protein. Our phylogenetic analysis revealed that BCL viruses are genetically closest to PCL viruses and form a distinct lineage within the Kirkoviridae family, together with viruses from equine, camel, and human origins. Given the limited reporting on BCL virus to date, no systematic epidemiological survey has been conducted in China. Therefore, we conducted a CRESS DNA virus detection from 868 cow fecal samples collected from 71 farms in 20 provinces across China between April 2020 and April 2022. The results indicated that the virus was detected in 85.0% (17/20) of the provinces, suggesting a widespread geographical distribution in China. Furthermore, the overall positive rate in all samples was 23.6% (205/868), indicating a high prevalence of BCL among cow populations in China.

Previous genomic and phylogenetic analyses have suggested a strong similarity between BCL and PCL viruses, indicating a potential common origin and the possibility of cross-species transmission. To investigate this hypothesis, we conducted a detection of BCL virus in 113 sheep fecal samples collected from three provinces in China (Guangxi, Jiangsu, and Xinjiang). The overall positive rate was 20.4% (23/113). The partial Rep nucleotide sequences of the positive samples were obtained via PCR and compared with those of PCL, BCL, and Ovine Circular Virus Rep. The Rep sequences from the three hosts exhibited high similarity, further supporting the potential cross-species transmission risk of the virus. These results align with our bioinformatics analysis, highlighting the virus's broad host range adaptability. Phylogenetic analysis of the Rep gene suggested the presence of two genotypes (a and b) among the viruses. Our results indicate that the dominant strain in China is BCLa. However, it is not clear whether the virus is a direct pathogen and further studies are needed to understand its specific pathogenic mechanisms. Our attempts to isolate the virus from Martin-Darby Bovine Kidney cells were unsuccessful.


## Conclusion

Our study identified the BCL virus in bovine diarrheal feces and provided the first systematic investigation of its pathogenicity in China, confirming its widespread prevalence. Additionally, we reported the first detection of the virus in sheep, suggesting that it may be transmitted across multiple species, including pigs, cattle, and sheep. Therefore, we recommend implementing an intensive surveillance program to investigate the epidemiology of the virus and to strengthen viral surveillance in other animals (including humans) to assess the risk of spillover.

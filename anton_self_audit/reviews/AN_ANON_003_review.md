### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript evaluates whether testing for purifying selection via the nonsynonymous-to-synonymous substitution rate ratio ($K_A / K_S < 1$) between orthologous human and mouse sequences can reliably identify mammalian protein-coding exons. Using a curated dataset of 153 reviewed human–mouse orthologous gene pairs from NCBI LocusLink (comprising 1,244 coding exons: 114 initial, 991 internal, 114 terminal, and 25 single-exon genes), the authors align coding sequences guided by protein ClustalW alignments, slice alignments into exons assuming conserved human exon–intron boundaries in mouse, and run a maximum likelihood ratio test (LRT) in PAML `codeml` (F3$\times$4 codon model) comparing free $\omega = K_A / K_S$ against fixed $\omega = 1$. They report that 90.5% of all exons (and 100% of exons $\ge 300\text{ bp}$, including all 25 single-exon genes) achieve $K_A / K_S < 1$ at $P < 0.05$, corresponding to an exon-level false-negative rate (FNR) of 9.5%. Using 24,000 simulated sequence pairs across 24 length and divergence bins, they report an average false-positive rate (FPR) of 2.6%. Links to download the curated dataset and analysis tables are provided in the Methods.

The empirical demonstration that typical mammalian internal exons (~132 bp at ~13.5% human–mouse nucleotide divergence) contain sufficient mutational signal for a likelihood-based $K_A / K_S$ test to detect purifying selection in >90% of cases is a useful comparative genomics result, particularly for long exons and single-exon genes where single-genome *ab initio* Markov models degrade. However, the manuscript's statistical calibration and headline comparisons against *ab initio* gene predictors (e.g., GenScan) suffer from three methodological issues: (1) the paper compares a **one-sided** biological criterion ($K_A / K_S < 1$) using a **two-sided** $\chi^2_1$ threshold ($P < 0.05$), failing to recognize that the nominal asymptotic FPR of a one-sided test at a two-sided 5% critical value is 2.5% by construction; (2) the "noncoding" FPR simulation generates stop-codon-free sense-codon alignments in a known reading frame without indels or multiple-reading-frame testing across the 6 possible frames; and (3) comparing this oracle-boundary, oracle-frame test directly to full de novo gene predictors in the Abstract overstates what the standalone test achieves. All of these issues can be addressed through straightforward re-analysis and textual reframing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Most human–mouse orthologous coding exons (~90.5% overall, and 100% of exons $\ge 300\text{ bp}$ and single-exon genes) have sufficient length and divergence for the $K_A / K_S < 1$ LRT to reach statistical significance: [Well Supported].**  
  Across 1,244 biologically validated coding exons, 1,126 pass the LRT at the threshold used, and the stratified analyses in Figure 1A–B clearly establish how statistical power scales with exon length (failing exons average 89 bp vs. 147 bp overall, $P < 0.0001$) and human–mouse sequence divergence (optimal power in the 10–15% divergence bin, with only 4.1% failing). The finding that all 87 exons $\ge 300\text{ bp}$ pass the test is solid.

* **Differences in false-negative rates among first (16%), last (14%), and internal (8%) exons are "not statistically significant": [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the section "The $K_A / K_S$ Ratio Test" (line 76), the authors report failure rates of 16% ($N = 114$, ~18 exons) for first exons, 14% ($N = 114$, ~16 exons) for last exons, and 8% ($N = 991$, ~79 exons) for internal exons, and assert that "this difference was not statistically significant." In fact, a standard contingency-table test on these exact counts yields $\chi^2 = 10.6$ ($df = 2, P = 0.0050$) across the three exon classes, and $\chi^2 = 7.74$ ($df = 1, P = 0.0054$; Fisher's exact $P = 0.008$) for first vs. internal exons. The authors appear to have inferred non-significance from visual inspection of wide single-group 95% confidence intervals rather than performing a formal two-sample or three-sample comparison. Biologically and methodologically, higher failure rates in initial and terminal exons are expected both because terminal coding regions often experience relaxed purifying constraint and because the pipeline assumed identical exon–intron boundaries between human and mouse without verifying mouse splice junctions.

* **The $K_A / K_S$ test has a false-positive rate of 2.6% in noncoding regions, "lower than all current gene prediction methods": [Overstated].**  
  This conclusion is compromised by three factors:
  1. *Unrecognized two-sided vs. one-sided $\chi^2_1$ calibration:* When testing $H_0: \omega = 1$ against the one-sided alternative $H_1: \omega < 1$, the likelihood ratio statistic $LR = 2(\ln ML_2 - \ln ML_1)$ rejects in both tails ($\hat{\omega} < 1$ and $\hat{\omega} > 1$). Because only alignments with $\hat{\omega} < 1$ and $LR > \chi^2_{1, 0.95} = 3.841$ are called coding exons, the nominal Type I error rate under the null hypothesis is $\alpha / 2 = 0.025$ (2.5%), not 5%. The simulated mean FPR of 2.6% in Table 3 is simply recovering this $\alpha/2 = 2.5\%$ nominal level rather than demonstrating an unexpectedly low error rate of the estimator.
  2. *Biologically unrealistic "noncoding" simulation with oracle reading frame and stop-codon removal:* In Methods (line 139), the "noncoding" sequence pairs are simulated in a known reading frame using 61 sense codons with uniform probability, and whenever a random nucleotide mutation creates a stop codon (TAA, TAG, TGA), "this site is mutated again until a nonstop codon different from the original one is created." Real conserved noncoding sequences (introns, UTRs, intergenic CNSs) are not constrained to evolve without stop codons in a pre-specified reading frame, and an unannotated genomic alignment must be scanned across all **6 reading frames** (3 forward, 3 reverse). Scanning 6 frames (or conditioning on frames free of stop codons) substantially inflates the false-positive rate unless a multiple-testing correction is applied.
  3. *Asymmetric comparison against de novo gene predictors:* In the Abstract and Discussion (line 115), the authors compare their 2.6% FPR and 9.5% FNR—obtained on pre-segmented, protein-aligned exons with known boundaries and known reading frame—directly against the 7–28% FPR and 8–32% FNR of single-sequence *ab initio* gene finders (e.g., GenScan in Rogic et al.) that must discover splice sites and reading frames de novo from unannotated genomic DNA. While the authors acknowledge in Discussion line 119 that the $K_A / K_S$ test cannot predict exon/intron boundaries on its own, the Abstract states without qualification that the test has a false-positive rate "lower than all current methods."

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Evaluate False-Positive Rates on Authentic Noncoding Alignments Scanned Across All Six Reading Frames
* **WHY:** Simulating sense-codon-only sequences in a single known reading frame and actively mutating away stop codons (Methods, line 139) does not represent the actual classification problem faced when scanning conserved human–mouse genomic blocks of unknown coding status and unknown frame.
* **WHAT:** Compute the empirical false-positive rate on (a) authentic conserved noncoding human–mouse alignments (orthologous introns, 5'/3' UTRs, or intergenic conserved noncoding sequences from the same 153 LocusLink genes) and (b) neutral nucleotide simulations that allow stop codons and evaluate all open reading frames across the 6 possible frames (+1, +2, +3, $-1$, $-2$, $-3$).
* **HOW:** Extract orthologous conserved intronic and UTR segments (matched to the 6 length classes in Figure 1A) from the 153 human–mouse gene pairs already in the authors' MySQL database. For each segment, identify all stop-free reading frames across the 6 frames, run the `codeml` LRT on each valid frame, and report both the per-frame FPR and the segment-level family-wise FPR (where a noncoding block is a false positive if *any* of the 6 frames passes $K_A / K_S < 1$ at $P < 0.05$ or Bonferroni-corrected $P < 0.05 / k$).

#### 2. Correct the One-Sided Likelihood Ratio Test Calibration Explanation
* **WHY:** Comparing $LR = 2(\ln ML_2 - \ln ML_1)$ to $\chi^2_{1, 0.95} = 3.841$ while requiring $\hat{K}_A / \hat{K}_S < 1$ is a one-sided test at significance level $\alpha = 0.025$ ($\frac{1}{2} \chi^2_1$ mixture or signed likelihood root $z = \text{sign}(\hat{\omega} - 1)\sqrt{LR} < -1.96$). Furthermore, in the simulated null (line 139), actively re-mutating nonsense mutations selectively removes nonsynonymous pathways (since synonymous mutations never create stop codons from sense codons), slightly biasing $\hat{K}_A / \hat{K}_S$ downward at higher divergences (explaining why the simulated FPR drops to 1.1% at 1002 bp and 20% divergence in Table 3).
* **WHAT:** Explicitly clarify in Methods and in the "False-Positive Rate of the Test" section that a directional $K_A / K_S < 1$ filter applied to a two-sided $\chi^2_1$ critical value of 3.841 has a nominal Type I error rate of 2.5%, and report results at both $\alpha_{\text{one-sided}} = 0.05$ ($LR > 2.706$) and $\alpha_{\text{one-sided}} = 0.025$ ($LR > 3.841$).
* **HOW:** Re-tabulate the existing `ML1` and `ML2` values already stored in the authors' MySQL results table against the one-sided 5% critical value ($LR > 2.706$ with $\hat{\omega} < 1$) and the one-sided 1% critical value ($LR > 5.412$ with $\hat{\omega} < 1$) to provide a receiver-operating trade-off between FNR and FPR.

#### 3. Correct the Statistical Comparison of Initial, Internal, and Terminal Exons
* **WHY:** The claim on line 76 that the failure rates of first (16%), last (14%), and internal (8%) exons are "not statistically significant" is contradicted by a $\chi^2$ or Fisher's exact test on the reported sample sizes ($P = 0.005$).
* **WHAT:** Report the exact contingency-table $P$-value comparing exon position classes and discuss why initial and terminal exons fail at roughly twice the rate of internal exons.
* **HOW:** Replace "although this difference was not statistically significant" (line 76) with the exact $\chi^2$ test statistic ($\chi^2 = 10.6, df = 2, P = 0.005$) and note two contributing causes: (1) biological relaxation of purifying constraint near N- and C-termini (reflected in Table 2, where median $K_A$ is 0.041 and 0.045 in first and last exons vs. 0.034 in internal exons), and (2) potential boundary shifts or N/C-terminal indels caused by assuming human exon coordinates map 1:1 onto mouse CDS alignments.

#### 4. Reframe Comparisons with De Novo Gene Prediction Algorithms
* **WHY:** Abstract item (3) states unconditionally that "the test has a false-negative rate, lower than most of current gene prediction methods and a false-positive rate lower than all current methods," whereas Discussion line 119 notes that the test cannot identify exon/intron boundaries and cannot be used alone for gene prediction.
* **WHAT:** Qualify the Abstract and Discussion claims so readers understand that the reported 9.5% FNR and 2.6% FPR evaluate the **classification of pre-aligned candidate exons**, not end-to-end de novo gene structure prediction.
* **HOW:** Revise Abstract item (3) to state: *"when applied to pre-aligned candidate exons, the test achieves a false-negative rate (9.5%) and false-positive rate (~2.6%) that compare favorably with the exon-level error rates of single-genome gene prediction methods."*

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Table Corrections
* **WHY:** Several numerical typos and formula sign inversions appear in the text and tables.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. *Likelihood Ratio Formula Sign Inversion (Methods, line 134):* `ML1` is defined as the likelihood with $K_A / K_S$ fixed at 1 (null model) and `ML2` with $K_A / K_S$ free (alternative model), so `LR = 2( ln ML1 - ln ML2 )` is non-positive. Correct the equation to `LR = 2( ln ML2 - ln ML1 )`.
  2. *Table 2 Range Contradiction (line 69 vs. line 72):* The range of $K_a$ is reported as `0–1.863` for `Internal` exons and `0–1.340` for `Last` exons, but `0–0.863` for `All` exons. Correct the `All` $K_a$ upper range to `0–1.863`.
  3. *Table 3 Header Typo (line 96):* The first column header under `Length (codons/nucleotides)` reads `17/5`; correct to `17/51` (17 codons = 51 nucleotides, matching "length = 51 bp" in the text on line 90).
  4. *Table 3 Confidence Interval Typo (line 101):* In row `20` (% Divergence), column `34/102`, the entry reads `2.9 (1.5–3.5)`. A 95% confidence interval of `(1.5–3.5)` is centered at `2.5`, not `2.9`; correct the interval to `(1.9–3.9)` (matching the `2.9 (1.9–3.9)` entry in the adjacent column `50/150`).

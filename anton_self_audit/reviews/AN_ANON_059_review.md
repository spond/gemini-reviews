### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces B-STILL (Bayesian Significance Test of Invariant Low Likelihoods), an empirical Bayes framework built on the MG94$\times$REV codon model and FUBAR discrete-grid variational Bayes architecture to distinguish extreme purifying selection from stochastic invariance or low substitution opportunity near the zero-rate origin $(\alpha, \beta) \to (0,0)$. By concentrating grid points near zero via a quadratic grid spacing and defining a tree-length-scaled "Proximal Stasis" regime where the expected substitution count satisfies $E[S] = (\alpha d_s + \beta d_{ns})T \le X$ (default $X = 0.5$), B-STILL computes site-level Empirical Bayes Factors ($\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$, $\text{EBF}_{\text{syn}}$) relative to the gene-wide empirical prior, paired with a nonparametric hypergeometric scan statistic (with 10,000 permutations for FWER control) to detect contiguous "Stasis Clusters." The authors benchmark B-STILL across 1,800 simulated alignments spanning six empirical tree topologies, 19,117 mammalian genes from the 120-mammal alignment, 111 viral alignments from the FRESCO dataset, human population variation in gnomAD v4.1.1 ($N = 68$ genes), clinical pathogenicity in ClinVar ($N = 18$ genes) and REVEL ($N = 85$ fast-evolving genes; 34,206 positions), 815 poorly characterized "dark proteome" genes, and a 38-gene head-to-head comparison against `phyloP`. Code is openly distributed in a standard phylogenetic package and standalone Python scripts.

B-STILL addresses a genuine limitation of standard frequentist site-level likelihood ratio tests (`phyloP`, `FEL`, `SLAC`), which saturate at a flat significance ceiling across all completely invariant sites in an alignment ("the 0/0 plateau") without accounting for codon-specific synonymous degeneracy or transition/transversion substitution opportunity. However, several mathematical and methodological issues require attention: (1) all defining mathematical equations in the Materials and Methods (lines 23–55) are missing or blank in the manuscript text; (2) the nomenclature and definition of "Proximal Stasis" ($\alpha \approx 0, \beta \approx 0$, i.e., total nucleotide-level stasis) is repeatedly conflated with "Protein Wobble Anchors" (where the amino acid is invariant, $\beta = 0$, but synonymous substitutions occur, $\alpha > 0$); (3) gene-wide prior pooling creates an interpretation paradox where ultra-conserved genes (histones, calmodulin) have low EBFs while fast-evolving genes yield $\text{EBF} > 10^6$, which can mislead downstream across-gene rankings (such as pooling 34,206 positions across 85 genes against REVEL); and (4) the mammal-wide gene count fluctuates between 19,117 and 19,152 across sections, and FRESCO fluctuates between 111 and 110 viral genes. These issues are addressable through textual and mathematical restoration, clear separation of $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ ($\alpha, \beta$ joint stasis) from $\text{EBF}_{\text{ns}}$ ($\beta = 0$ protein stasis), and within-gene vs. across-gene stratified benchmarking.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Conditioning on codon degeneracy and GTR exchangeabilities breaks the frequentist "0/0 plateau" at invariant sites and controls false positives ($\text{FPR} \ll 1\%$) across tree depths: [Well Supported].**  
  In the 1,800-replicate simulation study across six empirical tree topologies and five tree-scaling factors ($0.1\times$ to $5.0\times$), defining the stasis domain by expected substitutions $E[S] \le 0.5$ naturally compresses EBFs toward $1.0$ in shallow trees (e.g., SARS-CoV-2 Spike, $T = 0.12$, yielding 0 false calls) while achieving high power in deep trees ($T \ge 5.0$, HIV-1 RT, `rbcL`, Camelid VHH) and maintaining null $\text{FPR} < 0.05\%$ at $\text{EBF} \ge 10$ (Figure 2a–c). Furthermore, 4-fold degenerate codons (e.g., HIV-1 RT Pro217, $\text{EBF} = 1,986.41$) appropriately receive stronger stasis support than 2-fold codons (Tyr232, $\text{EBF} = 107.39$) due to higher unrealized synonymous substitution opportunity (Figure 2d).

* **Hypergeometric scan statistics of ESAs recover overlapping viral reading frames and conserved mammalian structural domains: [Well Supported].**  
  Applying the permutation-calibrated hypergeometric scan statistic identifies 45 Stasis Clusters in the FRESCO viral benchmark that map to well-characterized overlapping ORFs (HEV ORF2/ORF3, $p = 2.7 \times 10^{-16}$; HCV core+1/F protein, $p = 4.4 \times 10^{-20}$; rotavirus NSP5/NSP6; TuMV PIPO; Figure 5) and 4,888 clusters across the mammalian exome enriched for RNA-binding ($\text{OR} = 2.4, p < 10^{-15}$) and transcription factors, as well as a 3D structural hub in the uncharacterized protein `FAM214A` ($p < 10^{-4}$ by 3D spatial permutation).

* **$\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ identifies "Protein Wobble Anchors" where the protein sequence is invariant across 120 mammals despite active synonymous variation (`APOBEC3D` site 59, `F8` site 2103, `BRCA2` site 2777): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  There is an internal contradiction between the mathematical definition of Proximal Stasis ($\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$) and the biological description of "Protein Wobble Anchors" in lines 177–178. By definition (lines 45, 75, 87), $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ integrates posterior mass over $(\alpha, \beta)$ grid points where total expected substitutions $(\alpha d_s + \beta d_{ns})T \le 0.5$ (i.e., $\alpha \approx 0$ AND $\beta \approx 0$). In a 120-mammal alignment where the tree length $T$ is several substitutions per site, a codon that actively undergoes synonymous substitutions across mammals (e.g., `GTA` $\leftrightarrow$ `GTG` wobble variation in `APOBEC3D` site 59) has multiple observed synonymous changes ($S_{\text{syn}} \gg 0.5$), which pulls its posterior distribution away from $\alpha \approx 0$ and forces $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ toward zero—unless either (a) the statistic reported in lines 177–178 is actually Nonsynonymous Invariance ($\beta = 0$ marginal EBF, defined on line 43) rather than $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$, or (b) only 1 of 120 species had a single wobble difference. Similarly, at HIV-1 RT site 217 (line 91), the text states that despite mixed synonymous bases (`CCW` and `CCM`), "the total lack of nonsynonymous change is statistically significant ($\text{EBF}_{217} = 1,986.41$)." The authors must clarify whether $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ or marginal $\beta \approx 0$ EBF is being reported at sites with synonymous polymorphism.

* **B-STILL EBFs accurately predict clinical pathogenicity in ClinVar ($\text{AUROC} = 0.88$ for synonymous, $0.65$ for missense) and correlate with REVEL ($\rho = 0.40$) and gnomAD allele frequencies: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the negative Spearman correlations with gnomAD allele frequencies (Table 1, $\rho = -0.12$ to $-0.36$) and positive correlation with REVEL ($\rho = 0.40$ across 34,206 variants in 85 fast-evolving genes) confirm biological relevance, two major caveats apply: first, the ClinVar evaluation is restricted to a hand-curated subset of 18 genes out of the 38-gene panel without reporting the exact number of pathogenic vs. benign synonymous and missense variants tested per gene; second, because B-STILL EBFs are normalized against each gene's empirical prior, an invariant site in an ultra-conserved disease gene (e.g., `CALM1`, where nearly every missense mutation is pathogenic) receives a low EBF, whereas an invariant site in an olfactory receptor receives $\text{EBF} > 10^8$. Pooling raw EBFs across genes to compute a single global AUROC (0.65 and 0.88) or global REVEL correlation confounds within-gene relative surprise with across-gene baseline constraint.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Restore All Missing Mathematical Equations in the Materials and Methods
* **WHY:** In the Materials and Methods section (lines 24, 28, 30, 34, 48, 54), every display equation is completely blank after its introductory colon or "defined by / calculated as" lead-in. Readers cannot verify the exact parameterization of the MG94$\times$REV rate matrix $q_{ij}$, the VB0 marginal log-likelihood and Dirichlet hyperparameter $\alpha_0$, the quadratic grid formula for $(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$, the exact definition of $d_s$ and $d_{ns}$, or the hypergeometric tail formula.
* **WHAT:** Insert the complete mathematical expressions for all six missing display equations and define all symbols.
* **HOW:** Explicitly write out: (1) $q_{ij}$ for synonymous, nonsynonymous, and multi-nucleotide changes; (2) the marginal likelihood $\prod_{s=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^{K^2} \pi_k L_s(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$ and Dirichlet prior on $\pi$; (3) the posterior $P(\theta_k \mid x_s) = \frac{\pi_k L_s(\alpha_k, \beta_k)}{\sum_j \pi_j L_s(\alpha_j, \beta_j)}$; (4) the quadratic grid spacing formula; (5) $\text{EBF}(S) = \frac{P(S \mid x_s) / (1 - P(S \mid x_s))}{P(S) / (1 - P(S))}$; and (6) the hypergeometric upper-tail sum $p = \sum_{m=k}^{\min(K, d)} \frac{\binom{K}{m}\binom{L-K}{d-m}}{\binom{L}{d}}$.

#### 2. Resolve the Contradiction Between $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ ($E[S] \le 0.5$) and "Protein Wobble Anchors"
* **WHY:** If $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ requires total expected substitutions $(\alpha d_s + \beta d_{ns})T \le 0.5$, any codon with active synonymous turnover across 120 mammals ($\alpha d_s T \gg 0.5$) cannot have high posterior mass in $E[S] \le 0.5$. Conversely, if a site is truly invariant at both synonymous and nonsynonymous positions ($\alpha = \beta = 0$), calling it a "protein wobble anchor" because `GTA` and `GTG` occur is contradictory unless the synonymous count is $\le 1$ across the entire 120-mammal tree.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact observed nucleotide codon counts across the 120-mammal alignment for `APOBEC3D` site 59, `F8` site 2103, and `BRCA2` site 2777, and distinguish between three distinct statistics defined on lines 39–45: (i) Total Proximal Stasis ($(\alpha d_s + \beta d_{ns})T \le X$), (ii) Protein-Only Stasis ($\beta d_{ns} T \le X$ with $\alpha$ unconstrained), and (iii) Synonymous-Only Stasis ($\alpha d_s T \le X$ with $\beta$ unconstrained).
* **HOW:** Check the HBL implementation and JSON outputs for these sites to verify how $E[S]$ and $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$ vs. $\text{EBF}_{\text{ns}}$ are computed, and update lines 89–92 and 177–180 so the mathematical definitions match the empirical examples.

#### 3. Account for Gene-Wide Prior Normalization When Evaluating Across-Gene Clinical Benchmarks
* **WHY:** As the authors note in lines 119–120, ultra-conserved genes (`CALM1`, histones) have high prior mass near $(0,0)$, which compresses their site-level EBFs, whereas fast-evolving genes (mucins, olfactory receptors) have low prior mass near $(0,0)$, inflating site-level EBFs above $10^6$. Consequently, an uncalibrated aggregate AUROC across genes (line 147) penalizes B-STILL on highly constrained disease genes.
* **WHAT:** Report both within-gene (per-gene) and across-gene clinical performance, and compare the EBF (which measures relative surprise against the gene prior) against the raw posterior probability of stasis $P(E[S] \le X \mid x_s)$ or posterior mean rate $E[\alpha d_s + \beta d_{ns} \mid x_s]$ (which retains the gene-wide constraint level).
* **HOW:** In the 18-gene ClinVar benchmark and 85-gene REVEL benchmark, report a table listing each gene, the number of pathogenic and benign variants (for both missense and synonymous categories), the per-gene AUROC using $\text{EBF}_{\text{prox}}$/$\text{EBF}_{\text{syn}}$, and the aggregate AUROC using both EBF and raw posterior stasis probability $P(E[S] \le X \mid x_s)$.

#### 4. Reconcile Numerical Discrepancies in Dataset Sizes Across Sections
* **WHY:** The total number of mammalian genes analyzed is stated as 19,117 in lines 110 and 113, but as 19,152 in lines 161, 164, and 181. Similarly, the FRESCO viral dataset is described as 111 alignments in line 75, but as 110 genes in lines 154 and 159. In addition, line 65 states a validation panel of 38 genes was selected for synonymous pathogenicity, line 71 states $N = 68$ genes were used for gnomAD (with only 20 genes shown in Table 1) and $N = 18$ genes for ClinVar.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Audit and harmonize the exact gene counts (19,117 vs. 19,152 mammalian genes; 110 vs. 111 FRESCO viral genes) throughout the text and figure captions, explain the relationship between the 38-gene synonymous panel (line 65), the 68-gene gnomAD panel (line 71), and the 18-gene ClinVar subset (line 71), and include all 68 genes in a supplementary table alongside the 20 highlighted in Table 1.

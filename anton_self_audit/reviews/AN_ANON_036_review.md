### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces `PRIME` (PRoperty Informed Models of Evolution), a family of codon-level maximum likelihood substitution models implemented in a widely used molecular evolution framework that parameterizes nonsynonymous amino acid exchangeability $\beta_{xy} = \exp\left(\psi^{(s)} - \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i^{(s)} |x_i - y_i|\right)$ as a function of $D$ standardized physicochemical property differences (Hydrophobicity, Volume, Isoelectric Point, Alpha-Helix Propensity, and Beta-Sheet Propensity, or Atchley factors). The authors deploy three model architectures: `G-PRIME` (gene-wide fixed property weights), `E-PRIME` (branch-site random-effects mixture model ported from `BUSTED`), and `S-PRIME` (site-specific fixed-effects property inference with two-stage Benjamini–Bogomolov selective FDR and Holm–Bonferroni FWER control). The framework is evaluated across (1) 24 empirical benchmark alignments ($9,998$ total codon sites), (2) a proteome-wide screen of $18,944$ mammalian gene alignments mapped to $5,125$ experimental human PDB structures ($1,447,182$ residues), (3) $\sim 5,000$ parametric coalescent/spliced simulation replicates ($80\%$ `MG94` null + $20\%$ `G-PRIME` alternative across $>20$ regimes, analyzing $1,111,446$ neutral and $\sim 200,000$ alternative sites), (4) a 7-regime model-misspecification benchmark generated under Halpern–Bruno Mutation–Selection (`MutSel`) dynamics in `Pyvolve` (`S1–S7`), (5) representation alignment against `ESM-2` (`esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`) principal components, and (6) experimental Deep Mutational Scanning (DMS) fitness profiles for H3N2 Hemagglutinin (`A/Perth/16`, $280$ variable sites).

`PRIME` is a thorough, mathematically principled, and extensively validated advance in mechanistic molecular evolution. The manuscript's standout strength is its self-critical statistical diagnostics: rather than presenting only favorable simulations, the authors explicitly characterize the **invariance trap** (where extreme purifying constraint $\lambda > 10$ extinguishes substitution variation and reduces site-level power to $0\%$), the **two-state toggling artifact** (where high substitution counts restricted to $N_{\text{aa}} = 2$ amino acids create complete separation in the 5-property parameter space, inflating raw Wilks' LRT false positives to $>65\%$ at redundancy ratio $R = N_{\text{subs}}/N_{\text{aa}} \ge 20$ before BH-FDR correction), and **property cross-talk** under `MutSel` misspecification (where strict purifying constraint on Hydrophobicity induces apparent diversifying selection $\lambda_{\text{Vol}} < 0$ on Volume because the allowed hydrophobic residues `Val, Ile, Phe` span wide volume differences). The primary areas requiring attention are (1) missing display equations in the formatted Methods text (Equations 1, 2, and the power formula), (2) parameter boundary clamping ($\lambda = \pm 15.0$) that clusters structural medians at the optimizer ceiling (`13.89`) and violates interior-point regularity conditions for 1-d.f. LRTs, and (3) a few overstated interpretations of weak correlations (`ESM-2` $\text{mean }\max |\rho| = 0.19$, contact number $R \approx 0.11$, and DMS Spearman $\rho = 0.157$).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Incorporating quantitative physicochemical exchangeabilities (`G-PRIME` and `E-PRIME`) substantially improves codon model fit over `MG94`, `CoRa`, and `BUSTED` across 24 benchmarks and $98.6\%$ of $18,944$ mammalian genes, acting synergistically with rate heterogeneity ($I < 0$ in $84.4\%$ of genes): [Well Supported].**  
  Across the mammalian proteome, 5-property `G-PRIME` outperforms `MG94` in $98.6\%$ of genes ($\text{mean }\Delta\text{AICc} = 441.5$) and outperforms binary conservative/radical `CoRa` in $98.5\%$ of genes (Figure 3a). Combining branch-site rate heterogeneity with property weights in `E-PRIME` yields a mean $\Delta\text{AICc} = 723.6$, exceeding the additive sum of `BUSTED` ($211.1$) and `G-PRIME` ($441.5$) with mean interaction information $I = -71.0$ (Table 4).

* **Mammalian protein evolution follows a depth-dependent biophysical hierarchy—rigid conservation of core Volume, Hydrophobicity, Isoelectric Point, and Beta-Sheet scaffolds versus adaptive/neutral flexibility in exposed surfaces and Alpha-Helix propensities: [Well Supported].**  
  Across $18,944$ mammalian genes, `G-PRIME` infers purifying conservation (FDR $\le 0.05$) for Volume ($85.8\%$), Beta-Sheet propensity ($75.5\%$), and Hydrophobicity ($74.2\%$), whereas $44.0\%$ of genes exhibit $\lambda < 0$ for Alpha-Helix propensity (Figures 3b, 4). Structural mapping across $5,938$ benchmark residues (Figure 7) and $1,447,182$ mammalian PDB residues (Figure 8a) confirms a consistent buried-to-exposed constraint decay across $3,025$ genes ($\text{mean }\rho_{\text{RSA},\lambda} = -0.18\text{ to }-0.23$).

* **Site-level `S-PRIME` detection power and specificity are governed by the informational redundancy ratio ($R = N_{\text{subs}}/N_{\text{aa}}$, $\text{AUC} = 0.91$) and remain well-calibrated under `MutSel` frequency bias (`S3–S5`) and lineage heterotachy (`S6`): [Well Supported].**  
  Logistic regression across $\sim 200,000$ simulated alternative sites establishes that detection power rises from $7.9\%$ at $R = 1.0$ to $28.5\%$ at $R = 2.0$, $49.4\%$ at $R = 3.0$, and $>93.4\%$ at $R \ge 10.0$ (Figure 10f). Crucially, the Frequency-Biased Neutral (`FBN`) controls in `S3–S5` (which match the stationary amino acid frequencies of `MutSel` partitions while evolving at $\omega = 1.0$) yield $\text{FPR} \le 1.7\%$, proving that `S-PRIME` detects substitution rate suppression rather than static amino acid composition.

* **Individual property 1-d.f. LRTs and structural median comparisons are unaffected by optimizer boundary clamping ($\lambda \in [-15, +15]$) and property cross-talk: [Fragile].**  
  In multiple places in the Results, estimated site-level $\lambda$ values hit a hard numerical boundary of $\pm 15.0$ (e.g., H3N2 Site 226 $\lambda_{\text{pI}} = 15.0$, HIV-1 RT Sites 184 and 100 $\lambda_{\text{Hydro}} = 15.0, \lambda_{\text{Vol}} = 15.0$, and `MutSel` Scenario S2 $\lambda_{\text{Hydro}} = 15.0$), and in the mammalian structural screen (line 78), the Buried medians for Volume, Isoelectric Point, and Hydrophobicity are all reported as the exact same number (`13.89`). When a nuisance parameter $\lambda_j$ diverges to the optimization boundary (`15.0` or `13.89`) due to quasi-complete separation at a site with limited amino acid diversity ($N_{\text{aa}} \le 4$), (a) the profile log-likelihood surface becomes flat/non-quadratic at the boundary, distorting the asymptotic $\chi^2_1$ distribution for testing individual properties $\lambda_i = 0$, and (b) as shown in `MutSel` Scenario S2, clamping one conserved property at $+15.0$ forces a compensatory negative estimate ($\lambda_{\text{Vol}} = -4.4$) on a correlated or orthogonal property to match the overall nonsynonymous rate $\psi^{(s)}$. Thus, negative site-level $\lambda_i^{(s)}$ estimates at strongly conserved sites can represent mathematical compensation for boundary-clamped conserving properties rather than true diversifying selection on property $i$.

* **S-PRIME phylogenetic imputation accurately captures experimental DMS fitness landscapes ($r = 0.605$) and aligns with `ESM-2` latent axes: [Moderately Supported].**  
  On H3N2 Hemagglutinin DMS ($280$ variable sites), `S-PRIME` achieves a higher mean Pearson correlation ($r = 0.605$) than `ESM-2` ($r = 0.533$), but a substantially lower Spearman rank correlation ($\rho = 0.157$ vs. `ESM-2` $\rho = 0.305$) and higher Jensen–Shannon divergence ($\text{JSD} = 0.382$ vs. $0.327$). As the authors candidly note (line 84), `S-PRIME` assigns $>90\%$ posterior probability to the wild-type/consensus residue at most sites, driving the linear Pearson $r$ via a single dominant outlier point per site while capturing only $21.2\%$ of the experimental preference mass among viable states ($>0.10$) and poorly ranking the 19 non-consensus amino acids ($\rho = 0.157$). Similarly, the correlation between `S-PRIME` weights and the top 5 `ESM-2` PCs ($\text{mean }\max |\rho| \approx 0.19$ after taking the maximum over 5 PCs) is statistically non-zero but accounts for $<4\%$ of shared rank variance.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add Weak L2 (Ridge) Regularization or Boundary Diagnostics to Prevent Compensatory Cross-Talk at Boundary-Clamped Sites
* **WHY:** In `MutSel` Scenario S2, pure stabilizing selection on Hydrophobicity drives $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{Hydro}}$ to the upper optimizer bound (`15.0`) while inducing a spurious diversifying estimate ($\hat{\lambda}_{\text{Vol}} = -4.4$) on Volume. In empirical proteins (e.g., H3N2 Site 226 where $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{pI}} = 15.0$ and $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{Helix}} = 11.8$ co-occur with $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{Hydro}} = -2.49$ and $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{Vol}} = -1.81$), readers cannot tell whether negative $\lambda$ values reflect genuine adaptive diversification or mathematical compensation for another parameter hitting the $+15$ boundary.
* **WHAT:** Flag sites where one or more $\hat{\lambda}_i^{(s)}$ estimates hit the numerical boundary ($\pm 15.0$ or $\pm 13.89$), and evaluate whether a small quadratic penalty $\operatorname{pen}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) = -\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i^2$ (or testing each property marginally in a 1-property model when another property hits the boundary) eliminates spurious opposite-sign cross-talk in Scenario S2.
* **HOW:** Re-run the `MutSel` Scenario S2 benchmark checking how often a significant negative $\hat{\lambda}_j < 0$ call occurs conditionally on another property hitting the upper bound $\hat{\lambda}_i \ge 14.9$. In the site classification decision tree (Figure 1), require that a site classified as "Constrained Diversification" also show $\hat{\lambda}_j < 0$ in a marginal single-property fit (so that its negative sign is not an artifact of collinear compensation against a boundary-clamped parameter).

#### 2. Report Wild-Type-Excluded DMS Correlations for H3N2 Hemagglutinin
* **WHY:** Because phylogenetic imputation conditional on an alignment where the focal sequence (`A/Perth/16`) shares its wild-type amino acid with closely related H3N2 tips naturally places $>90\%$ probability mass on the observed wild-type state, computing Pearson $r$ across all 20 amino acids ($1$ wild-type near $(1.0, \text{high})$ and $19$ non-wild-type states clustered near $(0.0, \text{low})$) inflates Pearson $r = 0.605$ via leverage of the wild-type amino acid alone, even if `MG94` (with $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$) were used.
* **WHAT:** Add (a) an `MG94` ($\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$) phylogenetic imputation baseline to the H3N2 DMS comparison, and (b) report Pearson $r$ and Spearman $\rho$ across the **19 non-wild-type (mutant) amino acids** at each site.
* **HOW:** Using the existing `A/Perth/16` masked imputation pipeline (Methods, lines 127–129), compute the codon probabilities under `MG94` ($\lambda_i = 0$) alongside `S-PRIME` and `ESM-2`. Reporting the gain of `S-PRIME` over `MG94` on the 19 non-consensus amino acids will directly isolate the predictive contribution of the physicochemical $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ parameters from baseline phylogenetic tree imputation.

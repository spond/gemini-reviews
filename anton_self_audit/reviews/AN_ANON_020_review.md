### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates RNA–DNA sequence differences (RDDs) in the human mitochondrial transcriptome by comparing paired deep Illumina sequencing of mtDNA (100-nt paired-end) and mRNA/total RNA (100-nt single-end) from lymphoblastoid (B-cell) lines of five Caucasian individuals (`ERP001523` and `ERP002075`). Applying stringent alignment and calling filters—unique mapping to the revised Cambridge Reference Sequence (`rCRS`) and not to `hg19`, exclusion of low-complexity regions, coverage $\ge 1000\times$, exclusion of DNA heteroplasmies, bidirectional strand support $\ge 0.8\%$ per strand ($\ge 1.6\%$ total), and $\ge 5$ variant reads per strand—the authors identify three recurrent mitochondrial RDD sites: position `295` (C-to-U in the D-loop), position `13710` (A-to-U and A-to-G at a synonymous 3rd codon position of *MT-ND5*), and position `2617` within the *16S rRNA* gene (>30% A-to-U and ~15% A-to-G across all five samples). Focusing on the prominent *16S rRNA* `2617` site, they rule out nuclear mitochondrial DNA (NUMT) contamination using isolated mitoplast RNA datasets and `BLAT` screening against `hg19`, validate the `2617` A-to-U and A-to-G cDNA variants via direct Sanger sequencing and plasmid cloning across six additional human cell/tissue types and non-human primates (*Gorilla gorilla*, *Pongo pygmaeus*, *Macaca mulatta*), detect lower levels (3.2%–5.3% A-to-U) on uncleaved precursor polycistronic transcripts via targeted MiSeq sequencing, reconstruct the ancestral vertebrate/primate state across 1,755 vertebrate mtDNAs (showing that `T` is ancestral in prosimians and non-primate mammals while simians fixed `A` at `2617` and exhibit the A-to-U/G cDNA difference), and perform 3D structural modeling on bacterial/yeast *23S rRNA* templates (`1NKW`, helix H71–H64 interface) to propose that converting `2617A` restores structural stability.

The study is experimentally thorough, combining deep DNA/RNA sequencing, mitoplast controls, NUMT divergence checks, precursor polycistronic RT-PCR amplicon sequencing with no-RT controls, cross-primate comparative assays, and open raw data deposition in ENA (`ERP001523`, `ERP002075`). However, the manuscript's central mechanistic framing—treating the `2617` A-to-U and A-to-G cDNA changes primarily as canonical or non-canonical "RNA editing / RDDs" that "recapitulate the ancestral 16S rRNA" (Abstract, lines 6, 30, 41, 49) while relegating post-transcriptional nucleotide methylation (`m1A`, $N^1$-methyladenosine at human mitochondrial *16S rRNA* position 947, i.e., mtDNA position `2617`) to a brief speculative mention in the third paragraph of the Discussion (line 53, hypothesis 3)—inverting the biochemical reality of how reverse transcriptase (RT) reads $N^1$-methyladenosine. Because a methyl group at the Watson-Crick $N^1$ position of adenosine blocks canonical base-pairing with dTTP during cDNA synthesis, RT enzymes stall or misincorporate dATP (yielding T/U in cDNA) and dCTP (yielding G in cDNA) at characteristic rates, which explains *both* the simultaneous appearance of A-to-U (>30%) and A-to-G (~15%) at the exact same nucleotide (`2617` and `13710`), *and* why the structural modeling in Figure 5 had to invoke water/ion bridges for U whereas an $N^1$-methylated positively charged adenine (`m1A`) directly forms the electrostatic/hydrogen-bond interaction with the H64 phosphate backbone. Re-centering the interpretation and structural modeling around post-transcriptional `m1A` base methylation (and RT misincorporation signature) rather than base-replacement RNA editing would resolve the mechanistic puzzles raised in the Discussion.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: Deep sequencing, mitoplast controls, BLAT screening, and Sanger/clonal cDNA sequencing establish genuine, NUMT-free RNA–cDNA sequence differences at human mtDNA positions `2617` (>30% A-to-U, ~15% A-to-G), `13710` (A-to-U, A-to-G), and `295` (C-to-U): [Well Supported].**  
  The experimental and bioinformatic controls excluding sequencing/mapping artifacts and nuclear pseudogenes (NUMTs) are rigorous: (1) unique mapping with $>1000\times$ depth and bidirectional strand filters excludes end-of-read and strand-bias artifacts (line 14, 51); (2) presence in purified mitoplast RNA (Mercer et al.) and absence of co-occurring flanking variants present in all 30 `hg19` BLAT NUMT hits rule out NUMT cross-mapping (lines 24–26); and (3) direct Sanger sequencing across six human tissues and 24 sequenced plasmid clones from `GM14447` (9 A, 13 T, 2 G; line 19) independently confirm the multi-allelic cDNA signature at `2617`.

* **Claim 2: The simultaneous A-to-U and A-to-G RDDs at `2617` (and `13710`) represent an RNA base-replacement editing event that converts `2617A` into `U` or `G` to recapitulate the ancestral primate `T` allele: [Overstated].**  
  In the Abstract, Results (lines 30, 41), and Discussion (line 49), the authors frame the `2617` A-to-U/G cDNA calls as an evolutionary RNA replacement of `A` by `U` (or `G`) that restores the ancestral `T` base present in prosimians and non-primate mammals. While the authors briefly acknowledge in Discussion line 53 (possibility 3) that *"a single enzyme replaces the 2617 A at the 16S rRNA for an unknown modified nucleotide, which is read by the resultant sequence mainly as a U but also as a G,"* they do not test or integrate this explanation into their main conclusions. Biochemically, no known RNA editing deaminase replaces an adenine ring with a uracil pyrimidine ring (A-to-U transversion) alongside A-to-G at a constant 2:1 ratio; rather, $N^1$-methyladenosine (`m1A`, catalyzed by mitochondrial tRNA/rRNA methyltransferase TRMT61B at position `2617` of *16S rRNA* and `13710` of *MT-ND5* mRNA) blocks Watson-Crick base pairing during reverse transcription, causing reverse transcriptase (`iScript` / `TaqMan` RT) to misincorporate dATP (read as U/T, ~30%) and dCTP (read as G, ~15%). Thus, the native mitochondrial *16S rRNA* molecule in vivo does **not** carry a uracil (`U`) or guanine (`G`) at position `2617`—it carries a methylated adenosine (`m1A`), and the `U` and `G` bases exist only as reverse-transcription misincorporation products in the cDNA.

* **Claim 3: Replacing `A` with `U` or `G` in the 3D ribosomal structure model (Figure 5) stabilizes the *16S rRNA* H71–H64 subunit interface, whereas an `A` abolishes the hydrogen bond to the H64 backbone: [Fragile].**  
  In line 41 and Figure 5, the authors model canonical unmodified bases (`A`, `U`, `G`, `C`) at the homologous position in the *Deinococcus radiodurans* `1NKW` 50S ribosome crystal structure. Because they assume the in vivo RNA base is literally `U` or `G` (the cDNA sequence) rather than `m1A`, they conclude that unmodified `A` disrupts the hydrogen bond to the H64 backbone whereas `U` stabilizes it via hypothetical water/ion bridges. In reality, because the biological modification at *16S rRNA* `2617` in simian mitochondria is $N^1$-methylation of adenosine (`m1A`), the $N^1$-methyl group introduces a constitutive positive charge on the purine ring (and alters the hoogsteen/sugar-edge electrostatic potential) that directly interacts with the negatively charged phosphate backbone of helix H64 without requiring conversion of the purine to a pyrimidine (`U`). Modeling unmodified `U` and `G` instead of `m1A` misidentifies the structural mechanism of stabilization.

* **Claim 4: Phylogenetic reconstruction across 1,755 vertebrates and comparative Sanger sequencing in 5 non-human primates demonstrate that `T` is the ancestral eutherian/primate allele at `2617`, that `A` fixed in the simian ancestor, and that the `2617` cDNA modification occurs strictly in species carrying genomic `A`: [Well Supported].**  
  The comparative analysis across 1,755 vertebrate mtDNAs and 334 eutherian mammals (Figures 2–3, Supplemental Figures 5–6) combined with paired DNA/cDNA Sanger sequencing in *Pongo pygmaeus*, *Gorilla gorilla*, *Macaca mulatta* (all carrying genomic `2617A` and displaying the A-to-U cDNA peak) versus *Lemur catta* and *Nycticebus coucang* (carrying genomic `2617T` with no cDNA modification; Figure 4) provides compelling evolutionary evidence that the post-transcriptional modification at `2617` co-evolved with the `T`-to-`A` substitution in the anthropoid/simian lineage.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reframe the Primary Mechanistic Interpretation Around $N^1$-Methyladenosine (`m1A`) Reverse-Transcriptase Misincorporation and Model `m1A` in Figure 5
* **WHY:** Treating cDNA `T` (>30%) and `G` (~15%) reads at `2617` and `13710` as literal uracil and guanine bases in the native mitochondrial RNA leads to biologically implausible speculation about two-step A$\to$I$\to$U base-replacement enzymes (line 53) and causes the structural model (Figure 5) to evaluate unmodified `U`/`G` instead of the actual modified adenosine base (`m1A`) present in the mitochondrial ribosome.
* **WHAT:** Elevate post-transcriptional adenosine modification (specifically $N^1$-methyladenosine, `m1A`, which blocks the Watson-Crick face during cDNA synthesis and induces RT misincorporation of A/C, read as U/G) to the primary biochemical explanation in the Abstract, Results (line 41), and Discussion (line 53), and model `m1A` alongside `A`, `U`, and `G` in the H71–H64 ribosome structure (Figure 5).
* **HOW:** (1) In Coot/PyMOL (line 86), place an $N^1$-methyladenosine (`1MA` ligand code in the PDB) at the position homologous to `2617` in the `1NKW` / `3R8S` H71 loop and measure the distance and electrostatic/hydrogen-bonding geometry between the positively charged `1MA` ring ($N^6$ amino / $N^1$-methyl) and the adjacent H64 backbone phosphates; and (2) note that testing two different reverse transcriptases with distinct fidelity profiles (e.g., AMV vs. SuperScript III vs. TGIRT) on the same `GM14447` RNA sample is a simple doable check that alters the U:G cDNA incorporation ratio when the underlying event is an `m1A` RT-block rather than a fixed stoichiometric mixture of `U` and `G` RNA transcripts.

#### 2. Quantify the Exact Primer-Extension / Amplicon Boundaries in the Precursor Polycistronic Transcript Assay
* **WHY:** In line 28, the authors compare RDD levels in four RT-PCR amplicons spanning the 5' and 3' processing junctions of the *16S rRNA* precursor polycistronic transcript (3.2%–5.3% A-to-U) against total RNA-seq (>30% A-to-U). However, in Methods line 84, the four PCR fragments (`fragments 1–4`, primers 7–12) were ligated to Illumina adapters and sequenced on a MiSeq **without shearing** (`"to allow adaptors ligation for sequencing in the Illumina MiSeq platform without shearing"`). If the amplicons were sequenced from their ends without fragmentation/shearing, only amplicons where position `2617` lies within the MiSeq read length from the primer 5' end can cover site `2617`, whereas reads starting at the distant tRNA junction would not reach `2617` unless the amplicon was short or sheared.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact genomic coordinates of primers 7–12 relative to position `2617` and the *tRNA-Val* / *16S rRNA* / *tRNA-Leu(UUR)* processing junctions, and explain how sequencing without shearing covered both the uncleaved tRNA junction and internal position `2617` (which lies 947 nt downstream of the 5' end of *16S rRNA* at position `1671` and 612 nt upstream of the 3' end of *16S rRNA* at position `3229`).
* **HOW:** Since position `2617` is >600 bp away from both the 5' (`1671`) and 3' (`3229`) cleavage junctions of *16S rRNA*, a single PCR amplicon that spans a cleavage junction *and* includes position `2617` must be at least >650–1,000 bp long. If that >650-bp PCR product was ligated to Illumina adapters *without shearing* (line 84), a MiSeq read initiating at the adapter on the cleavage-junction end cannot sequence 650–950 bp into position `2617`, and conversely a primer placed close to `2617` within *16S rRNA* would amplify mature cleaved *16S rRNA* unless paired with an external primer in *tRNA-Val* or *tRNA-Leu* (in which case only the internal *16S* end of the un-sheared amplicon reaches `2617`). State the exact primer coordinates, amplicon lengths, and MiSeq read length in the main text (line 28) and Methods (line 84).

* **WHAT & HOW:** Correct the taxonomic spellings (*Pongo pygmaeus*, *Lemur catta*, *Deinococcus radiodurans*) and restore the *E. coli* 23S rRNA coordinate (`1954`) in lines 41 and 86.

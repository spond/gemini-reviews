### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the molecular evolution of the *GNAS1* XL-exon across eight primate species (human, chimpanzee, gorilla, orangutan, gibbon, colobus monkey, macaque, and squirrel monkey). The XL-exon is unusual in encoding two functionally interacting neuroendocrine signaling proteins—XL$\alpha$s and ALEX—in completely overlapping reading frames shifted by +1 nucleotide. The authors sequence the XL-exon across the seven non-human primates, document repeat-number variation in the internal 13-amino-acid repeat region, compare frame-specific nonsynonymous substitution rates ($\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$) using maximum likelihood (`codeml` free-ratio model) and a neighbor-dependent modification of the Nei–Gojobori (NG) method, and perform `evolver` codon simulations and *in silico* CpG deamination analyses. They report that high GC content (68–71%) and elevated CpG density (~20% of the exon) account for both the avoidance of premature stop codons in the overlapping frame and the rapid nucleotide substitution rate (~50% of substitutions at CpG sites), while proposing that XL$\alpha$s and ALEX evolve via "evolutionary oscillation" to maintain mutual binding affinity. Sequence accessions are deposited in GenBank, whereas custom Perl scripts for the modified NG estimator and CpG analyses are available only upon request.

The compositional and mutational insights of the paper—specifically that maintaining two long overlapping reading frames requires high GC content to avoid AT-rich stop codons (TAA, TAG, TGA), which in turn creates a dense CpG mutational hotspot driving rapid sequence divergence under purifying selection—are biologically perceptive and well-supported by the simulation and *in silico* deamination analyses. However, the paper's central statistical claims regarding "evolutionary oscillation" and co-evolutionary rate balancing between XL$\alpha$s and ALEX rest on a self-contradictory statistical argument and a failure to account for the structural non-independence of +1 shifted reading frames. Specifically, the authors claim that branch-specific $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ ratios oscillate across the primate tree while simultaneously reporting that no branch or pairwise ratio is statistically distinguishable from 1.0 ($p = 0.14\text{--}0.77$), and they interpret this lack of statistical power on short primate branches as positive confirmation of co-evolutionary rate equality. These methodological flaws can be resolved through computational re-analysis using explicit joint overlapping-reading-frame substitution models, formal power and null-distribution calibration, and tempered framing of the repeat-variation and oscillation claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **High GC content (68–71%) is required to maintain long overlapping reading frames without stop codons, which secondarily elevates CpG density (~20%) and drives rapid sequence evolution: [Well Supported].**  
  The `evolver` simulations across 1,000,000 replicates demonstrate that coding sequences simulated under average human RefSeq codon usage never retain an intact $\ge 1,000\text{ bp}$ overlapping reading frame in the +1 or $-1$ phase across the eight-primate phylogeny (0%), whereas sequences simulated under XL$\alpha$s or ALEX GC-rich codon usage retain the alternative frame in ~1% of replicates. Furthermore, the empirical CpG enrichment (18–21% vs. 7.3% RefSeq mean, empirical $p = 0.0013$) and the *in silico* CpG deamination analysis (showing that all 109–118 potential CpG $\to$ TpG/CpA transitions generate only 4 stop codons in XL$\alpha$s and 0 in ALEX while causing 140 and 129 amino acid replacements, respectively) provide a coherent, quantitative mutational explanation for why an essential dual-coding locus exhibits high divergence.

* **XL$\alpha$s and ALEX evolve in an "oscillating fashion" across primate lineages while maintaining a balanced $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A \approx 1$ due to compensatory co-evolution: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This headline conclusion suffers from three compounding statistical and modeling flaws:
  1. *Simultaneous claim of oscillation and constancy from the same non-significant p-values:* On the branch-level analysis (Figure 2), the authors state that $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ ratios "vary considerably among branches" (e.g., branches $3 \to \text{Pp}$ and $3 \to 2$) to claim evolutionary "oscillation," yet immediately note that *none* of the branch ratios ($p = 0.14\text{--}0.77$, Fisher's exact test) or pairwise ratios (Table 1, at the 1% level) differ significantly from 1.0. If no branch deviates significantly from the null ratio, the observed branch-to-branch fluctuations are statistically indistinguishable from Poisson sampling noise on short primate branches and cannot be claimed as biological "oscillation." Conversely, failing to reject the null hypothesis $H_0: \text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A = 1$ on sparse branch counts cannot be interpreted as positive statistical proof of co-evolutionary balancing without a power analysis and a neutral overlapping-frame null baseline.
  2. *Violation of independence in single-frame likelihood and Fisher's exact tests:* Because the ALEX reading frame is shifted by +1 nucleotide relative to XL$\alpha$s, codon positions (1, 2, 3) of XL$\alpha$s correspond to codon positions (3, 1, 2) of ALEX. Consequently, every nucleotide substitution at codon position 2 of XL$\alpha$s (0-fold degenerate) is simultaneously at codon position 1 of ALEX (~95% nonsynonymous), meaning a single mutation increments the nonsynonymous count in *both* proteins. Applying `codeml` free-ratio models independently to each frame and testing $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A = 1$ via Fisher's exact test violates conditional independence because the two frames share the exact same underlying nucleotide substitutions.
  3. *Un Derived Neutral Baseline for $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$:* Even without compensatory protein-protein co-evolution, a purely mutational process (dominated by CpG transitions) acting on two overlapping frames with equal selective constraint against stop codons will naturally produce a baseline $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ close to 1.0 because positions 1 and 2 of XL$\alpha$s map to positions 3 and 1 of ALEX while positions 2 and 3 of ALEX map to positions 1 and 2 of XL$\alpha$s (symmetric 0-fold + 2-fold vs. 4-fold degeneracy pairing).

* **The XL-exon experienced repeat expansion in the great ape lineage followed by a dramatic repeat loss specific to the human lineage, potentially linked to species-specific neurological differences: [Overstated].**  
  As the authors note in the Introduction, the repeat region is polymorphic within humans (13-unit major allele vs. 14-unit disease-associated allele at 2.2% frequency in $n = 276$ individuals). However, the comparative analysis sequences only a single individual ($n = 1$) per non-human primate species and explicitly assumes "that the sequenced alleles are fixed in the respective primate populations." Inferring lineage-specific evolutionary expansion and human-specific repeat loss—and speculating on species-specific neurological adaptation—without assessing intraspecific repeat-length polymorphism in chimpanzees, gorillas, or orangutans overstates what a single sequence per taxon can establish.

* **A neighbor-dependent modification of the Nei–Gojobori method accurately estimates $K_A$ and $K_S$ in overlapping reading frames: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Excluding mutational pathways that introduce nonsense codons in the alternative reading frame is a sensible heuristic improvement over standard single-frame NG, and the manuscript candidly notes that because of the 68–71% GC content, nonsense pathways are rare and the resulting estimates closely match standard NG, Yang–Nielsen, and ML estimates. However, this heuristic still treats each codon of the focal frame conditioning only on static flanking nucleotides rather than modeling the joint 4-nucleotide (codon-pair overlap) Markov substitution process.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Formulate a Proper Null Model for $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ Under Overlapping Reading Frame Coupling
* **WHY:** Because codon positions (1, 2, 3) of XL$\alpha$s are positions (3, 1, 2) of ALEX, nucleotide substitutions in the two frames are mechanically coupled. Specifically, substitutions at XL$\alpha$s position 2 are nonsynonymous in both frames, while substitutions at XL$\alpha$s positions 1 and 3 trade off nonsynonymous changes in one frame against largely synonymous changes in the other. A ratio $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A \approx 1$ is therefore expected under simple symmetric purifying selection without any dynamic co-evolutionary "oscillation."
* **WHAT:** Establish the expected null distribution of $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ (and its branch-to-branch variance) under a memoryless, non-oscillating nucleotide substitution process that respects the empirical CpG dinucleotide context and rejects stop codons in both frames.
* **HOW:** Using the authors' existing `evolver` / Perl simulation pipeline, simulate nucleotide evolution along the eight-primate tree starting from the reconstructed ancestral XL-exon sequence, applying empirical transition/transversion and CpG mutation rates while rejecting only nonsense mutations in either frame (or applying constant, independent purifying selection factors $w_{\text{XL}}$ and $w_{\text{ALEX}}$). Compute the simulated branch-specific and pairwise $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ distributions across 10,000 replicates and test whether the empirical branch variance is either significantly *higher* (true oscillation) or significantly *lower* (tight co-evolutionary coupling) than the Poisson expectation.

#### 2. Replace Independent Fisher's Exact Tests with a Joint Overlapping-Frame Likelihood or Permutation Test
* **WHY:** Fisher's exact test assumes independent observations between the two groups being compared, which is violated when a single nucleotide mutation at XL$\alpha$s codon position 2 simultaneously counts toward both $\text{XL } K_A$ and $\text{ALEX } K_A$. Furthermore, non-significant $p$-values ($0.14\text{--}0.77$) on branches with small substitution counts reflect low statistical power rather than proof of rate equality.
* **WHAT:** Perform a formal heterogeneity test across branches (e.g., a likelihood ratio test or multinomial permutation test on the counts of substitutions at nucleotide phases 1, 2, and 3 across branches) and report 95% confidence intervals on $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ for each branch and species pair.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the exact branch-specific substitution counts by codon phase $(n_{b,1}, n_{b,2}, n_{b,3})$ across all branches $b$ of the primate phylogeny from Figure 2. Apply a $B \times 3$ contingency table exact test (or parametric bootstrap under the joint sequence simulation from Suggestion 1) to test whether the phase distribution of substitutions actually shifts significantly between the Old World monkey/orangutan branches and the human/African ape branches.

#### 3. Temper Claims of "Evolutionary Oscillation" and Fixed Lineage-Specific Indels
* **WHY:** If the phase distribution across branches does not reject homogeneity—or if branch-specific $\text{XL } K_A / \text{ALEX } K_A$ ratios do not significantly deviate from 1.0—describing the pattern as "striking oscillation" in the Abstract, headings, and Figure 2 legend is unsupported by the statistical tests presented. Similarly, inferring fixed species-level repeat expansions and losses from $n = 1$ individual per non-human primate ignores known VNTR-like polymorphism at this locus.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the "oscillation" narrative in the Abstract and Results/Discussion to reflect comparable cumulative nonsynonymous substitution rates in both frames under shared mutational and purifying constraints, and explicitly caveat the repeat-number comparison with the sample size of $n = 1$ per non-human species.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract and Results text to state that $\text{XL } K_A$ and $\text{ALEX } K_A$ accumulate at statistically indistinguishable overall rates across primates, and present the compensatory co-evolution + repeat-adjustment ("sandbags on an air balloon") model strictly as a testable mechanistic hypothesis rather than an empirically demonstrated oscillation.

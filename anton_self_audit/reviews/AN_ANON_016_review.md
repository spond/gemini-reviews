### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a Galaxy workflow implementation of the SPRING (Structure-based Protein Interaction Prediction) pipeline, which predicts protein–protein interactions (PPIs) and generates multimeric 3D structural models by threading query sequences with `HHsearch` against the monomeric `PDB70` database (58,900 entries) and mapping homologous pairs onto 988,784 experimental dimeric interface frameworks extracted from 170,860 PDB entries via `PSI-BLAST` (`SPRING Cross`, `SPRING Map`, `SPRING Min-Z`, and `SPRING Model` with `TM-align`). The authors evaluate the pipeline at genome scale on 20,610 human protein-coding genes (~212 million pairs) and 6,045 yeast protein-coding genes (~18 million pairs), comparing predicted interactions against ten BioGRID experimental assay sets using Matthews Correlation Coefficients (MCC; Figure 1). They then apply the pipeline to SARS-CoV-2 proteins against the human proteome, reporting top-scoring putative human substrates for the Nsp3 papain-like protease (PLpro; Table 1, topped by ISG15 and ANKUB) and putative human receptor/binding partners for the SARS-CoV-2 Spike (S) glycoprotein, highlighting ACE2, myelin-oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG; Table 2, Figure 3), and dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP4; Table 3, Figure 4).

Integrating template-based interface threading (`SPRING`, `HHsearch`, `TM-align`) into an automated, shareable Galaxy workflow with self-updating PDB/PDB70 indexing tools (`DBkit`) is a useful bioinformatics contribution. However, both the statistical validation of the genome-wide PPI network and the biological interpretation of the SARS-CoV-2 structural predictions suffer from major methodological flaws: (1) the cross-validation MCC benchmark in Figure 1 suffers from severe template leakage (complexes already solved in the PDB and curated into BioGRID are not excluded from the PDB template library prior to predicting the human and yeast interactomes) and evaluates balanced 1:1 positive-to-negative subsamples where negatives are drawn from different subcellular compartments, artificially inflating precision and MCC compared to real genome-wide screening (~212 million pairs where true positives are $<0.5\%$); (2) the novel prediction that SARS-CoV-2 Spike RBD binds human MOG (Figure 3, Table 2) is a classic template-threading false positive—MOG possesses an immunoglobulin variable (IgV) beta-barrel fold, so `HHsearch` threads MOG onto the variable heavy/light domains of anti-SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibodies (`6XC2`, `7C01`, `7BZ5`, `6XC4`) and synthetic nanobody/sybody (`7C8V`) co-crystal structures; and (3) the pipeline was run on concatenated GenBank CDS records where 20,610 human genes encode ~75,776 isoforms without separating individual proteins (line 17). Addressing template circularity, correcting the antibody-mimicry artifact for MOG, and calibrating genome-wide precision-recall curves are essential before the biological claims can be trusted.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: On the Human (~212 million pairs) and Yeast (~18 million pairs) genomes, the SPRING Galaxy pipeline achieves PPI prediction performance comparable to or better than experimental methods in BioGRID: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The evaluation in Figure 1 and Methods (lines 17, 142–146) contains three compounding flaws:
  1. **Data Leakage / Circularity:** The multimeric template library was constructed from all 170,860 PDB entries available up to November [YEAR] (line 129), and matched via PSI-BLAST ($E \le 0.001$) to PDB70. Many BioGRID experimental interactions (especially under "Co-crystal Structure", "Reconstituted Complex", "Affinity Capture-MS", and "Two-hybrid") are either the exact human/yeast structures deposited in the PDB or close paralogs. Without a strict sequence-identity or temporal holdout excluding human/yeast PDB complexes present in BioGRID, SPRING is largely "predicting" known complexes from their own crystal structures.
  2. **Biased Negative Sampling and 1:1 Class Balance:** In Methods line 144–146, `SPRING MCC` constructs the negative set by sampling pairs from *different subcellular regions* at a size *equal* to the positive set (1:1 ratio). First, because homologous template complexes predominantly occur between co-localized proteins, pairing proteins from disjoint compartments creates an artificially easy negative control. Second, evaluating MCC on a balanced 1:1 subsample completely obscures the false discovery rate across the 212 million evaluated human pairs, where the prior probability of interaction is $\sim 10^{-3}$.
  3. **Unseparated Multi-Isoform CDS Inputs:** Line 17 states: *"Note that we applied SPRING on the raw protein coding sequences without separating the individual proteins using the CDS record provided by GenBank [4]. In total the 20,610 protein coding genes encode for about 75,776 individual proteins."* Concatenating or failing to split alternative isoforms per gene distorts alignment length, $Z$-scores, and `TM-align` coordinates.

* **Claim 2: The pipeline identifies human ubiquitin-like substrates (ISG15, UBB, UBC, SUMO3, etc.) interacting with the SARS-CoV-2 Nsp3 papain-like protease (PLpro): [Well Supported].**  
  Table 1 recovers ISG15 (`SM-score = 1.11`, `TM = 0.96`), UBB (`1.09`), and UBC (`1.02`) as top hits for SARS-CoV-2 PLpro, which is biologically sound because coronavirus PLpro has well-established deubiquitinating and deISGylating activity. However, as the authors themselves acknowledge in Table 1 (`6XAA`, `6XA9`, Klemm [49]) and line 99, these top predictions are directly templated on the already-solved crystal structures of SARS-CoV-2 PLpro in complex with ISG15 (`6XAA`) and di-ubiquitin (`6XA9`), and theRemaining hits (ANKUB, ELOB, MIDN, TMUB1, SUMO3, UBL7, RAD23A/B, IQUB, UBD) are all ubiquitin-fold domain proteins threaded onto the exact same ubiquitin/ISG15 binding interface (`6XAA`, `5W8T`, `5WFI`, `5BZ0`). Thus, while the structural alignments are valid, they represent ubiquitin-fold homology detection rather than de novo discovery.

* **Claim 3: SARS-CoV-2 Spike (S) RBD forms a putative complex with human myelin-oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG) via two distinct binding modes that may explain MOGAD relapse in COVID-19 patients: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This claim (lines 54–68, Figure 3, Table 2) is a classic domain-superfamily threading artifact. MOG extracellular domain has an immunoglobulin variable (IgV) beta-barrel fold. When `HHsearch` threads human MOG against `PDB70`, it inevitably hits the IgV heavy/light chain domains of antibodies and nanobodies (`Z-score = 102.2`) because all immunoglobulins share the highly conserved Ig beta-barrel framework. Consequently, `SPRING Min-Z` maps MOG onto five PDB co-crystal structures of SARS-CoV-2 Spike RBD bound to **neutralizing antibodies and synthetic nanobodies** (`7C8V`: Sybody SR4; `6XC2`: Antibody CC12.1; `7C01`: Antibody CB6; `7BZ5`: Antibody B38; `6XC4`: Antibody CC12.3; Table 2). Antibody complementarity-determining0 regions (CDRs) are hypervariable loops evolved specifically by somatic hypermutation to bind Spike RBD; the fact that MOG shares the underlying IgV beta-sheet scaffold with antibodies does not mean MOG's loops have binding affinity for SARS-CoV-2 Spike RBD (nor would this explain why thousands of other human Ig-superfamily proteins do not functionally act as SARS-CoV-2 receptors).

* **Claim 4: SARS-CoV-2 Spike RBD is predicted to bind human DPP4 at sites shared with MERS-CoV plus novel sites (T186, T282, S334), potentially explaining higher COVID-19 mortality in diabetics: [Overstated].**  
  The DPP4 model (Figure 4, Table 3) arises because SARS-CoV-2 RBD is homologous to MERS-CoV RBD, so `SPRING` threads SARS-CoV-2 RBD onto the MERS-CoV RBD–human DPP4 crystal structures (`4KR0` and `4L72`, $Z_{\text{com}} = 216.3$ and $213.4$). However, experimental surface plasmon resonance and cell-entry studies have repeatedly demonstrated that despite sharing the core betacoronavirus RBD fold with MERS-CoV, SARS-CoV-2 RBD has completely diverged in the receptor-binding motif (RBM) and does **not** bind human DPP4. The modest `TM-score` of `0.62` and `SM-score` of `0.69` (line 75)—well below the $>0.85–1.10$ scores in Tables 1–2—reflect this poor interface complementarity.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate PDB Template Leakage and Benchmark Unbalanced Genome-Wide Precision-Recall
* **WHY:** Evaluating human and yeast interactome predictions against BioGRID while retaining all human and yeast experimental complexes in the PDB template library (and evaluating MCC on a 1:1 balanced sample with different-compartment negatives) severely overestimates real-world predictive accuracy.
* **WHAT:** Perform a leak-free benchmark by holding out homologous templates and reporting precision-recall (PR) and MCC across realistic class-imbalance ratios.
* **HOW:** (1) Re-run `SPRING Min-Z` on the human and yeast datasets after excluding any PDB template chain with $>30\%$ (or $>70\%$) sequence identity to both query proteins in a pair; (2) evaluate performance using both co-localized (same subcellular compartment) non-interacting negatives and random genome-wide negatives at realistic positive:negative ratios (e.g., 1:100 and 1:1000) in addition to 1:1; and (3) ensure each gene is represented by its canonical UniProt protein sequence rather than unseparated multi-isoform GenBank CDS concatenations (line 17).

#### 2. Recognize and Control for Immunoglobulin-Fold Antibody Mimicry in the MOG–Spike Prediction
* **WHY:** All five multimeric templates supporting the putative MOG–Spike RBD interaction (`7C8V`, `6XC2`, `7C01`, `7BZ5`, `6XC4`; Table 2) are anti-SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibodies or sybodies. Because antibody specificity resides entirely in the hypervariable CDR loops rather than the conserved Ig beta-barrel framework (`TM-align`), threading any human Ig-superfamily protein (such as MOG, CD4, PD-1, or ICAM-1) onto an antibody–antigen complex will produce high backbone `TM-scores` (>0.80) despite zero CDR loop complementarity.
* **WHAT:** Filter out antibody/nanobody–antigen complexes from general receptor homology threading, or explicitly evaluate interface loop sequence identity and binding free energy, and reframe the MOG discussion accordingly.
* **HOW:** (1) Check the sequence identity and atomic clash/contact energy ($E_{\text{contact}}$) specifically restricted to the aligned CDR1/CDR2/CDR3 contact loops of `6XC2`/`7C8V` versus MOG (note that in Table 2, $E_{\text{contact}}$ for the antibody templates `6XC2` to `6XC4` is very weak: $-2.54$ to $-3.53$, compared to $-15$ to $-24$ for PLpro substrates in Table 1); (2) add a filter option in the Galaxy workflow to exclude PDB templates annotated as immunoglobulin/antibody/nanobody chains (`SAbDab` or IMGT annotations) when screening for endogenous host receptors; and (3) revise the Abstract, Results (lines 54–68), and Discussion to explain that the MOG hit reflects Ig-domain structural homology to neutralizing antibodies rather than a confirmed physiological receptor interaction.

#### 3. Temper Claims Regarding SARS-CoV-2 Spike Binding to DPP4 and Distinguish Known vs. Novel PLpro Templates
* **WHY:** The DPP4 model has a low `SM-score` (`0.69`) and `TM-score` (`0.62`, line 75) because it forces SARS-CoV-2 RBD onto the MERS-CoV RBD–DPP4 crystal structure (`4L72`/`4KR0`), and experimental assays show SARS-CoV-2 does not use DPP4 for entry. Similarly, in Table 1, `6XAA` and `6XA9` (Klemm [49]) are actual crystal structures of SARS-CoV-2 PLpro bound to ISG15 and Ubiquitin.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly distinguish self-template recovery (`6XAA`/`6XA9` for ISG15/UBB/UBC) from homologous template transfer, and caveat the DPP4 structural model.
* **HOW:** In Table 1, annotate which PDB entries are native SARS-CoV-2 PLpro co-crystal structures (`6XAA`, `6XA9`) versus homologous viral PLpro/USP templates (`5W8T`, `5WFI`, `5BZ0`, `4WUR`). In the DPP4 section (lines 70–89), explicitly note that the model transfers the MERS-CoV–DPP4 interface (`4L72`, `4KR0`) at moderate structural similarity (`TM = 0.62`, `SM = 0.69`) and cite the experimental literature showing that native Wuhan-Hu-1 SARS-CoV-2 RBD does not measurably bind human DPP4 in vitro, framing the model instead as a structural comparison of why MERS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2 differ at the DPP4 interface.

#### 4. Restore the Missing Mathematical Formula for `SM-score` in Methods
* **WHY:** Line 148–150 introduces the `SPRING model score` (`SM-score`) with a colon followed by a blank line (`"evaluated by the recently established SPRING model score [ 38 ]: \n\n where TM-score is..."`), omitting the actual equation.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Insert the explicit mathematical formula on line 149:
  $$\text{SM-score} = \text{TM-score} - w_0 E_{\text{contact}}$$
  (with $w_0 = 0.01$ as defined on line 150), and verify that all values in Tables 1 and 2 satisfy this identity (for example, in Table 1 row 1, $\text{TM} = 0.96$ and $E_{\text{contact}} = -15.07$ gives $0.96 - 0.01(-15.07) = 1.1107 \approx 1.11$).

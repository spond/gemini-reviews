### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents an automated, modular genome assembly pipeline (version 2.1) developed jointly by the Vertebrate Genomes Project (VGP) and the European Reference Genome Atlas (ERGA) and deployed on a public cloud-backed workflow platform across global instances (US, EU, and Australia). The pipeline is organized into 10 modular workflows supporting eight analysis trajectories—including solo PacBio HiFi assembly, Hi-C phased/scaffolded assembly, and trio-binned assembly using parental short reads—alongside dedicated mitochondrial genome assembly, exogenous and mitochondrial decontamination, Bionano optical mapping scaffolding, and extensive quality control (GenomeScope k-mer profiling, Merqury haplotype completeness/QV, and BUSCO completeness). The authors validate the trajectories on the zebra finch (*Taeniopygia guttata*) reference genome and benchmark the pipeline across 51 vertebrate species with PacBio HiFi and Hi-C data, comparing the resulting assemblies against 19 earlier PacBio continuous long-read (CLR) assemblies.

This is a well-engineered, practical bioinformatic resource paper that makes chromosome-scale, haplotype-phased reference genome assembly accessible on public computational infrastructure without requiring local HPC installation. The workflows, tutorials, and resulting assemblies (deposited in GenomeArk and NCBI BioProject PRJNA489243) are openly documented and reproducible. The primary limitation of the manuscript as written is that nearly all quantitative validation metrics, comparative statistics (such as exact QV improvements, switch error rates, contig/scaffold NG50 gains over CLR assemblies, and scaffolding strategy comparisons), and the mentioned *XBP1* gene analysis are relegated to the Supplementary Information or visual rings in Figure 2 rather than reported with summary statistics and hypothesis tests in the main text. Summarizing these quantitative benchmarks directly in the main text and clarifying coverage-vs-continuity trade-offs would further strengthen the manuscript.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The 10-workflow VGP assembly pipeline reliably produces chromosome-scale, haplotype-resolved vertebrate assemblies across 51 species: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 2 and the accompanying workflow architecture (Figure 1) demonstrate consistent execution across 51 diverse vertebrate genomes spanning ~1 Gb to >8 Gb in size, with high Merqury k-mer completeness, high BUSCO vertebrate orthology completeness, and scaffold NG50 values approaching chromosome scale across multiple vertebrate clades.

* **PacBio HiFi + Hi-C assemblies generated by the pipeline outperform 19 previous PacBio CLR-based assemblies of similar size and complexity: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the improvement of HiFi over CLR technology is well established and referenced (line 150, citing Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 5, and Supplementary Fig. 6), the main text reports no summary numbers (e.g., median fold-improvement in contig NG50, consensus quality value [QV], frameshift error rates, or structural accuracy) nor details how the 19 CLR assemblies were matched for genome size, repeat content, and sequencing depth.

* **Hi-C Hifiasm phasing resolves haplotypes effectively even for large (~4.3 Gbp), repeat-rich genomes when parental trio data are unavailable: [Well Supported].**  
  The pipeline's integration of Hi-C phasing within Hifiasm is validated on the benchmark zebra finch dataset and across the 51-species vertebrate cohort (including large, repeat-rich genomes in Figure 2), providing a practical solo-specimen alternative when parental samples cannot be collected in the wild.

* **Recommended sequencing depths of 30x–60x PacBio HiFi and 30x Hi-C per haplotype ensure uniform Poisson sampling and accurate assembly of repetitive regions: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The coverage guidelines (line 137) are grounded in standard Lander-Waterman Poisson sampling theory (reference 7) and VGP empirical experience, though the main text does not present a downsampling sensitivity curve showing where contig continuity or repeat resolution degrades between 30x and 60x coverage across the 51 genomes.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Quantitative Summary Statistics for the 51 HiFi vs. 19 CLR Benchmark in the Main Text
* **WHY:** Line 150 states that 51 HiFi+Hi-C assemblies were compared against 19 previous CLR assemblies, but not a single quantitative metric from this comparison appears in the main text.
* **WHAT:** Add a concise summary sentences or small inline table in the main text reporting the median and interquartile range (IQR) of contig NG50, scaffold NG50, Merqury QV, Merqury phasing switch/hamming error, and BUSCO completeness across the 51 HiFi assemblies versus the 19 CLR assemblies.
* **HOW:** Extract the summary distributions directly from Supplementary Table 5 and Supplementary Fig. 6, and report the exact matched comparison metrics (controlling for genome size and repeat fraction) in the main text around line 150.

#### 2. Reconcile Author Contributions with Main Text Content (*XBP1* Analysis)
* **WHY:** The Author Contributions statement (line 129) states that "A.N. performed the analyses on XBP1," yet *XBP1* is never mentioned anywhere in the main text or figure legends of this manuscript.
* **WHAT:** Clarify where the *XBP1* locus validation resides and briefly state its outcome in the main text (e.g., as an example of resolving a previously gapped or misassembled locus in the HiFi vs. CLR comparison).
* **HOW:** Add a 1–2 sentence summary in the validation paragraph (lines 148–150) referencing the specific supplementary section or figure where the *XBP1* assembly comparison is presented.

#### 3. Quantify Computational Runtime, Storage Footprint, and Cloud Cost Scaling
* **WHY:** A primary goal of the manuscript is scaling reference genome production by two orders of magnitude on public infrastructure (lines 133, 139). Prospective users and infrastructure providers need to know the CPU/memory hours and peak disk footprint required to assemble a 1-Gb versus a 4-Gb genome through the 10 workflows.
* **WHAT:** Report empirical runtime and peak memory/storage metrics as a function of genome size and repeat content across the 51-species dataset.
* **HOW:** Aggregate the job execution logs from the public workflow instances for the 51 assemblies and state the typical wall-clock time, core-hours, and intermediate storage required for solo, Hi-C, and trio trajectories.

* **WHY:** In the legend for Figure 2 (line 155, item ix), the radial axis lines for genome size are listed as "lines at 9, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 Gb," where `9` is a typographical error for `1` (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 Gb).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Change `lines at 9, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 Gb` to `lines at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 Gb` in the Figure 2 caption (line 155).

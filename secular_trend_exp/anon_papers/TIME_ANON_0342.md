## Abstract

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is a malignant tumor type with the fastest growing incidence and mortality rates worldwide, accounting for over 80% of all lung cancer cases and presenting a substantial menace to human health. Abnormal expression of long non-coding RNAs has been linked to the advancement of NSCLC. RP11-480I12.5 is highly overexpressed in NSCLC, nevertheless its function remains unknown.

qPCR was utilized to analyze the expression levels of RP11-480I12.5 in NSCLC cell lines. To elucidate the functional significance of RP11-480I12.5, H1650 and Calu-1 cells were infected with lentivirus carrying RP11-480I12.5 -shRNA or IGF2BP3 -shRNA. Subsequently, cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay, cell proliferation capacity was evaluated through colony formation experiments, and apoptosis rates were determined using flow cytometry. The interactions between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 were assessed through RNA‑binding protein immunoprecipitation assay (RIP) and RNA pull-down assays.

RP11-480I12.5 was considerably increased in both the lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous carcinoma (LUSC) subtypes of NSCLC. IGF2BP3, which is extensively expressed in NSCLC, has poor predictive relevance, notably in LUAD. Interaction tests indicated a strong binding between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3, indicating a functional synergy in NSCLC pathogenesis. The co-regulated gene, WDHD1 , which was considerably increased in NSCLC, facilitated the tumorigenic effects of the RP11–480I12.5–IGF2BP3 axis via influencing cell proliferation and death.

RP11-480I12.5 interacted with IGF2BP3 to increase WDHD1 expression, promoting NSCLC cell proliferation and anti-apoptotic ability. Current findings elucidate how RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 interact at the molecular level, which has important implications for the progression of NSCLC.

## Introduction

Lung cancer is the malignancy with the highest incidence and mortality rates globally [ 1 ]. According to statistics from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), in [YEAR], there were approximately 2.5 million new cases diagnosed, with over 1.8 million related deaths [ 1 ]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common histological subtype, accounting for 80–85% of cases, and primarily divided into lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) [ 2 , 3 ]. Although surgical resection is still typically the top choice for treating early stage NSCLC, only about one-quarter of patients are eligible for this therapy, and about 30–55% of those who undergo surgery may experience cancer recurrence [ 4 ].

lncRNA aberrant expression can have profound effects on gene transcription, mRNA processing, and tumor biology, thereby accelerating oncogenic initiation and progression. Indeed, studies have shown dysregulation of a plethora of lncRNAs in NSCLC, where they play regulatory roles in RNA transcription and post-transcriptional modifications, orchestrating the activation of cancer cell cycles, metastasis, and sensitivity to radiotherapy and chemotherapy [ 5 ]. Researchers have proposed lncRNA expression quantification as a biomarker to delineate clinical–pathological characteristics, forecasting long-term prognoses, and gauging therapeutic susceptibilities [ 6 ]. Moreover, insights into lncRNA involvement in drug resistance mechanisms relevant to chemotherapy and targeted therapeutic modalities [ 6 , 7 ], are reshaping the therapeutic landscape, highlighting their potential as strategic targets in cancer therapeutics. Among the burgeoning cohort of lncRNAs, lncRNA RP11-480I12.5, emerges as an RNA transcript derived from the tubulin alpha 5 pseudogene (TUBA5P). Recent research has shown that lncRNA RP11-480I12.5 can cause cancer in breast and cervical cancer. It does this by increasing cell growth, migration, and the ability to invade other cells. This speeds up the disease by changing the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and the miR-490-3p/AURKA/Wnt signaling axes [ 8 , 9 ]. However, the function of lncRNA RP11-480I12.5 in NSCLC has not been fully elucidated.

The structural architecture of lncRNAs can be broadly categorized into two types: interaction elements, which form direct physical associations with other nucleic acids, proteins, or lipids, and structural elements, which participate in the formation of secondary and/or tertiary three-dimensional RNA structures [ 10 ]. Researchers have reported that the lncRNA PTTG3P maintains mRNA stability through its interaction with Interleukin enhancer-binding factor 3 (ILF3) and forms a positive feedback loop with E2F transcription factor 1 (E2F1), thereby accelerating the progression of NSCLC [ 11 ]. The mitochondrial lncRNA RMRP modulates NSCLC cancer stemness attributes by orchestrating the recruitment of the transcription factor YBX1 to the TGFBR1 promoter locus [ 12 ]. Exploring the network of interactions between lncRNAs and their partner molecules constitutes a promising research area in the field of molecular biology. RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are a group of proteins that shape the function and fate of RNA by attaching to specific RNA elements. They are key players in many processes, from RNA transcription to terminal degradation, and they perform functions, such as localization, stabilization, and modification [ 13 , 14 ]. Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 3 (IGF2BP3), a constituent of the evolutionarily preserved single-stranded RNA-binding oncofetal protein family, is architecturally characterized by two RNA recognition motif (RRM) domains and four K homology (KH) domains [ 15 ]. A minimum of 15 distinct tumor types have shown significant overexpression of IGF2BP3 [ 16 ]. Studies have identified IGF2BP3 as upregulated in the context of NSCLC, emphasizing its significance in tumor initiation and cancer development [ 17 ]. As a key post-transcriptional regulator, IGF2BP3 manifests a multifaceted array of physiological functions, including the promotion of embryonic development in organisms and the induction of fetal hemoglobin synthesis in adult erythroid cells [ 18 ]. Furthermore, IGF2BP3 has been demonstrated to serve as an m 6 A reader, bolstering target RNA stability and augmenting translational efficiency [ 18 ]. These findings herald IGF2BP3 as a prospective therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker.

In preliminary research, m 6 A binding sites within lncRNA RP11-480I12.5 were identified, hinting at a plausible interaction with IGF2BP3. Subsequently, the functional roles and molecular mechanisms of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC were validated, underscoring their clinical relevance and exploring potential downstream pathways. This discovery broadens the understanding of the roles played by RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC and paves the way for the development of pharmacological targeting strategies centered around the interactions between lncRNAs and RBPs.


## Methods


## Bioinformatics analysis

GEPIA2 (Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2, http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/ ) was applied for conducting cancer genomics research. This involved comparing the expression profiles of target genes in LUAD and LUSC tissue samples with healthy control. The screening criteria for lncRNA RP11-480I12.5 were set at |log 2 FC|≤ 0.5 and P < 0.01, whereas its four transcripts (001, 002, 003, 004), along with IGF2BP3 and WDHD1 , were screened under criteria of |log 2 FC|≤ 1 and P < 0.05. In addition, GEPIA2 facilitated correlation analysis between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 examination of IGF2BP3 and WDHD1 expression pattern across tumor stages, and the analysis of overall survival (OS) curves.

CPTAC (Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium, https://proteomics.cancer.gov/data-portal ) was utilized for conducting cancer proteomic research, comparing the expression profiles of target proteins in LUAD and LUSC tissue samples to healthy normal samples.

SRAMP ( http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp/ ) was utilized to forecast the sites of m 6 A modifications within the transcripts of RP11-480I12.5 by inputting the respective RNA sequence. m6A2Target ( http://m6a2target.canceromics.org/ ) [ 19 ] was utilized to analyze and predict the potential target genes (writers, erasers, and readers, WER) of RP11-480I12.5-002.

Enrichr ( https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/ ) was utilized to perform a KEGG enrichment analysis based on the top 100 genes most positively associated with RP11-480I12.5-002. Furthermore, the Similar Gene Detection feature in GEPIA2 enabled the identification of 100 genes most closely associated with RP11-480I12.5-002 and IGF2BP3 , respectively. Subsequently, the "Venn" package in R was employed to determine the genes shared between these two sets.


## Cell culture

The NSCLC cell lines A549 (TCHu150), H1299 (TCHu160), H1650 (TCHu152), and Calu-1 (TCHu192) were obtained from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures, China. A549 cells were cultured in F-12 K basal medium (N6658, Sigma, USA), supplemented with 1% L-alanyl-L-glutamine. Both H1299 and H1650 cells were grown in RPMI-1640 basal medium (01-100-1ACS, BI, Israel), and Calu-1 cells were cultured in McCoy's 5 A basal medium (C3020-0500, VivaCell, Shanghai, China) [ 20 ]. All media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (10100147, Gibco, NY, USA), 100 μg/mL streptomycin and 100 U/mL penicillin ( PB180120 , Procell, Wuhan, China). Cultivation of all cell lines took place within a humidified incubator (Forma 371, Thermo, MA, USA) set at 37 °C, with an atmosphere containing 5% CO 2 .


## Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Cells were seeded at a density of 4 × 10 5 /well in a 6-well plate (3516, Corning, NY, USA). Upon reaching approximately 80% confluence, the cells were harvested and isolated for RNA extraction using Trizol Reagent (15596026, Invitrogen, CA, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from the total RNA using a reverse transcription kit (R333-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, China) in RNase-free tubes, following the instructions to ensure optimal cDNA quality and yield. The reverse transcription procedure was conducted in a thermocycler set at 50 °C for 15 min. RT-qPCR was conducted on a qTOWER 3G real-time PCR system (Analytik Jena, Germany) utilizing the SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Takara, Catalog No. RR820A). Each sample was analyzed in triplicate. The relative mRNA expression levels of IGF2BP3 , RP11-480I12.5 (including its specific transcript variant 002) and WDHD1 were normalized to GAPDH and calculated using the 2 −∆∆Ct method [ 21 ]. The primer pairs are listed in Table S1.


## Construction of stable lentiviral transfectants

For the construction of a stable cell line with the silenced RP11-480I12.5-002 (shRP11) gene, a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) sequence (5’-GATCGCAGTGATAACTCCTTCAACTCGAGTTGAAGGAGTTATCACTGCTTTTTG-3’) targeting the conserved region of the lncRNA RP11 was designed and chemically synthesized. The interfering sequence was inserted into a lentiviral plasmid JS039 (pHBLV–U6–MCS–PGK–PURO) through BamH I and EcoR I restriction enzyme (R0136, R0101, NEB, MA, USA) [ 22 ]. The recombinant was mixed with the packaging plasmids at a predetermined ratio and incubated at room temperature (25 °C) for 25 min. The transfection mixture was then used to infect 293T cells in a serum-free medium. In addition, the medium was replaced with a full culture medium to continue the culture after 4 h. The harvested viral supernatant, containing the lentivirus particles, was utilized to infect the target cells, H1650 and Calu-1, at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 100. The cells were further selected with 2 μg/ml Puromycin (SX50113, Shanghai Shaoxinbio Co., LTD, China).

To construct the stable cell line for shIGF2BP3, IGF2BP3 shRNA sequence (shIGF2BP3#1: 5’-GATCCGGTGAATGAACTTCAGAATTCTCGAGAATTCTGAAGTTCATT CACCGTTTTTG-3’, shIGF2BP3#2: 5’-GATCGCAGGAATTGACGCTGTATAACTCGAGTTA TACAGCGTCAATTCCTGCTTTTTG-3’, or shIGF2BP3#3: 5’-GATCTCTGCGGCTTGTAAG TCTATTCTCGAGAATAGACTTACAAGCCGCAGATTTTTG-3’) was inserted into the JS039 vector to construct IGF2BP3 interference recombinant plasmids. After virus packaging, these were used to infect H1650 and Calu-1 cells at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 100. Empty vector was used as a control. The knockdown efficiency was detected by qPCR.


## Small interfering RNA (siRNA)

siRNA and siRNA control (si-NC) targeting WDHD1 were designed and synthesized at GenePharma (Shanghai). The siRNA sequences were as follows: siWDHD1-1-sense: 5'-CCCUGCUGUUGCUAUAUUATT-3', siWDHD1-1-antisense: 5'-UAAUAUAGCAACAGCAGGGTT-3'; siWDHD1-2-sense: 5'-GGAACUCUA UUGGAAUUAUTT-3', siWDHD1-2-antisense: 5'-AUAAUUCCAAUAGAGUUCCTT-3'; siWDHD1-3-sense: 5'-GGCGUUCAGUUAUAUUUCATT-3', siWDHD1-3-antisense: 5'-UGAAA UAUAACUGAACGCCTT-3'; siNC-sense: 5'-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3', siNC- antisense: 5'-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3'.

Cells were cultured in a 24-well plate at a seeding density of 1 × 10 5 cells per well. The GP-transfect-mate transfection reagent ( G04009 , GenePharma, Shanghai, China) [ 23 ] was mixed with serum-free basal medium, incubated at room temperature for 5 min, and then dropwise added to the diluted siRNA. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 20 min to form the transfection complex. The cells were subjected to a 6 h incubation period with the transfection complex, and subsequently underwent incubation in a complete culture medium for 48 h. Cells were then collected for subsequent experiments.


## Actinomycin D chase assay

After 24 h of transduction, cells were treated with actinomycin D (HY-17559, MedChemExpress, USA) at a final concentration of 5 µg/mL to investigate the effect of shRP11 and shIGF2BP3 on the stability of RP11-480I12.5 and WDHD1 mRNAs [ 24 ]. Cells were collected at various timepoints post-actinomycin D treatment (immediately (0 h) and at 3, 6, 12, and 24 h), followed by total RNA extraction and RT-qPCR to detect the relative expression levels of RP11-480I12.5 and WDHD1 . The results at each timepoint were presented as the percent remaining mRNA with the 0 h data set as 100%.


## Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Probes RP11-480I12.5-002 and RP11-480I12.5-004 were custom synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China); their exact sequences are provided in Table S2. Cell smears were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (G1113, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) for 20 min, after which they were delineated within a hydrophobic boundary drawn using an immunohistochemistry pen (G6100, Servicebio, Wuhan, China). A 5-min digestion with 20 μg/ml proteinase K (G1234, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) preceded the pre-hybridization step, conducted in a humidified environment at 37 °C for 1 h. Subsequently, slides were incubated with the addition of probe-containing hybridization solution (500 nM of either RP11-480I12.5-002 or RP11-480I12.5-004) in a wet box at 40 °C overnight. The next day, the slide washing was performed using the SSC buffer (G3016-4, Servicebio, Wuhan, China). The slides underwent a second round of hybridization at 40 °C for 45 min. This entire process was repeated to ensure thorough hybridization, and to enhance signal detection. DAPI was used to counterstain the nuclei. Single 2D optical sections were captured at the plane of maximal nuclear diameter using an inverted fluorescence microscope NIKON ECLIPSE CI (Japan), with blue representing the nuclei and red representing the Cy3-labeled probes. All reagents and instruments were pretreated with diethyl pyrocarbonate (DEPC; E174, Amresco, OH, USA) [ 25 ].


## Immunofluorescence (IF)

Cell smears were fixed using a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for a duration of 20 min. Subsequently, the slides underwent permeabilization with a 0.5% Triton X-100 solution (X100, Amresco, OH, USA) for 5 min, and were then blocked with 10% goat serum in 0.1% Triton X-100 PBS for a period of 1 h. The primary anti-IGF2BP3 antibody (14642-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) was diluted to 1:50 and incubated with cells at 4 °C overnight. Following this, the fluorescent secondary antibody FITC-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (1:500, A0562, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was used to incubate at room temperature, away from light, for 2 h. DAPI was used to counterstain the nuclei. Single 2D optical sections were captured at the plane of maximal nuclear diameter, with a green signal to indicate the presence of IGF2BP3 protein [ 25 ].


## Western blotting

Cells were initially cultured in a 6-well plate at a density of 4 × 10 5 cells per well. Upon reaching an approximate confluence of 80%, the cells were harvested and total protein extraction was conducted utilizing RIPA buffer (P0013C, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The lysates were centrifuged to collect the supernatant, followed by protein quantification with a BCA Protein Assay Kit (P0010, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Subsequently, the protein samples were separated by 12% SDS–PAGE, and transferred onto 0.45 μm PVDF membranes (B1000B, Invitrogen, CA, USA) [ 26 ]. After being blocked with 5% skim milk, the membranes were incubated with the primary anti-IGF2BP3 antibody (1:6000, 64 kDa, 14642-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) or the primary anti-WDHD1 antibody (1:2500, 120 kDa, AY0169, Abways, Shanghai, China) at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Expression levels of the target proteins were normalized to those of GAPDH (1:5000, 36 kDa, AB0037, Abways, China). The bands showing immunoreactivity were visualized using an ECL Kit (P0018S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), images were captured using the Tanon 4600 system.


## Cell viability assay

After suspending the cells, they were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 2 × 10 4 cells/well. The cells were then cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO 2 for 0, 24, 48 and 72 h. Assessment of cellular viability was carried out using the CCK8 assay kit (15596026, Invitrogen, CA, USA) [ 27 ]. According to the manufacturer's protocol, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to the cells and co-incubated for 60 min at 37 °C. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (1510, Thermo, MA, USA).


## Clone formation assay

Cell cultures were enzymatically dissociated using trypsin (25200072, Gibco, NY, USA) and prepared into a cell suspension. Calu-1 cells were seeded at a concentration of 700 cells/well, whereas H1650 cells were seeded at 200 cells/well in a 6-well plate, with 2 mL of culture medium added to each well for growth as previously described [ 27 ]. The plate was placed in a 37 °C incubator for a cultivation period of 12 days, during which the size of the colonies was observed. After cultivation, cells underwent a series of procedural steps, including washing, fixation, and staining with 0.2% crystal violet (C0121, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for a duration of 15 min. Subsequently, images depicting the colonies were obtained utilizing an inverted microscope (RVL-100-G, ECHO, CA, USA).


## Cell apoptosis

Subsequent to suspending the cells, they were seeded into a 12-well plate at a density of 3 × 10 5 cells/well. The next day, cisplatin was added to the wells at a concentration of 5 µg/L. After a 24-h exposure period, the cells were harvested and subjected to detection of cellular apoptosis using an assay kit (P-CA-066, Procell, Wuhan, China). According to the manufacturer's protocol, 5 μL of Annexin-V-APC and 10 μL of 7AAD Staining Solution were added to the cells for dual staining, and the cells were co-incubated for 15 min at room temperature. Flow cytometry and CellQuest software [ 28 ] were then used to analyze the proportion of apoptotic cells.


## RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP assays were conducted using a Magna RIP ® kit (17–700, Sigma, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol [ 29 ]. Briefly, 2 × 10 7 cells were harvested and lysed in an equal volume of RIP lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors (1 mM) and RNase inhibitors (10 U/mL) on ice for 5 min. A total of 5 μg of the anti-IGF2BP3 antibody was pre-incubated with protein A/G magnetic beads at room temperature for 30 min to form the antibody-bound bead complex. The cell lysate was centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and 100 μL of the supernatant was incubated with the antibody-bead complex in RIP immunoprecipitation buffer at 4 °C overnight. Proteinase K buffer was incorporated to facilitate protein digestion at 55 °C for 30 min. The RNA was precipitated by adding mixture of solution composed of anhydrous ethanol supplemented with 5% Salt Solution I, 1.5% Salt Solution II, and 0.5% Precipitate Enhancer, and kept at −80 °C overnight. Finally, the RNA precipitate was dissolved in DEPC–H 2 O. Normal rabbit IgG (CST) was employed as the negative control. The enrichment level of RP11-480I12.5-002 was quantified through RT-qPCR analysis.


## RNA pull-down

RNA pull-down assay was conducted using a Pierce Magnetic RNA–Protein Pull-down Kit (20164, Thermo, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol [ 30 ]. Briefly, 2 × 10 7 cells were harvested to prepare cell lysates. Target RNA was labeled using the RNA 3’ Desthiobiotinylation Kit (20163, Thermo, MA, USA), and then added to magnetic beads in a microcentrifuge tube and washed with Tris–HCl (pH7.5). Subsequently, the cell lysates were incubated with streptavidin agarose magnetic beads at room temperature for 1 h. The supernatant was removed and stored for western blot analysis.


## Statistical method

Protein bands were analyzed using ImageJ software (version 1.48; NIH) [ 31 ]. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software [ 32 ], and the data were expressed as mean ± SD. For comparisons between two groups, an unpaired Student's t test was used. For comparisons among three or more groups, one-way ANOVA was performed, followed by a Tukey's HSD test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results


## RP11-480I12.5 exhibits significantly elevated expression in NSCLC and is associated with IGF2BP3

Through GEPIA2 analysis, RP11-480I12.5 was identified as significantly upregulated in NSCLC (LUAD and LUSC) (Fig. 1 A), with transcript 001 exhibiting significant upregulation in both LUAD and LUSC, transcript 002 and 003 showing significant upregulation in LUAD but not in LUSC, and transcript 004 displaying no significant expression in LUAD and LUSC (Fig. 1 B). Subsequent prediction analysis using the online tool SRAMP indicated potential m 6 A modification sites present in the sequences of transcripts 002 (GGACU) and 004 (GGACA) (Fig. 1 C). Thus, the research focus was directed towards RP11-480I12.5 and its transcript 002. Analysis using m6A2Target revealed that METTL3 , YTHDF2 , HNRNPG , HNRNPC , and IGF2BP3 predominantly bound to RP11-480I12.5 (Fig. 1 D), prompting IGF2BP3 as the primary target for further investigation due to its significant expression levels in LUAD and LUSC (Fig. 1 E). In addition, a weak correlation ( R ≥ 0.2) between RP11-480I12.5 and transcript 002 with IGF2BP3 in NSCLC (LUAD and LUSC) was observed (Fig. 1 F).

## IGF2BP3 is significantly upregulated in NSCLC and is associated with poor prognosis

Using GEPIA2, the analysis showed a significant upregulation of IGF2BP3 in both LUAD and LUSC (Fig. 2 A), with expression levels increasing as the tumor stage advanced (Fig. 2 B). The analysis of the CPTAC database confirmed the significant upregulation of IGF2BP3 in LUAD and LUSC (Fig. 2 C). A comparative analysis using GEPIA2 was performed on the TCGA database to compare the OS of LUAD and LUSC patients. The results indicated that higher expression of IGF2BP3 mRNA was significantly associated with poorer survival rates in patients with LUAD (OS: HR = 1.6, P = 0.0019, Fig. 2 D), whereas there was no notable association with survival rate in LUSC patients (OS: HR = 0.89, P = 0.38, Fig. 2 E). In addition, Enrichr was used to perform a KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on the top 100 genes linked to RP11-480I12.5 (positively correlated), which indicated connections to ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis and the spliceosome (Fig. 2 F). Furthermore, the intersection of the top 100 genes associated with RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 identified 7 genes, with GEPIA2 analysis confirming that only WDHD1 and MSH2 were significantly upregulated in both LUAD and LUSC (Fig. 2 G).

## Expression level and subcellular location of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC cell lines

qPCR analysis revealed the overexpression of RP11-480I12.5 along with its transcript 002 in NSCLC cells, particularly in H1650 and Calu-1 cell lines (Fig. 3 A). Subsequent subcellular localization experiments demonstrated the predominant localization of RP11-480I12.5-002 and RP11-480I12.5-004 within the cytoplasm and nucleus of these cells (Fig. 3 B). To investigate the functional role of RP11-480I12.5, recombinant viruses targeting RP11-480I12.5 were used for infection to establish H1650 and Calu-1 cell lines with low expression of RP11-480I12.5 . qPCR analysis showed that the expression levels of RP11-480I12.5 and its transcript 002 were significantly reduced in cells with knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 (Fig. 3 C). Immunofluorescence staining further revealed the expression of IGF2BP3 predominantly within the cytoplasm and nucleus of the aforementioned cell lines (Fig. 3 D). Concurrently, IF-FISH showed that RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 co-localized primarily within the cytoplasm of H1650 and Calu-1 cells, with a minor presence in the nucleus (Fig. 3 E). Cell lines with low expression of IGF2BP3 were established in H1650 and Calu-1 cells, and qPCR and Western blot confirmed a significant decrease in IGF2BP3 expression after knockdown IGF2BP3 (Fig. 3 F, G).

## Knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 or IGF2BP3 results in reduced cell proliferation and increased apoptosis

The CCK-8 assay results indicated that knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 significantly inhibited the viability of NSCLC cells (Fig. 4 A, B). Meanwhile, cell proliferation experiments demonstrated that knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 significantly suppressed the proliferative capacity of cells (Fig. 4 C, D). The flow cytometry results showed that the apoptosis rate of Calu-1 cells was much higher when RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 were knocked down compared to the shNC group. Furthermore, compared to Calu-1 cells with only knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 , the addition of cisplatin significantly increased the cell apoptosis rate (Fig. 4 E, F). Similarly, knockdown of IGF2BP3 in H1650 cells significantly elevated the apoptosis rate. Furthermore, the addition of cisplatin to H1650 cells with knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 significantly enhanced the apoptosis rate (Fig. 4 E, F). These findings suggest that cisplatin enhances the apoptotic sensitivity of cells, thereby reducing their anti-apoptotic capacity.

## Interaction between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC cells

Subsequently, we studied the relationship between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC. The interaction between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 was confirmed in H1650 and Calu-1 cells through RIP and RNA pulldown experiments (Fig. 5 A, B). The knockdown of IGF2BP3 led to a significant decrease in the expression of RP11-480I12.5 in H1650 and Calu-1 cells, as indicated by FISH, immunofluorescence staining and qPCR (Fig. 5 C, D). The results of the Actinomycin D chase assay suggest that the suppression of IGF2BP3 led to a reduction in the stability of RP11-480I12.5 (Fig. 5 E). This indicates that RP11-480I12.5 interacts with IGF2BP3, and IGF2BP3 may maintain the stability of RP11-480I12.5.

In addition, eight downstream genes modulated by IGF2BP3, as documented in existing literature ( CDK4 , COL6A1 , LAMA5 , FN1 , CCND1 , VEGF , MYCN and TP53 ), were included within the scope of this research. ## Discussion

Despite the many advancements that have been made, NSCLC remains a common malignant tumor, with many patients still experiencing recurrence and metastasis. This study revealed a significant overexpression of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 in NSCLC. Knocking down either RP11-480I12.5 or IGF2BP3 in NSCLC cells resulted in the inhibition of cell proliferation and promotion of apoptosis. RIP and RNA pull-down analysis showed that RP11-480I12.5 can interact with IGF2BP3, indicating that there is an interaction between the two. When IGF2BP3 is knock down, the expression of RP11-480I12.5 decreases. Furthermore, the decreased expression of WDHD1 was observed when RP11-480I12.5 or IGF2BP3 was knocked down, resulting in the inhibition of cell proliferation and the enhancement of apoptosis. Current research revealed that the interaction between IGF2BP3 and RP11-480I12.5 enhances the expression of WDHD1 and exerts cell proliferation and anti-apoptotic function in NSCLC (Fig. 8 ). These findings provide a basis for further research towards the development of targeted therapies for NSCLC.


> **Fig. 8:** Interaction between IGF2BP3 and RP11-480I12.5 increases WDHD1 expression and exerts anti-apoptotic functions in NSCLC

Recent research has revealed that numerous lncRNAs play a role in the onset and progression of NSCLC [ 33 , 34 ]. A previously unstudied lncRNA, RP11-480I12.5, was recently discovered in NSCLC. It was observed that elevated levels of RP11-480I12.5 and its transcript 002 in NSCLC cells stimulate cancer cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, suggesting that targeting this lncRNA could be a potential therapy for NSCLC. Currently confirmed, both RP11-480I12.5 and its transcript 004 facilitate the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer cells via distinct pathways, thereby hastening the onset and progression of breast cancer [ 8 , 35 ]. Studies in cervical cancer have shown that RP11-480I12.5/Wnt/β-catenin axis contributes to disease progression [ 9 ].

Recently, considerable scholarly attention has been devoted to investigating the impact of m6A modification on non-coding RNA in the context of cancer research. The modification of lncRNA by m 6 A can have two main effects. It can induce RNA-binding proteins to regulate their function by providing binding sites for m 6 A reader proteins or by affecting the local structure of RNA. In addition, m 6 A modification can also impact the binding of lncRNAs to specific DNA sites by altering the RNA–DNA triple-helix structure [ 36 ]. In this study, the online tool SRAMP was applied to predict potential m 6 A modification sites on the sequences of four different transcripts of the lncRNA, particularly the transcript RP11-480I12.5-002. Analysis with m6A2Target revealed that the m 6 A WERs (writers, erasers, and readers) that bind to RP11-480I12.5 mainly include METTL3 , YTHDF2 , HNRNPG , HNRNPC , and IGF2BP3 . Further analysis using GEPIA2 compared the mRNA levels of these WERs in LUAD and LUSC. Moreover, GEPIA2 analysis was performed on RP11-480I12.5 and its associated genes, which were co-enriched in pathways related to the spliceosome and other relevant pathways. The potential role of IGF2BP3 in mRNA binding and selective splicing in cancer cells, as well as its regulatory function, has been documented previously [ 37 , 38 ]. Herein, IGF2BP3 was selected for further study, as it showed a weak correlation with RP11-480I12.5 and its transcript 002 in LUAD and LUSC.

The IGF2BPs constitute a family of RNA-binding proteins that exert critical influence on pathogenesis through their modulation of mRNA stability and translation, particularly in the context of pivotal cellular processes, such as division and metabolism [ 39 ]. IGF2BP3 exhibits aberrant expression patterns in numerous types of human cancers, often correlating with unfavorable clinical outcomes. In the context of lung cancer, researchers have identified IGF2BP3 as a driver of tumor progression. IGF2BP3, through a mechanism dependent on m 6 A modification, sustains the stability of key anti-ferroptotic factors’ mRNA, including GPX4 , SLC3A2 , ACSL3 , and FTH1 , thereby facilitating lung cancer advancement [ 40 ]. This interaction also promotes NSCLC cell proliferation by interacting with METTL5 [ 41 ]. STRIP2 interacts with IGF2BP3, leading to the m 6 A modification of TMBIM6 mRNA, which increases its stability and promoting tumor growth and metastasis [ 42 ]. IGF2BP3-driven metabolic reprogramming facilitates the development of resistance to EGFR inhibitors in NSCLC [ 43 ]. IGF2BP3 destabilizes the mRNA of EIF4E-BP2 , augmenting the activity of the oncogenic protein eIF4E and promoting cell proliferation [ 44 ]. GEPIA2 analysis revealed that IGF2BP3 is overexpressed in both LUAD and LUSC, and LUAD patients with high IGF2BP3 levels have a lower overall survival probability. These findings indicate that high expression of IGF2BP3 promotes cancer cell proliferation and anti-apoptotic effects, and is linked to poor prognosis in LUAD. All of these findings collectively indicate the potential utility of IGF2BP3 as a prognostic biomarker to guide the selection of treatment strategies.

Recent progress in RNA modifications has identified the critical roles of m 6 A involved in ncRNA metabolism and tumor biological processes [ 45 ]. m 6 A readers could modulate lncRNAs. In pancreatic cancer cells, IGF2BP2 upregulation enhanced its interaction with DANCR, stabilizing DANCR and promoting cancer stem-like properties and pancreatic cancer progression [ 46 ]. In this study, through RIP and pull-down experiments, the interaction between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 was validated. IF-FISH analysis, qPCR and Actinomycin D chase assay demonstrated that IGF2BP3 knockdown decreased the expression and stability of RP11-480I12.5 in cancer cells. These results indicate that IGF2BP3 may enhance RP11-480I12.5 stability through m 6 A modification, increasing its expression. Our investigation into cellular co-localization reveals that RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 predominantly localize in the cytoplasm, suggesting that their interaction likely occurs within this compartment. Further research is needed to investigate this potential relationship.

The screening of downstream target genes of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 using the online tool GEPIA2 identified WDHD1 as being associated with both genes. Knockdown experiments of either RP11-480I12.5 or IGF2BP3 decreased WDHD1 expression levels. RIP and RNA pull-down assays further indicated that these two proteins can bind to each other, suggesting that RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 may synergistically promote WDHD1 expression [ 47 ]. In addition, the Actinomycin D chase assay revealed that the knockdown of RP11-480I12.5 or IGF2BP3 had a significant impact on the stability of WDHD1 mRNA. This finding suggests that RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 may act in concert to enhance the expression of WDHD1 by augmenting the stability of its mRNA. WDHD1 may be intricately interconnected with aspects of tumor heterogeneity, tumor stemness, mismatch repair, and RNA methylation alterations, all of which are critical processes that contribute to the advancement of tumors [ 48 ]. The absence of WDHD1 is vital for proper homologous recombination and repair during the S phase, resulting in heightened DNA damage, slower advancement through the S phase, hindered transition from the S phase to the G2 phase in the cell cycle, and the promotion of cancer cell death [ 49 – 51 ]. This study shows that WDHD1 promotes proliferation and anti-apoptosis in NSCLC cells. Moreover, WDHD1 induces resistance to cisplatin in lung adenocarcinomas, and inhibition of WDHD1 expression significantly enhances apoptosis in NSCLC. Additional clarification indicates that RP11-480I12.5/IGF2BP3/WDHD1 could potentially contribute to drug resistance in LUAD, specifically against cisplatin. Suppressing the expression of WDHD1 has been observed to promote apoptosis in NSCLC cells, thus improving their responsiveness to treatment. Therefore, investigating the involvement of RP11-480I12.5/IGF2BP3/WDHD1 in LUAD will advance understanding of the disease’s pathogenesis and offer a foundation for the development of novel therapeutic interventions.

The research is subject to certain limitations, which warrant consideration. Currently, the study solely relied on public data sets to analyze the expression levels of RP11-480I12.5, IGF2BP3, and WDHD1. To supplement these results, a large number of independent clinical samples are needed to verify the expression of these genes in NSCLC samples. In addition, the functionality of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 was investigated using cell line studies. Therefore, additional validation through animal experiments and clinical research is required to enhance the credibility and applicability of the results. Although this study focuses on the interaction between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3, emphasizing the interaction between biological molecules and their preliminary exploration in NSCLC, the lack of in-depth research on the specific regulatory mechanisms of downstream signaling pathways may limit understanding at the mechanistic level. While our in vitro cell experiment results demonstrate that RP11-480I12.5 knockdown alone significantly reduces WDHD1 expression and impairs cancer cell viability, suggesting its potential as a standalone therapeutic target, the observed reciprocal regulation between RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 indicates that combined targeting may yield superior therapeutic outcomes. This situation mirrors other lncRNA/protein regulatory pairs in oncology, where isolated lncRNA inhibition often shows transient effects due to compensatory mechanisms, while concurrent targeting of both components enhances treatment durability. Subsequent investigations are required to ascertain the potential synergistic anti-tumor effects and improved therapeutic outcomes resulting from the dual knockout of the identified targets. Furthermore, the validity of these findings necessitates verification through pharmacological evaluations utilizing animal models and efficacy assessments in clinical trials.


## Conclusions

This study revealed elevated expression levels of RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 within NSCLC cells. It was further revealed that RP11-480I12.5 interacts with IGF2BP3, resulting in the upregulation of WDHD1 expression, which in turn facilitates the proliferation and anti-apoptotic mechanisms of cancer cells. These findings imply the potential utility of targeting RP11-480I12.5 and IGF2BP3 as therapeutic interventions for NSCLC. Furthermore, it is proposed that considering the regulation of WDHD1 stability and expression by the RP11-480I12.5/IGF2BP3 axis further expands the therapeutic strategies for NSCLC.

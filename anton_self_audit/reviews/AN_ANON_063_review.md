### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This protocol and tutorial manuscript illustrates how to combine two web-based bioinformatics platforms—`[WORKFLOW_PLATFORM]` (Galaxy) for genomic interval manipulation and `EpiGRAPH` for statistical enrichment (Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Bonferroni and FDR adjustments) and machine-learning classification (linear support vector machines evaluated via 10 repetitions of 10-fold cross-validation)—to analyze genomic and epigenomic datasets without local programming. The authors walk through three progressive case studies on human genome build `hg18`: (1) predicting CpG island DNA methylation status on chromosome 21 from DNA sequence $k$-mers, DNA structure, repeats, and gene overlap, followed by testing on a chromosome 21 promoter bisulfite sequencing dataset; (2) identifying the top 100 most SNP-dense putative promoters (defined as 500 bp upstream of RefSeq genes intersected with dbSNP release 126); and (3) contrasting "highly polymorphic" promoters ($\ge 10$ SNPs in 1 kb upstream) against "non-polymorphic" promoters ($\le 1$ SNP in 1 kb upstream) within ENCODE regions across 10 default `EpiGRAPH` attribute groups. Pre-configured XML files and step-by-step video screencasts accompany the tutorial.

As a pedagogical walkthrough of web-based genomic interval operations and feature enrichment, the manuscript is clearly structured and accessible to bench biologists. However, the biological and statistical formulation of the three example case studies exhibits several methodological flaws that set a poor precedent for tutorial readers: (1) in Case Study 1 (Section 3.2), the "independent test set of unseen genomic regions" is drawn from the exact same chromosome (chromosome 21 promoter regions) as the chromosome 21 CpG island training set, creating spatial and locus overlap between training and test instances; (2) in Case Studies 2 and 3 (Sections 3.3–3.4), defining promoters by taking 500 bp or 1,000 bp upstream of "each gene's start" without strand-aware TSS disambiguation or isoform deduplication treats `txStart` as the 5' end on both strands (unless handled by a specific tool option not mentioned in the text) and counts overlapping RefSeq isoforms multiple times; (3) equating high raw dbSNP 126 density in promoters with "adaptive evolution or relaxation of selective constraint" ignores CpG hypermutability ($\text{CpG} \to \text{TpG}$), local mutation rate variation, and collapsed segmental duplications in dbSNP 126; and (4) in Case Study 3 (Section 3.4), evaluating `EpiGRAPH` on positive/negative classes defined by dbSNP counts while keeping Attribute Group 6 ("population variation", which contains dbSNP features) in the feature matrix constitutes direct target leakage ($96\%$ accuracy, $r = 0.92$). These issues can be corrected by revising the tutorial workflows and adding explicit statistical caveats in the text and Notes.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **`[WORKFLOW_PLATFORM]` and `EpiGRAPH` enable web-based genomic interval joins, Wilcoxon rank-sum feature screening, and cross-validated SVM classification via shareable histories and XML configurations: [Well Supported].**  
  The operational mechanics of importing UCSC Table Browser tracks, performing interval coverage/joins, exporting tab-delimited coordinate files, and running standardized non-parametric tests and $10 \times 10$-fold cross-validated SVMs via `EpiGRAPH` XML templates are accurately documented and reproducible.

* **A linear SVM trained on chromosome 21 CpG islands accurately predicts DNA methylation status ($78\%$ accuracy, $r = 0.50$ in CV) and generalizes to an "independent test set of unseen genomic regions" on chromosome 21: [Fragile].**  
  While the cross-validation accuracy ($78\%$) and enrichment of `CG` vs. `CA` dinucleotides replicate known sequence correlates of CpG island methylation, the external validation in lines 106–108 tests the chromosome 21 CpG island classifier on the top 25% most methylated and top 25% most unmethylated *promoter regions on chromosome 21*. Because roughly 60–70% of human promoters overlap CpG islands, many promoter regions on chromosome 21 physically overlap the exact chromosome 21 CpG islands used to train the SVM in Step 1. Consequently, this test set is not spatially independent of the training data, and filtering to extreme quartiles (top 25% vs. bottom 25%, discarding the middle 50%) artificially inflates classification accuracy.

* **Selecting the top 100 RefSeq promoters (500 bp upstream) with the highest number of dbSNP 126 entries identifies promoters undergoing "adaptive evolution or relaxation of selective constraint": [Overstated].**  
  In Section 3.3 (lines 115, 129–133), raw SNP count in dbSNP build 126 within a 500-bp window is interpreted as a signature of adaptive evolution or relaxed constraint. In reality, raw dbSNP 126 counts in the human genome are heavily driven by: (a) mismapped paralogous sequence variants in segmental duplications and copy-number variable regions; (b) differential ascertainment depth across loci in early dbSNP builds; (c) neutral mutation rate heterogeneity (particularly methylated $\text{CpG} \to \text{TpG}$ transitions); and (d) neutral genealogical coalescent variance. Inferring adaptive evolution or relaxed purifying selection requires normalizing polymorphism against interspecies divergence (e.g., human–chimpanzee divergence in a McDonald–Kreitman or Hudson–Kreitman–Aguadé framework) and masking segmental duplications.

* **`EpiGRAPH` machine learning analysis of polymorphic ($\ge 10$ SNPs) vs. non-polymorphic ($\le 1$ SNP) promoters achieves $96\%$ accuracy ($r = 0.92$) on the "population variation" attribute group and identifies enrichment of CpG islands and conserved TFBSs in non-polymorphic promoters: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 3.4 (lines 144–149), the binary class label is explicitly constructed in Galaxy by thresholding on dbSNP counts ($\ge 10$ SNPs vs. $\le 1$ SNP in 1 kb upstream). Feeding those regions into `EpiGRAPH` with Attribute Group 6 ("population variation", which includes dbSNP density) included as a predictor is textbook target leakage (predicting a thresholded variable from the variable itself). Furthermore, comparing unadjusted $\ge 10$ SNP vs. $\le 1$ SNP promoters without matching for GC/CpG content (despite Note 2 discussing GC matching) confounds regulatory conservation with baseline mutation rate: unmethylated germline CpG island promoters are protected from $\text{CpG} \to \text{TpG}$ deamination and therefore exhibit lower SNP density than methylated CpG-containing promoters even under identical selective constraint.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Target Leakage and Match for GC Content in Case Study 3 (Section 3.4)
* **WHY:** Defining positive and negative promoter classes by dbSNP count ($\ge 10$ vs. $\le 1$ SNP) and then evaluating predictive accuracy on `EpiGRAPH` Attribute Group 6 ("population variation", which contains dbSNP features) is circular target leakage ($96\%$ accuracy, $r = 0.92$). Moreover, failing to match the two promoter classes for GC content and repeat overlap confounds the downstream comparisons of regulatory elements and recombination hotspots.
* **WHAT:** Exclude Attribute Group 6 ("population variation") from the feature matrix when predicting SNP-density classes, and apply `EpiGRAPH`'s built-in control matching (described in Note 2) to match high-SNP and low-SNP promoters for GC content and repeat overlap.
* **HOW:** In Section 3.4 (line 146), uncheck Attribute Group 6 ("population variation") prior to launching the machine learning run (or explicitly label Group 6 in line 148 as a trivial positive-control leakage check that must be excluded from combined multi-group classifiers), and demonstrate how the results change when GC-content matching is enabled on Step 2 of the `EpiGRAPH` submission workflow.

#### 2. Prevent Chromosomal Training–Test Overlap in Case Study 1 (Section 3.2)
* **WHY:** Training a methylation classifier on chromosome 21 CpG islands and testing it on chromosome 21 promoters (lines 106–108) violates training–test independence because promoter CpG islands on chromosome 21 appear in both sets.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the chromosome 21 CpG island classifier on promoter regions from a held-out chromosome (e.g., chromosome 22 or ENCODE regions on other autosomes), or remove any chromosome 21 promoter interval that overlaps (`bedtools intersect` / Galaxy `Intersect`) a training CpG island before computing external test performance.
* **HOW:** Using the Galaxy `Subtract` or `Intersect` tool introduced in Section 3.3, filter out all chromosome 21 promoter intervals in the bisulfite test table that overlap the chromosome 21 training CpG island coordinates by $\ge 1$ bp, and report the truly out-of-sample test accuracy on the non-overlapping regions.

#### 3. Ensure Strand-Aware TSS Promoter Extraction and RefSeq Isoform Deduplication in Sections 3.3–3.4
* **WHY:** Lines 119 and 131 instruct users to "transform coordinates of genes into coordinates of potential promoter regions by taking 500 base pairs immediate upstream on each gene's start." In UCSC `refGene` tables, `txStart` is the 5' transcription start site only for `+` strand genes; for `-` strand genes, the 5' TSS is `txEnd`, and "upstream" is toward higher genomic coordinates. Taking `[txStart - 500, txStart]` on both strands extracts the 3' downstream region for all `-` strand genes. Additionally, multiple RefSeq transcripts (`NM_*`) for the same gene often share the exact same TSS, creating duplicate promoter intervals that bias the top-100 SNP-dense list and inflate statistical significance in `EpiGRAPH`.
* **WHAT:** Specify the exact strand-aware flanking tool (`Get flanks` with `Upstream` and strand awareness enabled) and deduplicate overlapping/identical promoter intervals (`Merge` or `Cluster` in Galaxy) before counting SNPs.
* **HOW:** Update Sections 3.3 and 3.4 to explicitly state: (1) use Galaxy's strand-aware `Get flanks` tool (500 bp or 1,000 bp upstream of the 5' end respecting the `strand` column), and (2) apply `Merge` on overlapping promoter intervals so each unique genomic locus is represented once prior to intersecting with dbSNP.

#### 4. Temper Evolutionary Claims About Raw dbSNP Density in Promoters
* **WHY:** High SNP density in dbSNP 126 within a 500-bp region is frequently an artifact of collapsed segmental duplications, CpG deamination hotspots, or variable sequencing depth rather than positive/adaptive selection or relaxed purifying constraint.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Revise lines 115 and 129 to frame the Section 3.3 workflow as a descriptive screen for "highly polymorphic promoters" rather than direct evidence of adaptive evolution, and add a brief caveat in Section 4 (Notes) explaining that distinguishing adaptive evolution or relaxed constraint from neutral mutation rate variation and paralogous mapping artifacts requires filtering segmental duplications and comparing polymorphism to interspecies divergence (`phyloP`/`phastCons` or HKA test).

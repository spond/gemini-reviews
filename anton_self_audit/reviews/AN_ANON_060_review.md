### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the prevalence, somatic divergence, and intergenerational transmission of human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) heteroplasmy across 39 healthy mother–child pairs of European ancestry (156 total samples from blood and buccal swabs). Using long-range PCR enrichment (two overlapping ~9-kb amplicons) followed by deep Illumina MiSeq sequencing (~19,789× mean depth), spike-in cross-contamination controls (pUC18 and PhiX174), $\rho^0$ cell controls for nuclear mitochondrial DNA segments (numts), and orthogonal validation by Sanger sequencing (MAF $\ge 10\%$) and droplet digital PCR (ddPCR; MAF $1\text{--}10\%$), the authors identify 172 validated point heteroplasmies across 98 mother–child quartets. From these data, they report an average of ~1 heteroplasmy ($\text{MAF} \ge 1\%$) per individual, carrier status for disease-associated mtDNA variants in 1 of 8 individuals, a positive association between maternal age at fertilization and child heteroplasmy count ($P \le 0.010$), an effective germline mtDNA bottleneck size of $N \approx 30\text{--}35$ (IQR $9\text{--}141$), and a germline mutation rate of $1.3 \times 10^{-8}$ mutations per site per year. Raw sequencing reads are deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (`SRP047378`).

The experimental design—pairing two somatic tissues per individual with high sequencing depth, physical contamination spike-ins, and re-amplified ddPCR validation that directly quantifies PCR-induced false positives ($2/18 = 11\%$ in the $1\text{--}10\%$ MAF range)—provides a well-controlled empirical dataset. However, the quantitative population-genetic inferences have notable statistical limitations: (1) estimating the bottleneck size $N = p(1-p)/\sigma^2_{\text{gen}}$ per site from a single mother–child transmission ($n=1$ squared difference) and taking the median across 51 sites introduces systematic transformation bias because the median of a single squared normal deviate is $\approx 0.455\sigma^2$ rather than $\sigma^2$, and subtracting $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ from individual squared differences can yield zero or negative variance denominators; (2) the maternal age at fertilization effect is confounded by maternal age at collection ($R^2 = 43\%$) and conflates inherited maternal heteroplasmies with de novo germline mutations; and (3) a factual misstatement in the Results reports that 96 of 98 heteroplasmies were transversions (rather than transitions). These issues can be resolved through likelihood-based beta-binomial/drift modeling, covariate-adjusted regression, and textual correction without generating new data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Prevalence of point heteroplasmy ($\sim 1$ per individual at $\text{MAF} \ge 1\%$) and purifying selection against nonsynonymous variants: [Well Supported].**  
  The experimental pipeline controls for numts via 9-kb long-range amplicons validated on $\rho^0$ cells, monitors cross-sample index/lane bleed via pUC18/PhiX174 spike-ins, and establishes empirical false-positive rates using re-amplified templates via Sanger ($0/44$ for $\text{MAF} \ge 10\%$) and ddPCR ($2/18$ for $\text{MAF } 1\text{--}10\%$). The enrichment of D-loop variants (34 observed vs. 6.6 random expected, Table 1), depletion of nonsynonymous coding variants ($d_N/d_S < 1$, $P = 5 \times 10^{-3}$), and lower transmission rate of nonsynonymous heteroplasmies ($12\%$ transmitted vs. $36\%$ untransmitted, $P = 0.025$) are internally consistent across multiple statistical tests.

* **Somatic divergence vs. intergenerational bottleneck drift across tissues ($R^2 = 92\%$ between child tissues vs. $49\%$ between maternal tissues vs. $13\text{--}29\%$ between generations): [Well Supported].**  
  Measuring both blood (mesodermal) and buccal (ectodermal) tissues in every mother and child directly demonstrates that mitotic segregation since embryonic germ-layer divergence produces substantially smaller allele frequency shifts than the intergenerational germline bottleneck, and that somatic divergence increases with age (mothers vs. children).

* **Effective germline mtDNA bottleneck size is $\sim 30\text{--}35$ segregating units: [Fragile].**  
  The authors compute a point estimate of $N_i = \hat{p}_i(1-\hat{p}_i) / [(p_{m,i} - p_{c,i})^2 - 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}]$ separately for each of the 51 transmitted quartets and report the sample median ($32.3$, IQR $10.5\text{--}103.3$). Statistically, for a single transmission ($n=1$), $(p_{m,i} - p_{c,i})^2 / [p_i(1-p_i)]$ is a single draw from a $\chi^2_1 / N$ distribution. Because $\text{median}(\chi^2_1) \approx 0.455$ whereas $\mathbb{E}[\chi^2_1] = 1$, taking the median of the reciprocal $1 / \chi^2_1$ overestimates $N$ by more than twofold under pure drift. In addition, subtracting $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ (Wait: when averaging two tissues in mother and two in child, $\bar{p}_m = (p_{m,1}+p_{m,2})/2$ and $\bar{p}_c = (p_{c,1}+p_{c,2})/2$, the measurement error variance of $\bar{p}_m - \bar{p}_c$ is $\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}/2 + \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}/2 = \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$, not $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$!) mis-scales the variance correction by a factor of 4 and produces undefined/negative $N_i$ whenever $(p_{m,i} - p_{c,i})^2 \le 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$. Similarly, estimating $N = 1/0.038 = 26.3$ from a single de novo mutation (site 4191) ignores conditioning on the variant surviving genetic drift and exceeding the $1\%$ detection threshold.

* **Positive association between child heteroplasmy count and maternal age at fertilization ($P = 0.010$) reflects oocyte aging: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Poisson regression shows a statistically significant positive slope between maternal age at conception and total child heteroplasmies. However, total child heteroplasmies include variants already present in the mother ("All" and "SL" categories, 47/98 quartets) as well as child somatic gains ("SG"), rather than solely de novo germline mutations ("Child" category, 16/98 quartets). Because maternal age at fertilization correlates strongly with maternal age at collection ($R^2 = 43\%$, $P = 0.002$), and older mothers harbor more heteroplasmies, the analysis does not fully disentangle increased de novo mutagenesis in aging oocytes from transmission of pre-existing maternal variants or time since birth (child age).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Single-Site Reciprocal Moment Inversion with a Joint Likelihood or Method-of-Moments Estimator for Bottleneck Size $N$
* **WHY:** For a single mother–child pair at site $i$, $\sigma^2_{\text{raw},i} = (\bar{p}_{m,i} - \bar{p}_{c,i})^2$ is a 1-degree-of-freedom squared residual whose median is $0.455\,\sigma^2_{\text{gen}}$, biasing the median of $p_i(1-p_i)/\sigma^2_{\text{gen},i}$ upward by $\sim 2.2\times$. Furthermore, because $\bar{p}_{m,i}$ and $\bar{p}_{c,i}$ are averages of two tissues, $\text{Var}(\bar{p}_{m,i} - \bar{p}_{c,i} \mid \text{true frequencies}) = \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_{\text{measure}} + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_{\text{measure}} + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_{\text{measure}} + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_{\text{measure}} = \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$, whereas the manuscript subtracts $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$.
* **WHAT:** Correct the measurement-error variance coefficient from $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ to $\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ (for two-tissue averages) or $2\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ (for single-tissue comparisons), and estimate $N$ jointly across all 51 sites via maximum likelihood (e.g., Kimura–Ohta diffusion / beta-binomial model) or by pooling normalized variance across sites before inverting: $\hat{N} = K / \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{(\bar{p}_{m,i} - \bar{p}_{c,i})^2 - \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}}{\bar{p}_{m,i}(1-\bar{p}_{m,i})}$.
* **HOW:** Re-run the bottleneck calculation on Dataset S1, Table S3 using both the pooled standardized variance estimator and a beta-binomial likelihood with nonparametric bootstrap confidence intervals across the 39 families, and propagate the updated $\hat{N}$ into the germline mutation rate formula $\mu = \mu_0 / [2 N \ln(1/\theta - 1)]$.

#### 2. Isolate De Novo Child Germline Mutations and Adjust for Confounders in the Maternal Age Regression
* **WHY:** Testing total child heteroplasmies against maternal age at fertilization conflates newly arising oocyte mutations ("Child" category, $n=16$) with inherited maternal variants ("All" and "SL" categories, $n=47$) and post-zygotic somatic mutations ("SG").
* **WHAT:** Fit separate generalized linear models (Poisson and negative binomial to check for family-level overdispersion) for (a) child de novo germline heteroplasmies ("Child" category only) and (b) transmitted heteroplasmies, adjusting for child age at sampling and maternal baseline heteroplasmy count.
* **HOW:** Using the quartet classifications in Table 3 / Table S3, regress the count of "Child"-category heteroplasmies per family on maternal age at fertilization with child age as a covariate, and report the partial regression effect size and $p$-value alongside Figure 2.

#### 3. Account for Asymmetric Two-Tier MAF Thresholding ($\ge 1\%$ vs. $\ge 0.2\%$) in Quartet Classification
* **WHY:** Conditioning on $\text{MAF} \ge 1\%$ in one sample to lower the calling threshold to $\ge 0.2\%$ in the remaining three samples of a family (Line 151) induces boundary classification artifacts: a quartet with MAFs $(0.011, 0.003, 0.000, 0.000)$ is classified as "Mother" (germline present in both maternal tissues), whereas $(0.009, 0.003, 0.000, 0.000)$ is undetected, and $(0.011, 0.0015, 0.000, 0.000)$ is classified as "Somatic Gain" despite $0.15\%$ vs. $0.20\%$ being indistinguishable within sequencing noise.
* **WHAT:** Perform a sensitivity analysis using a uniform calling threshold across all four samples of a quartet (e.g., $\ge 1.0\%$ in both tissues, or likelihood-based joint posterior probability of presence in each tissue) to verify that the counts in Table 3 and the bottleneck subset ($n=51$) are robust to the $0.2\%$ secondary cutoff.
* **HOW:** Re-tabulate Table 3 and re-run the nonsynonymous transmission Fisher's exact test under a strict $\ge 0.5\%$ and $\ge 1.0\%$ concordant threshold.

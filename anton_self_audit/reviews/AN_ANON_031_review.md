### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a quality-control and variant-inspection suite for Duplex Sequencing (DS) comprising four tools: Family Size Distribution (FSD), Tag Distance (TD), Chimera Analysis (CA), and Variant Analyzer (VAR-A). Using four published *TP53* DS libraries (PF1-CRISPR, PF1-Standard, PF2-CRISPR, and PF2-Standard; BioProject `PRJNA412416`) and a newly generated 4-variant spike-in dilution series ($10^{-1}$ to $10^{-4}$; BioProject `PRJNA684907`), the authors show how TD and FSD diagnose PCR/sequencing errors within barcodes and sub-optimal amplification profiles (e.g., over-amplification of PF1-Standard where 83% of DCS families exceed 20 reads), and introduce VAR-A to re-examine raw paired-end (PE) read alignments, mate-pair overlap, positional end-polishing biases, and phasing via an 11-tier classification system (Tiers 1.1–5). By lowering the minimum single-strand consensus sequence (SSCS) family size threshold from $FS \ge 3$ to $FS \ge 1$ and filtering through VAR-A tiers, the workflow increases on-target duplex consensus sequence (DCS) coverage by 10.9% in PF2-CRISPR (20,217 to 22,426 DCS) and 24.5% in PF2-Standard (101,095 to 125,826 DCS) while reproducing expected allele frequencies ($R^2 = 0.96$ in the dilution series). All Python tools, workflows, and raw sequencing datasets are publicly available under an open-source AFL 2.0 license.

This is a practical, well-engineered bioinformatic toolkit that addresses a genuine bottleneck in duplex sequencing—diagnosing library preparation inefficiencies and recovering usable read depth from small families ($FS = 1\text{–}2$) in low-input samples. Its primary limitations are: (1) a mathematical flaw in the Chimera Analysis (CA) interpretation, which equates any sharing of a single 10-bp half-tag ($\text{TD}a_{\min} = 0$ or $\text{TD}b_{\min} = 0$, yielding $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$) with PCR chimeras or dangerous barcode collisions, overlooking the fact that in a pool of $1.4 \times 10^5$ to $1.3 \times 10^6$ molecules labeled with $10+10$ bp bipartite tags, single-end 10-mer birthday-paradox repeats ($4^{10} \approx 1.05 \times 10^6$ combinations per end) occur at rates of 23–93% by pure chance without causing full 20-bp duplex tag collisions; (2) the absence of a formal false-positive rate (FPR per sequenced base pair) benchmark comparing $FS \ge 1$ (Tiers 1.2–3.2) against standard $FS \ge 3$ (Tier 1.1) across known wild-type bases; and (3) minor inconsistencies between the text and tables (e.g., Table 2 omits $\text{TD} > 7$ counts, and the text mislabels the Tier of `chr17:7669456-C-A`). These issues can be fully resolved through analytical birthday-collision modeling and empirical background error tabulation on the existing datasets.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **VAR-A tier classification enables lowering the minimum family size from $FS \ge 3$ to $FS \ge 1$, increasing DCS coverage by 10–25% while preserving variant allele frequencies and flagging end-polishing/homopolymer artifacts (Tiers 4.1–5): [Well Supported].**  
  Tables 4 and 5 (and Supplementary Tables S5–S11) demonstrate that relaxing $FS \ge 3$ to $FS \ge 1$ rescues 2,238 DCS in PF2-CRISPR and 25,569 DCS in PF2-Standard, accurately reproduces germline heterozygous (~49.3%) and low-frequency (`chr17:7674230-C-T` at 1.00–1.24%) variant calls compared to the University of Washington pipeline, and successfully flags 5'/3' end-repair artifacts (Tier 4.1) and poly-T homopolymer noise (Tier 5).

* **44–51% of families in PF2-CRISPR/PF2-Standard/PF1-CRISPR and 97% of families (29% of DCS) in PF1-Standard are "chimeras" or problematic barcode collisions ($\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Equations (2)–(3), a tag of length $10+10$ bp is split into upstream barcode $a$ ($4^{10} = 1,048,576$ possible 10-mers) and downstream barcode $b$ ($4^{10} = 1,048,576$ possible 10-mers), and $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ whenever *either* barcode $a$ or barcode $b$ matches any other tag's $a$ or $b$ in the entire dataset ($\text{TD}a_{\min} = 0$ or $\text{TD}b_{\min} = 0$). However, in a duplex library with $N$ distinct molecules, a true barcode collision requires *both* ends ($a$ and $b$, $4^{20} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{12}$ combinations) to collide on two molecules covering the same genomic locus, whereas the probability that at least one other molecule in the library happens to receive the same 10-bp half-barcode at end $a$ or end $b$ independently by random ligation is:
  $$P(\text{TD}a_{\min}=0 \text{ or } \text{TD}b_{\min}=0) = 1 - \left(1 - 4^{-10}\right)^{2(N-1)} \approx 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{2N}{4^{10}}\right)$$
  For $N = 138,631$ families (PF2-CRISPR), random independent ligation alone yields $1 - \exp(-277,262 / 1,048,576) = 23.2\%$ (and ~40–50% under typical random-decamer nucleotide synthesis bias), and for $N = 1,341,763$ families (PF1-Standard), random independent ligation yields $1 - \exp(-2,683,526 / 1,048,576) = 92.3\%$! Thus, $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ across the whole tag pool predominantly measures expected single-end 10-mer birthday coincidences across unrelated genomic fragments rather than PCR recombination chimeras or full 20-bp duplex collisions. Conversely, when VAR-A restricts chimera detection to families *sharing the same variant at the same genomic locus* (Results, line 103), only 5/7,643 (0.065%) and 37/6,834 (0.54%) calls are chimeric—confirming that true within-locus chimeras/collisions are two orders of magnitude rarer than the global CA metric implies.

* **TD distribution histograms distinguish sequencing/PCR tag errors ($\text{TD} = 1$ in SSCS, eliminated in DCS) from true barcode proximity and guide barcode error-correction thresholds: [Well Supported].**  
  Comparing the bimodal TD distribution in raw SSCS tags (30–48% at $\text{TD}=1$) against error-corrected tags and DCS tags (where $\text{TD}=1$ drops to 1–3%, Figure 2A–D) clearly demonstrates that the $\text{TD}=1$ peak is driven by single-base PCR/sequencing errors in the 20-bp tag, and the in silico tag-shortening experiment ($10+10 \to 8+8 \to 6+6$ nt, Figure 3) effectively illustrates the loss of separability between tag errors and independent molecules as tag length decreases.

* **FSD reveals that differences in DCS yield between Standard-DS and CRISPR-DS are driven by DNA input and over-amplification ($FS > 20$) rather than intrinsic protocol limitations: [Well Supported].**  
  Table 3 and Figure 5 show that while PF1-Standard (10 $\mu$g DNA, 35.7M reads, mean $FS = 27$) places 83% of DCS into over-amplified families ($FS > 20$), PF2-Standard (3 $\mu$g DNA, mean $FS = 11$) achieves 64% of DCS in the optimal $FS = 3\text{–}20$ range, comparable to PF1-CRISPR (62%) and PF2-CRISPR (56%).

* **Ultra-low-frequency variants (`chr17:7669456-C-A` and `chr17:7675381-T-C`, VAF $\sim 10^{-4}$) rescued only under $FS \ge 1$ represent true borderline variants demonstrating VAR-A's sensitivity: [Fragile].**  
  Because neither variant was validated orthogonally (and `chr17:7675381-T-C` sits adjacent to a noisy poly-T homopolymer where multiple Tier 4.1 and Tier 5 artifacts occur), a single duplex molecule supported by $FS = 1\text{–}2$ on one strand or incomplete consensus (2/3 reads on one strand) cannot be distinguished from an early-cycle PCR error or strand-complement lesion without a calibrated false-positive rate per base pair for Tiers 2.1–3.2.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Separate Random Single-End Half-Tag Birthday Coincidences from True PCR Chimeras in the CA Tool
* **WHY:** Comparing 10-bp half-tags ($a$ or $b$) across all $N \sim 10^5\text{–}10^6$ tags in a whole-genome or multi-target library guarantees high rates of $\text{TD}a_{\min} = 0$ or $\text{TD}b_{\min} = 0$ by the birthday paradox ($4^{10} \approx 10^6$), even when zero PCR chimeras exist and full 20-bp tags ($4^{20} \approx 10^{12}$) have near-zero collision probability.
* **WHAT:** Restrict the CA comparison to tags that map to the *same genomic start/end coordinates* (or the same CRISPR target fragment), and overlay the analytical null expectation $P_{\text{null}}(\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1) = 1 - (1 - 4^{-L})^{2(N-1)}$ on the global CA plots.
* **HOW:** In the CA Python script, add an optional BAM/coordinate grouping step (or compute the theoretical birthday-paradox collision rate for a half-barcode of length $L = 10$ given total unique tags $N$) so users can see what fraction of $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ exceeds the random single-end coincidence baseline. Guard Equation (3) against division by zero when $\text{TD}a + \text{TD}b = 0$.

#### 2. Quantify the Empirical False-Positive Rate (Errors per Sequenced Base Pair) by VAR-A Tier
* **WHY:** Lowering the threshold from $FS \ge 3$ (Tier 1.1) to $FS \ge 1$ (Tiers 1.2–2.4) or $>50\%$ consensus (Tiers 3.1–3.2) increases sensitivity and depth by 10–25%, but users need to know the exact specificity trade-off (background error rate per duplex base pair) for each tier.
* **WHAT:** Compute and report the cumulative background mutation frequency (non-germline, non-spike-in variant calls per total on-target DCS base pairs sequenced) as a function of cumulative VAR-A tiers (Tier 1.1 alone vs. Tier $\le 1.2$, Tier $\le 2.4$, Tier $\le 3.2$, and unfiltered).
* **HOW:** Using the spike-in dilution series (`PRJNA684907`) and the four *TP53* libraries (`PRJNA412416`), mask known SNPs and engineered spike-in sites, divide the count of remaining variant calls in each tier by the total sequenced duplex nucleotides (`On-target DCS × target length`), and add a summary column/table showing the empirical error rate per bp for each tier.

#### 3. Provide Full Per-Dilution Precision/Recall Metrics for the Spike-In Validation Experiment
* **WHY:** The orthogonal spike-in validation (`PRJNA684907`, four variants diluted $1:10$ to $1:10,000$) is currently summarized only by a single correlation value ($R^2 = 0.96$) and a reference to an unpublished manuscript (`[AUTHOR et al.], in preparation`).
* **WHAT:** Include a concise table in the main text (or expand Supplementary Table S12 description) comparing expected VAF, observed VAF under $FS \ge 3$ (Tier 1.1), and observed VAF and rescued alternate-allele DCS counts under $FS \ge 1$ (Tiers 1.1–2.4) across all four dilution steps ($10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}$).
* **HOW:** Tabulate the exact DCS depth and alternate allele counts for `c.742C>T`, `c.746C>G`, `c.749C>G`, and `c.1620C>A` under both $FS \ge 3$ and $FS \ge 1 + \text{VAR-A}$ to quantify how many true ultra-rare molecules are rescued by Tiers 1.2–2.4.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several minor discrepancies exist between equations, tables, and the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Equation (1) (Materials and Methods, line 30):** The summation index runs from $k = 0$ to $n$ ($\sum_{k=0}^n (X_{ik} \ne X_{jk})$), which sums over $n+1$ positions when $n$ is defined as "the total number of sites". Change the limits to $\sum_{k=1}^n$.
  * **Equation (3) and Text (line 94):** In line 94, the inline formula is written without parentheses as `relative delta TD = TDa min - TDb max /TDa min + TDb max`. Add parentheses `|TDa min - TDb max| / (TDa min + TDb max)` to match Equation (3).
  * **Table 2 vs. Figure 2A (lines 68, 80–85):** For `tag length 20 (10 + 10)`, Table 2 reports `TD = 1: 45%`, `TD = 2–3: 6.9%`, and `TD = 4–7: 48.1%`, which sum to $100.0\%$, implying $0\%$ of tags have $\text{TD} \ge 8$. However, Figure 2A clearly shows non-zero bars at $\text{TD} = 8, 9, 10, 11$. Rename the third column of Table 2 to `TD ≥ 4` (or `TD = 4–11`) so it matches the percentages.
  * **Results (chunk 2, line 1) vs. Table 5 (line 150):** The main text states that `chr17:7669456-C-A` in PF2-CRISPR is `"classified as tier 3.1"`, whereas Table 5 lists `0` under column `3.1` and `1` under column `3.2`. Since this variant occurred only once ($mut = 1$, non-recurring), it belongs to Tier `3.2` per Table 1 ("minimal FS ≥ 1 for both SSCS in at least one mate" with $>50\%$ alt base), not Tier `3.1` ("recurring mutation on this position"). Correct `"tier 3.1"` to `"tier 3.2"` in the text.
  * **Results (line 74):** Fix the double negative `"errors cannot not be distinguished anymore"` to `"errors cannot be distinguished anymore"`.

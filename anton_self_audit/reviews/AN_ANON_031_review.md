### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces a suite of four quality-control and post-processing bioinformatics tools—Tag Distance (TD), Chimera Analysis (CA), Family Size Distribution (FSD), and Variant Analyzer (VAR-A)—designed to diagnose data loss, barcode collisions, PCR chimeras, and variant-calling reliability in Duplex Sequencing (DS) datasets. The authors evaluate these tools across four previously published *TP53* DS libraries comparing standard random fragmentation (`PF1-Standard`, `PF2-Standard`, using $10\,\mu\text{g}$ and $3\,\mu\text{g}$ genomic DNA input, respectively) against targeted CRISPR/Cas9 fragmentation (`PF1-CRISPR`, `PF2-CRISPR`, $100\,\text{ng}$ input), alongside an orthogonal dilution series of four spiked-in variants (`c.742C>T`, `c.746C>G`, `c.749C>G`, and `c.1620C>A`) spanning $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-4}$ allele frequencies. By relaxing the single-strand consensus sequence (SSCS) minimum family size threshold from $\text{FS} \ge 3$ to $\text{FS} \ge 1$ and categorizing called variants into a rule-based tier system (Tiers 1.1 through 5), the authors report a $10\%$ to $25\%$ increase in duplex consensus sequence (DCS) coverage (`20,218` to `22,456` DCS in `PF2-CRISPR`; `101,145` to `126,714` DCS in `PF2-Standard`) and $R^2 = 0.96$ linearity in the dilution series. Raw sequencing reads (`PRJNA412416` and `PRJNA684907`) and open-source Python software (AFL 2.0 license) are publicly available.

The diagnostic toolset addresses a genuine practical bottleneck in duplex sequencing: substantial read loss caused by strict $\text{FS} \ge 3$ filtering, tag errors, and library preparation artifacts such as end-polishing and barcode collisions. However, several statistical and algorithmic limitations temper the manuscript's broader conclusions. First, the Chimera Analysis (CA) statistic ($\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$) mathematically conflates true PCR template-switching chimeras with independent barcode collisions at one adapter end, and its asymmetric min/max formulation across a $1,000$-tag random subsample is sensitive to library size. Second, the headline claim that lowering the family size threshold to $\text{FS} \ge 1$ rescues ultra-low-frequency variants without compromising specificity is not supported by a formal receiver operating characteristic (ROC) or false-positive rate (FPR) evaluation across the tier hierarchy; the two newly discovered $\sim 10^{-4}$ variants in `PF2-CRISPR` and `PF2-Standard` fall into lower-confidence tiers (`Tier 3.2`/`3.1` or adjacent to a homopolymer) and lack orthogonal validation. These issues can be resolved through mathematical refinement of the chimera vs. collision estimator, formal tier-specific FPR benchmarking on the spiked-in control libraries, and editorial cleanup of table/text discrepancies.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Tag Distance (TD) and Family Size Distribution (FSD) diagnostics identify sequencing/PCR tag errors and library over-sequencing (`PF1-Standard` vs. `PF2-Standard`): [Well Supported].**  
  Stratifying minimum Hamming distances across a $1,000$-tag subsample and the full DCS tag sets demonstrates that $30\text{--}48\%$ of raw SSCS tags differ by $\text{TD} = 1$, whereas error-corrected DCS tags exhibit only $1\text{--}3\%$ $\text{TD} = 1$ (Figure 2, Table 2). Furthermore, FSD analysis (Table 3, Figure 5) shows that the poor DCS yield of `PF1-Standard` ($11\%$ of families, $24\%$ of PE reads) stems from over-amplification ($\text{mean FS} = 27$, with $83\%$ of DCS having $\text{FS} > 20$), whereas `PF2-Standard` ($3\,\mu\text{g}$ input, $\text{mean FS} = 11$, $64\%$ of DCS in the optimal $\text{FS } 3\text{--}20$ range) performs comparably to the CRISPR-targeted libraries ($56\text{--}62\%$ in $\text{FS } 3\text{--}20$).

* **The Chimera Analysis (CA) metric ($\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$) quantifies chimeric read formation across libraries: [Overstated].**  
  Equations 2 and 3 define $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = \max\left(\frac{|\text{TD}_{a,\min} - \text{TD}_{b,\max}|}{\text{TD}_{a,\min} + \text{TD}_{b,\max}}, \frac{|\text{TD}_{a,\max} - \text{TD}_{b,\min}|}{\text{TD}_{a,\max} + \text{TD}_{b,\min}}\right)$, which equals $1$ whenever a sampled tag shares either its upstream ($a$) or downstream ($b$) $10\text{-nt}$ barcode identically ($\text{TD}_{a,\min} = 0$ or $\text{TD}_{b,\min} = 0$) with at least one other family while differing at the opposite end. While the text acknowledges that barcode collisions yield the exact same value ($\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$), the Results section repeatedly labels all such tags ($44\text{--}51\%$ in `PF2-CRISPR`/`PF2-Standard` and $97\%$ in `PF1-Standard`) as "chimeric families" or "chimeric reads" (Figure 4). Under a birthday-problem model with $4^{10} = 1,048,576$ possible $10\text{-nt}$ single-end barcodes and $N = 138,631$ to $1,341,763$ total families (Table 3), random single-end barcode sharing (where two independent molecules happen to receive the same $a$ adapter ligated to different $b$ adapters) occurs with probability $1 - (1 - 4^{-10})^{N-1} \approx 12.4\%$ per tag at $N = 138,631$ and $72.2\%$ per tag at $N = 1,341,763$ even in the complete absence of PCR chimeras. Thus, labeling $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ families as PCR chimeras substantially overestimates chimera rates by failing to subtract the null single-end collision expectation.

* **Relaxing consensus calling to $\text{FS} \ge 1$ with VAR-A tiering increases DCS coverage by $10\text{--}25\%$ while preserving accuracy for known heterozygous and $\sim 1\%$ variants: [Well Supported].**  
  Across `PF2-CRISPR` and `PF2-Standard`, lowering the Du Novo threshold from $\text{FS} \ge 3$ to $\text{FS} \ge 1$ increases on-target DCS depth from `20,217` to `22,426` ($+10.9\%$) and from `101,095` to `125,826` ($+24.5\%$), respectively (Table 4). For the known somatic variant `chr17:7674230-C-T` ($\sim 1\%$ VAF) and germline heterozygotes (`chr17:7674089-A-C`, `chr17:7674109-G-A`, `chr17:7674797-T-C`), allele frequencies remain invariant ($1.00\%$ vs. $1.04\%$, and $49.28\text{--}49.54\%$ vs. $49.3\text{--}49.4\%$; Table 5), while rescuing `147` to `183` additional mutant DCS in Tiers `1.2–2.4`.

* **VAR-A with $\text{FS} \ge 1$ reliably discovers ultra-low-frequency variants ($\sim 10^{-4}$) without increasing false positives: [Fragile].**  
  The two ultra-rare candidate variants highlighted in the text as rescued by $\text{FS} \ge 1$ (`chr17:7669456-C-A` at $1/3983 = 0.03\%$ in `PF2-CRISPR`, and `chr17:7675381-T-C` at $0.02\%$ in `PF2-Standard`) are weakly supported. Specifically, `chr17:7669456-C-A` is supported by a single molecule where one strand had only $2/3$ reads carrying the variant (failing the standard $>75\%$ consensus purity rule and falling into Tier 3), and is absent in `PF2-Standard` (sequenced from the same biological sample). Conversely, `chr17:7675381-T-C` sits adjacent to a noisy poly-T homopolymer (`chr17:7675394–7675411`) and co-occurs with Tier `4.1` and `5` artifact calls at the same locus. In the orthogonal dilution experiment, $2$ of $4$ spiked-in variants at $10^{-4}$ were missed due to Poisson sampling at the achieved coverage, and background non-spiked false-positive rates per tier at $\text{FS} \ge 1$ are not tabulated in the main text.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Incorporate an Explicit Null Model for Single-End Barcode Collisions in Chimera Analysis (CA)
* **WHY:** Because each tag consists of two independently ligated $10\text{-nt}$ random adapters ($a$ and $b$, each drawn from $4^{10} \approx 1.05 \times 10^6$ possibilities), two unrelated DNA templates frequently share one $10\text{-nt}$ adapter by chance when library complexity $N > 10^5$, producing $\text{TD}_{a,\min} = 0$ and $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ without any PCR template switching.
* **WHAT:** Derive and plot the analytical birthday-problem null expectation for single-end barcode sharing alongside the empirical $\text{relative }\Delta\text{TD} = 1$ rates, and rename the metric in Figure 4 from "Chimeric reads" to "Single-end shared tags (Chimeras + Collisions)".
* **HOW:** For a library with $N$ barcode-corrected families and single-end barcode length $k = 10$ (effective alphabet size $M \le 4^k$, accounting for base-composition bias), compute the expected probability that a random tag shares at least one end ($a$ or $b$) with another family under independent ligation: $P(\text{collision}) = 1 - (1 - 1/M)^{2(N-1)}$. Subtract this baseline or simulate $N$ random $(a,b)$ pairs drawn from the empirical nucleotide frequencies of `PF1`/`PF2` to estimate the true excess PCR chimera fraction.

#### 2. Quantify Tier-Specific False Positive Rates (FPR) Using the Spiked-In Dilution Dataset
* **WHY:** Including $\text{FS} = 1$ and $\text{FS} = 2$ single-stranded families (Tiers `1.2–2.4` and `3.1–3.2`) increases sensitivity for rare alleles, but early-cycle PCR errors or complementary-strand DNA damage occurring on singleton reads could inflate background error rates.
* **WHAT:** Report a tier-by-tier confusion matrix and background error rate (false-positive variant calls per duplex base sequenced outside the 4 spiked-in loci) comparing $\text{FS} \ge 3$ vs. $\text{FS} \ge 1$ on the `PRJNA684907` dilution libraries.
* **HOW:** Using the VAR-A outputs on the dilution series, tabulate for cumulative tier cutoffs (Tier `1.1` only; Tiers `1.1–2.4`; Tiers `1.1–3.2`; all tiers) both the recall of the 4 spiked variants across the $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-4}$ dilutions and the number of unexpected background substitutions divided by total duplex bases sequenced.

#### 3. Resolve Contradictions and Typographical Errors Between Main Text and Tables
* **WHY:** Several numerical and classification discrepancies between the main text and tables can confuse readers auditing the VAR-A tier definitions.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 5 vs. Main Text (Tier of `chr17:7669456-C-A`):** In the Results text, variant `chr17:7669456-C-A` in `PF2-CRISPR` is described as *"classified as tier 3.1"*, whereas Table 5 lists `0` under column `3.1` and `1` under column `3.2`. Since its mutation count is `1` (not a recurring mutation across multiple molecules as required by Tier `3.1` in Table 1), update the main text to state `tier 3.2`.
  2. **Equation 1 Indexing:** Equation 1 writes the sum from $k = 0$ to $n$ ($\sum_{k=0}^n (X_{ik} \ne X_{jk})$), which has $n + 1$ terms for a tag of length $n$. Change the lower index to $k = 1$ (or upper index to $n - 1$).
  3. **Table 5 Indel Call Discrepancy (`chr17:7676325-C-Del`):** Table 5 shows that the UW pipeline detects `chr17:7676325-C-Del` at $51.8\%$ VAF (`1532/2956`), whereas both Du Novo $\text{FS} \ge 3$ and $\text{FS} \ge 1$ columns are blank. Add a brief explanation in the text or Table 5 footnote clarifying why this germline heterozygous deletion was omitted or filtered out in the Du Novo/FreeBayes workflow.

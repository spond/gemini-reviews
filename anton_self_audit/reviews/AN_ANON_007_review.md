### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the evolutionary relationship, divergence timing, and nucleotide diversity of the endangered Przewalski's horse (*Equus przewalskii*) relative to the domestic horse (*Equus caballus*), using an outgroup Somali wild ass (*Equus africanus somaliensis*). Using Illumina Genome Analyzer II paired-end sequencing (35-bp reads for four Przewalski's horses representing all four surviving matrilines—Staraja II, Bijsk/2, Bijsk B, and Orlica III—and 100-bp reads for Somali wild ass) combined with Sanger capillary validation across eight additional Przewalski's horses, the authors assemble 10,840 bp of uniquely mapped mitochondrial DNA (60–260$\times$ coverage from heart tissue; 7$\times$ from blood) and recover 24.5–106.2 Mb of autosomal and 0.8–5.6 Mb of X-chromosomal nuclear sequence per individual at depth $\ge 2$. They find that Przewalski's horse mtDNA splits into two divergent haplogroups (haplotypes I/II vs. III, differing by 0.548%, BEAST coalescent estimate 0.156 Ma [95% HPD: 0.117–0.186 Ma]) that are paraphyletic with respect to domestic horses. In contrast, Neighbor-Joining (NJ) analysis of Tamura–Nei (TN93) pairwise autosomal distances groups the four Przewalski's horses into a monophyletic clade relative to the Thoroughbred reference genome (`eca2`), with mean autosomal diversity $\pi = 0.195\%$ within Przewalski's horses and mean divergence $d_{XY} = 0.226\%$ ($T_{\text{MRCA}} \approx 0.411\text{ Ma}$) between Przewalski's and Thoroughbred horses. Raw sequencing reads (`DRA000429`), mtDNA assemblies (`AP012267–AP012271`), and custom C bootstrapping code in `[SOFTWARE_PACKAGE]` are publicly archived.

This study represents a major advance in data scale over prior single-locus and control-region equine studies, and its mitochondrial conclusion—that surviving Przewalski's horse matrilines harbor two deeply divergent haplogroups that predate domestication (~5.5 ka) by >100,000 years and are paraphyletic with domestic mares—is robust and validated by capillary sequencing. However, the low-coverage nuclear genomic analysis suffers from three methodological issues that affect the quantitative coalescent dates, confidence intervals, and autosomal monophyly claims: (1) discarding all within-individual polymorphic sites at depth $\ge 2$ (`Materials and Methods`, line 36) while comparing 35-bp Illumina reads against the Sanger-assembled `eca2` reference creates asymmetric error and heterozygote-filtering biases between the four resequenced Przewalski's samples and the single reference domestic genome; (2) reporting raw pairwise sequence coalescence times ($T_{\text{MRCA}} = d_{XY}/2\mu = 0.411\text{ Ma}$) as lineage divergence estimates without subtracting ancestral diversity ($\pi = 0.195\%$, $T_{\text{MRCA}} = 0.353\text{ Ma}$) overstates the net population split time ($d_A = d_{XY} - \pi = 0.031\%$, corresponding to $T_{\text{split}} \approx 0.058\text{ Ma}$) by sevenfold; and (3) nucleotide-level bootstrapping under a fixed point calibration (2.0 Ma) produces unrealistically narrow confidence intervals (`0.409–0.413 Ma`) that ignore locus-to-locus coalescent variance and fossil calibration uncertainty. These issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis of the existing alignments.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Surviving Przewalski's horse maternal lineages comprise two deeply divergent mtDNA haplogroups (I/II vs. III, coalescing 0.117–0.186 Ma) that do not form a monophyletic clade relative to domestic horses: [Well Supported].**  
  The 10,840-bp mitochondrial alignment is backed by high unique read depth (60–260$\times$ in three individuals, 7$\times$ in Bars), targeted capillary confirmation across eight additional pedigree animals, Bayesian relaxed-clock phylogenetic inference in BEAST, and topological hypothesis tests (KH, SH, wKH, wSH in CONSEL, Supplementary Table S4) that significantly reject reciprocal monophyly and direct ancestor–descendant topologies.

* **Przewalski's horses retain substantial autosomal ($\pi = 0.195\%$) and mitochondrial nucleotide diversity despite a 20th-century bottleneck to 12–13 founders: [Well Supported].**  
  Both the divergence between mtDNA haplotypes I/II and III (59 substitutions across 10,840 bp, 0.548%) and the autosomal pairwise divergence across tens of millions of bases consistently show that the surviving captive population retained ancestral polymorphism from diverse wild founders (and/or historical introgression), directly informing conservation breeding management.

* **Autosomal sequences of Przewalski's horses form a monophyletic clade separate from domestic horses, with sequence coalescence of 0.411 Ma (95% CI: 0.409–0.413 Ma): [Fragile].**  
  While the authors appropriately caution that only a single domestic horse nuclear genome (the Thoroughbred reference `eca2`) was available (lines 65, 71, 80, 102), three methodological factors weaken the autosomal tree and dating:
  1. *Platform and pipeline asymmetry between the four Przewalski's samples and the `eca2` reference:* All four Przewalski's horses were sequenced with 35-bp Illumina GAII reads, mapped to `eca2` allowing $\le 2$ mismatches, and filtered to sites with $\ge 2$ identical reads (discarding all within-individual heterozygous calls). In contrast, the domestic horse is represented by the `eca2` reference sequence itself, which has no 35-bp mapping attrition and no Illumina base-calling error. At depth $k = 2$ with a $Q20$ filter ($P_{\text{err}} \le 0.01$), residual correlated sequencing errors or repetitive 35-bp read mismapping shared among the four Illumina samples will lengthen terminal/internal Przewalski branches relative to the reference genome, while mapping 35-bp reads with $\le 2$ mismatches against `eca2` introduces reference bias against highly divergent non-Thoroughbred alleles.
  2. *Conflation of sequence coalescence time ($T_{\text{MRCA}}$) with population divergence time ($T_{\text{split}}$):* In the Abstract ("diverged at least 0.117 Ma... diverged potentially as early as ~0.150 Ma") and Results (line 69), raw pairwise sequence coalescence times are used to characterize lineage divergence. However, from the authors' own autosomal numbers (line 69), mean divergence between Thoroughbred and Przewalski's horse is $d_{XY} = 0.226\%$ ($0.411\text{ Ma}$) while mean diversity within Przewalski's horse is $\pi = 0.195\%$ ($0.353\text{ Ma}$). Under standard coalescent theory ($d_{XY} = 2\mu T_{\text{split}} + \pi_{\text{anc}}$), over 85% of the pairwise distance $d_{XY}$ is pre-existing ancestral polymorphism ($\pi_{\text{anc}} \approx 0.195\%$), yielding a net mutational divergence of $d_A = d_{XY} - \pi = 0.031\%$ and an estimated population split time of $T_{\text{split}} = d_A / (2\mu) \approx \mathbf{0.056\text{--}0.058\text{ Ma}}$ (~56–58 ka). While 56–58 ka still predates horse domestication (~5.5 ka), failing to compute $d_A$ and $T_{\text{split}}$ leaves a major gap between the reported 0.411 Ma nuclear $T_{\text{MRCA}}$ and the 0.150 Ma model in the Discussion.
  3. *Severely anti-conservative confidence intervals (`0.409–0.413 Ma`):* Bootstrapping individual sites across 24–106 Mb treats linked nucleotides as independent and ignores both coalescent variance across independent genomic blocks and uncertainty in the 2.0 Ma *Equus* fossil calibration, producing a 95% CI spanning only $\pm 0.002\text{ Ma}$.

* **Rejection of within-individual polymorphic nuclear sites ("all polymorphic sites were discarded", line 36) deflates nucleotide diversity estimates (line 104): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The authors correctly recognize in Discussion line 104 that requiring $\ge 2$ identical reads and discarding polymorphic sites within an individual deflates nuclear diversity estimates. Specifically, at read depth $k$, a true heterozygous site in an individual is retained as a pseudohaploid homozygous call with probability $2^{-(k-1)}$ (50% at $k=2$, 25% at $k=3$) and discarded as polymorphic with probability $1 - 2^{-(k-1)}$. Because sites in regions of higher local coverage ($k \ge 3$) are disproportionately purged when heterozygous in either sample of a pairwise comparison, between-individual pairwise distances $\hat{\pi}$ and $\hat{d}_{XY}$ are systematically biased downward unless random single-read sampling (or a formal genotype likelihood model) is used.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Net Nucleotide Divergence ($d_A$) and Population Split Time ($T_{\text{split}}$) Alongside Sequence Coalescence Time ($T_{\text{MRCA}}$)
* **WHY:** Reporting $T_{\text{MRCA}} = 0.411\text{ Ma}$ ($d_{XY} = 0.226\%$) for Thoroughbred–Przewalski divergence alongside $T_{\text{MRCA}} = 0.353\text{ Ma}$ ($\pi = 0.195\%$) within Przewalski's horses without computing net divergence $d_A = d_{XY} - \pi$ conflates gene-tree coalescence in a large ancestral population with the population splitting event.
* **WHAT:** Compute and report net nucleotide divergence $d_A = d_{XY} - \pi_{\text{Prz}}$ (or fit an isolation-with-migration / coalescent divergence model) for both autosomes and the X chromosome.
* **HOW:** Using the pairwise distances in Supplementary Table S3 and line 69, add explicit calculations of $d_A = 0.226\% - 0.195\% = 0.031\%$ for autosomes (giving $T_{\text{split}} = d_A / (2 \times 2.75 \times 10^{-9}) \approx 0.056\text{ Ma}$) and $d_A = 0.179\% - 0.180\% \approx 0.000\%$ for the female X chromosome (consistent with Figure 3B showing complete X-chromosomal intermingling), and discuss how this reconciles the nuclear and mitochondrial time scales.

#### 2. Replace the Homozygous-Only Nuclear Filter with Random Single-Read Pseudohaploid Sampling or Quality-Weighted Genotype Likelihoods
* **WHY:** Discarding all sites where $\ge 2$ reads within an individual carry different alleles (`Materials and Methods`, line 36) removes $1 - 2^{-(k-1)}$ of true heterozygous sites within each individual, creating a coverage-dependent downward bias in $\pi$ and $d_{XY}$. Moreover, at depth $k = 2$, two concordant sequencing errors ($P_{\text{err}}^2$) or shared 35-bp mapping artifacts in the four Illumina samples can artificially inflate the branch separating the four Illumina-sequenced Przewalski's horses from the Sanger-sequenced `eca2` reference.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the autosomal and X-chromosomal pairwise distance matrices using unbiased pseudohaploid sampling (randomly drawing 1 read of base quality $\ge Q30$ at each covered site, or retaining polymorphic sites and weighting each allele at depth $\ge 2$ by 0.5) and verify whether the NJ topology and branch lengths in Figure 3A remain invariant.
* **HOW:** In the authors' alignment parsing pipeline, instead of discarding sites where two reads in an individual differ, assign fractional state counts $(0.5, 0.5)$ to the two observed nucleotides in the Tamura–Nei pairwise count matrix (or raise the minimum base quality filter from $Q20$ to $Q30$ at homozygous depth-2 sites to bound residual Illumina error rates below $10^{-6}$). Compare the resulting $\pi$, $d_{XY}$, and NJ tree against Figure 3A.

#### 3. Propagate Coalescent Block Variance and Calibration Uncertainty into Nuclear Divergence Time Intervals
* **WHY:** The reported 95% confidence interval for autosomal coalescence time (`0.409–0.413 Ma`, line 69) reflects only site-level binomial sampling noise across ~50 Mb while treating the 2.0 Ma fossil calibration and mutation rate ($\mu = 2.75 \times 10^{-9}\text{ site}^{-1}\text{ yr}^{-1}$) as exact constants and ignoring linkage disequilibrium across adjacent sites.
* **WHAT:** Compute genomic block-bootstrap confidence intervals (e.g., resampling 1-Mb chromosomal windows or whole chromosomes) and propagate a realistic prior interval on the *Equus* calibration point (e.g., 1.5–2.5 Ma).
* **HOW:** Partition the pairwise chromosomal alignments into non-overlapping 1-Mb blocks (or by the 31 equine autosomes), resample blocks with replacement 1,000 times in the custom C bootstrap utility to account for intralocus linkage and coalescent variance, and report both the mutation-scaled genetic distance CIs ($d_{XY}$ in %) and calibration-propagated age intervals.

#### 4. Control for Read-Length Asymmetry Between Somali Wild Ass (100 bp) and Przewalski's Horses (35 bp)
* **WHY:** Somali wild ass was sequenced with 100-bp reads whereas Przewalski's horses were sequenced with 35-bp reads, yet both were mapped to `eca2` using BWA with fixed default parameters (`<= 2 mismatches` across the full untrimmed read, line 34). Requiring $\le 2$ mismatches across a 100-bp read (maximum divergence 2%) is much more stringent than requiring $\le 2$ mismatches across a 35-bp read (maximum divergence 5.7%), selectively filtering out more divergent regions of the Somali wild ass genome and biasing the outgroup divergence estimate ($\sim 1.1\%$, line 69) downward—which in turn biases the calibrated mutation rate $\mu$ downward and inflates all inferred horse coalescent times!
* **WHAT:** Trim the Somali wild ass 100-bp reads to 35 bp prior to BWA mapping with $\le 2$ mismatches so that outgroup and ingroup reads experience identical mapping stringency.
* **HOW:** Truncate the Somali wild ass (`OR3030`) FASTQ reads to their first 35 bp, re-map to `eca2` with BWA (`<= 2 mismatches`), and recompute the Somali wild ass–horse autosomal divergence (currently 1.1%) and X-chromosome divergence (currently 0.817%) along with the resulting molecular clock rates.

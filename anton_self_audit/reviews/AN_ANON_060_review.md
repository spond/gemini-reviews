### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the prevalence, tissue concordance, somatic accumulation, and intergenerational transmission of human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) heteroplasmy across 39 healthy mother–child pairs of European ancestry (156 total samples: blood and buccal swabs from each mother and child). Sequencing two overlapping ~9-kb long-range PCR amplicons on the Illumina MiSeq platform to a mean depth of $19,789\times$ per sample, combined with alternating pUC18/$\phi$X174 spike-ins to monitor cross-contamination and orthogonal validation via Sanger sequencing (44/44 confirmed at $\text{MAF} \ge 10\%$) and droplet digital PCR (ddPCR; 16/18 confirmed at $1\% \le \text{MAF} < 10\%$ upon re-amplification), the authors identify 172 validated point heteroplasmies across 98 mother–child quartets at a $\text{MAF} \ge 1\%$ discovery threshold. They report an average of ~1 heteroplasmy per individual, a 1-in-8 carrier rate of disease-associated mtDNA variants, purifying selection against nonsynonymous heteroplasmies during transmission, a positive Poisson regression association between child heteroplasmy count and maternal age at fertilization ($P = 0.005\text{--}0.010$), an effective germ-line bottleneck size of $N \approx 30\text{--}35$ (IQR 9–141), and a germ-line mutation rate of $1.3 \times 10^{-8}$ mutations per site per year. Raw sequencing reads are deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (`SRP047378`), and galaxy tools and parameters are documented.

The experimental execution is rigorous: the dual-tissue mother–child quartet design, Rho0 negative controls, exogenous spike-ins, and independent ddPCR re-amplification from template DNA set a high standard for controlling NUMTs, cross-sample carryover, and PCR polymerase errors. However, the population-genetic estimator used to infer the effective bottleneck size $N$ contains a mathematical bias: computing $\hat{N}_i = p_i(1-p_i)/(\Delta p_i^2 - 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}})$ separately for each single-transmission quartet $i$ and taking the median across $i = 1, \dots, 51$ divides by the median of a single degree-of-freedom chi-squared variate ($\text{median}(\chi^2_1) \approx 0.455$ rather than $E[\chi^2_1] = 1$), which systematically inflates the median point estimate of $N$ by a factor of $\sim 2.2\times$ and produces undefined/negative values whenever $\Delta p_i^2 \le 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$. In addition, the Poisson regression of child heteroplasmy count on maternal age at fertilization does not control for the child's age at sample collection or maternal heteroplasmy count, and a factual typo in the main text misstates that 96 of 98 heteroplasmies are transversions rather than transitions. All of these issues can be resolved through statistical re-estimation on the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Healthy individuals carry ~1 point heteroplasmy ($\text{MAF} \ge 1\%$) on average, with $89\%$ reproducibility at $1\%\text{--}10\%$ MAF upon independent PCR re-amplification and ddPCR: [Well Supported].**  
  The combination of ultra-deep sequencing ($\ge 1,000\times$ required per site), strand-bias filtering, spike-in carryover controls, Sanger validation (44/44 for $\text{MAF} \ge 10\%, R^2 = 75\%$), and ddPCR on both initial and independently re-amplified mtDNA templates (16/18 for $1\% \le \text{MAF} < 10\%, R^2 = 79\%$, identifying 2 PCR-induced false positives) provides direct experimental quantification of both true biological prevalence and pre-sequencing PCR error rates.

* **Purifying selection acts against nonsynonymous mtDNA heteroplasmies both within individuals and during mother-to-child transmission: [Well Supported].**  
  Protein-coding nonsynonymous heteroplasmies are significantly depleted relative to neutral expectation ($25$ observed vs. $60.2$ expected under neutrality, $P = 5 \times 10^{-3}$, Table 1), exhibit a significant downward shift in allele frequency from mother to child ($P = 9.54 \times 10^{-7}$, one-tailed sign test), and represent a smaller fraction of transmitted heteroplasmies ($5/43 = 12\%$) than untransmitted maternal heteroplasmies ($8/22 = 36\%, P = 0.025$, Fisher's exact test).

* **Child heteroplasmy count increases significantly with maternal age at fertilization due to oocyte aging ($P = 0.010$ combined, $P = 0.005$ buccal, $P = 0.006$ blood): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the univariate Poisson GLM shows a positive slope with maternal age at fertilization (Figure 2), two potential confounders are unadjusted: (1) maternal age at fertilization correlates strongly with maternal age at collection ($R^2 = 43\%, P = 0.002$), and older mothers harbor more heteroplasmies themselves ($P = 0.039$), so mothers conceiving later may transmit more pre-existing maternal heteroplasmies rather than solely de novo oocyte mutations; and (2) the child's own age at collection (postnatal somatic accumulation window) is not included as a covariate in the model.

* **The effective human germ-line mtDNA bottleneck size is $N \approx 30\text{--}35$ (median $32.3$, IQR $10.5\text{--}103.3$) and the germ-line mutation rate is $1.3 \times 10^{-8}$ per site per year: [Fragile].**  
  In line 184, the authors invert the drift variance formula for each of the 51 maternal quartets individually: $\hat{N}_i = p_i(1-p_i)/(\sigma^2_{\text{raw},i} - 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}})$, where $\sigma^2_{\text{raw},i} = (p_{M,i} - p_{C,i})^2$ is a single squared difference ($1$ degree of freedom), and then report the median of $\hat{N}_i$ across the 51 quartets. Under pure drift, $(p_{C,i} - p_{M,i})^2 \approx \frac{p_i(1-p_i)}{N} Z_i^2$ with $Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Because $\text{median}(Z_i^2) = \text{median}(\chi^2_1) = 0.4549 \ne E[Z_i^2] = 1$, taking the median of the reciprocals $\hat{N}_i = N / Z_i^2$ yields $\text{median}(\hat{N}_i) = N / 0.4549 \approx 2.20 N$. Furthermore, whenever $(p_{C,i} - p_{M,i})^2 \le 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$, subtracting $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ yields a zero or negative variance estimate, which either truncates small-drift sites or flips their sign. Because the mutation rate estimator $\mu = \mu_0 / [2 N \ln(1/\theta - 1)]$ (line 186) divides directly by $N$, a $2.2\times$ upward bias in $N$ induces a $2.2\times$ downward bias in $\mu$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace the Biased Site-by-Site Median Inversion with a Joint Likelihood or Pooled Standardized Variance Estimator for $N$
* **WHY:** For a single transmission event $i$, $\Delta p_i^2 / [p_i(1-p_i)]$ is distributed as $\frac{1}{N}\chi^2_1$ (under the normal/diffusion approximation). Inverting a single $\chi^2_1$ draw and taking the median across sites estimates $N / \text{median}(\chi^2_1) \approx 2.20 N$ rather than $N$, and subtracting $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ site-by-site produces negative variances when $\Delta p_i^2 < 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$.
* **WHAT:** Re-estimate the effective bottleneck size $N$ (and consequently $\mu$) using either the pooled method-of-moments estimator across all $K = 51$ quartets or a maximum-likelihood / Kimura-Ohta beta-binomial transition density model that integrates over measurement error.
* **HOW:** Compute the pooled standardized drift variance across all $K = 51$ transmissions prior to inversion:
  $$\hat{N}_{\text{pooled}} = \left[ \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{(p_{C,i} - p_{M,i})^2 - 4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}}{p_{M,i}(1 - p_{M,i})} \right]^{-1}$$
  or fit $N$ by maximizing the exact binomial/Wright-Fisher log-likelihood $\sum_{i=1}^K \ln P(p_{C,i} \mid p_{M,i}, N, \sigma^2_{\text{measure}})$ with a 95% profile-likelihood or bootstrap confidence interval across families. Update the downstream mutation rate $\mu = 0.109 \mu_0 / N$ accordingly.

#### 2. Disentangle Inherited vs. De Novo Child Heteroplasmies and Adjust for Child Age in the Maternal Age Regression
* **WHY:** The total number of heteroplasmies in a child is the sum of inherited maternal heteroplasmies (category "all" and "somatic loss") and newly arising germ-line mutations (category "child"). Regressing total child heteroplasmies on maternal age at fertilization without adjusting for maternal heteroplasmy burden or child age at collection conflates transmission of pre-existing maternal variants with oocyte mutation accumulation.
* **WHAT:** Fit a multivariable Poisson (or negative binomial) regression for child heteroplasmies that adjusts for child age at collection and maternal heteroplasmy count, and test the maternal age at fertilization effect separately on transmitted quartets ("all" + "SL", $n = 47$) versus child-only candidate de novo quartets ("child", $n = 16$).
* **HOW:** Using the 39 mother–child pairs in Dataset S1, run `glm(child_het ~ maternal_age_fert + child_age_collection + mother_het, family = poisson)` and report the adjusted rate ratios and $P$-values for total child heteroplasmies as well as stratified by transmission category ("child" vs. "all").

#### 3. Account for Within-Family Clustering in Quartet-Level Statistical Tests
* **WHY:** The 98 quartets (and 51 maternal quartets used for bottleneck estimation) are nested within 31 heteroplasmy-positive families out of 39 total families, meaning multiple sites share the same mother–child transmission bottleneck and maternal age. Treating all 98 quartets as independent observations understates standard errors.
* **WHAT:** Account for family-level clustering when computing confidence intervals for $N$, tissue correlations ($R^2$), and selection tests.
* **HOW:** Compute cluster-robust standard errors or perform block-bootstrapping at the family level ($n = 39$ families resampled with replacement) to obtain confidence intervals for $N$, $\mu$, and the inter-tissue/inter-generational $R^2$ values in Figure 1.

#### 4. Correct the Transition vs. Transversion Error and Check Measurement Variance Scaling
* **WHY:** Line 110 states: *"Among the 98 point heteroplasmies 96 were transversions, resulting in a transition-to-transversion ratio of 48."* A transition-to-transversion ratio of $48 = 96/2$ means 96 were **transitions** and 2 were transversions (which also matches line 204, where the authors note the high transition-to-transversion ratio and absence of transversion-causing oxidative damage). Additionally, in line 184, the variance of the difference between the mean of two maternal tissues and the mean of two child tissues, $\Delta \bar{p} = \frac{p_{M1}+p_{M2}}{2} - \frac{p_{C1}+p_{C2}}{2}$, has measurement error variance $\text{Var}(\Delta \bar{p}_{\text{measure}}) = \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_m + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_m + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_m + \frac{1}{4}\sigma^2_m = \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$, not $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ (which would apply to the unaveraged sum $(p_{M1}+p_{M2}) - (p_{C1}+p_{C2})$).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Change "96 were transversions" to "96 were transitions" on line 110; and verify the exact coefficient on $\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ in line 184 ($\text{Var}(\frac{X_1+X_2}{2} - \frac{Y_1+Y_2}{2}) = \sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ vs. $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$) and recompute $\sigma^2_{\text{gen}}$ if $4\sigma^2_{\text{measure}}$ was subtracted from the squared difference of tissue means.

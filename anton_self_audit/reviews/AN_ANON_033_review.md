### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents `Du Novo`, a reference-free computational pipeline for generating single-stranded consensus sequences (SSCSs) and duplex consensus sequences (DCSs) from Duplex Sequencing (DS) reads. Unlike prior duplex sequencing pipelines that align raw reads to a reference genome before grouping by molecular tag, `Du Novo` lexicographically normalizes complementary $12\text{-bp}$ $\alpha$ and $\beta$ barcodes into a canonical strand-independent tag, performs within-family multiple sequence alignment via `MAFFT`, calls majority-rule quality-weighted SSCSs, and aligns paired complementary SSCSs via Smith–Waterman (`swalign`) to generate reference-free DCSs prior to mapping (`BWA-MEM`) and variant calling (`Naive Variant Caller` or `FreeBayes`). The authors evaluate `Du Novo` across three settings: (1) an *in silico* mitochondrial heteroplasmy simulation ($21$ mutations spiked at $10^{-4}$ frequency into human `rCRS` mtDNA across $2.5 \times 10^7$ reference and $2,500$ mutant fragments with $30$ PCR cycles and $0.1\%$ per-base per-cycle error); (2) a head-to-head re-analysis of a published clinical chronic myeloid leukemia `ABL1` dataset (`SRR1799908`, $6,921,891$ read pairs); and (3) new duplex sequencing of long-range PCR-enriched human mtDNA from child buccal tissue in family `SC8` ($17,385,100$ read pairs). All source code, galaxy workflows, and sequencing datasets are publicly available.

`Du Novo` is a well-engineered and practical methodological advance: decoupling consensus generation from reference alignment eliminates reference mapping bias at indel-rich or divergent loci and increases on-target DCS depth on the `ABL1` benchmark by $78\%$ (`1,099x` vs. `618x`) while matching the published `E279K` (`G>A`) minor allele frequency ($1.28\text{--}1.31\%$ vs. $1.29\text{--}1.31\%$). The manuscript also identifies a critical experimental pitfall when applying duplex sequencing to PCR amplicons—heteroduplex formation during late-cycle annealing causes mismatched strands to be masked as IUPAC ambiguities during DCS calling, depressing the apparent `SC8` site `13,708` heteroplasmy frequency from $2.2\%$ (and $1.7\%$ in SSCS) down to $0.53\%$ in DCS. The primary weaknesses are (1) an unverified mathematical model of barcode error accumulation (Equations 1–2 and the `SC8` singleton calculation) that conflates per-cycle PCR replication errors with read-level singleton family fractions and omits the equations themselves from the formatted text, and (2) an incomplete simulation benchmark that disables base quality filtering (`PHRED = 40` constant) and does not compare `Du Novo` directly against the reference-based pipeline on indel-containing or divergent sequences where a reference-free approach is claimed to excel.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Reference-free consensus building (`Du Novo`) matches the variant calling accuracy of reference-based duplex sequencing on clinical `ABL1` data while increasing DCS coverage by $78\%$: [Well Supported].**  
  Re-analysis of `SRR1799908` ($6,921,891$ read pairs; $20,746$ duplex families with $\ge 3$ read pairs per strand) recovers the imatinib-resistance mutation `E279K` at $1.28\%$ (`NVC`) and $1.31\%$ (`FreeBayes`), matching the published reference-based pipeline ($1.29\%$ and $1.31\%$) while increasing DCS depth at the locus from `618x` to `1,099x` (`14` mutant DCS supported by $5\text{--}102$ raw reads per family; Table 1, Figure 3a).

* **`Du Novo` achieves high sensitivity ($20/21$) and zero false positives at $\text{MAF} = 10^{-4}$ in simulated mitochondrial duplex sequencing: [Moderately Supported].**  
  At median DCS coverage of $166,574\times$, `Du Novo` detects $20$ of $21$ simulated $10^{-4}$ heteroplasmies with $0$ false positives above the cutoff, missing one locus whose sampled frequency fluctuated down to $4 \times 10^{-5}$. However, the simulation sets all base quality scores to a constant `PHRED = 40`, turns off `Du Novo` quality filtering (`PHRED threshold = 0`), spaces all $21$ point substitutions $\ge 600\text{ bp}$ apart without indels in the truth set, and does not run the Kennedy et al. reference-based pipeline on the same simulated reads for a controlled head-to-head comparison.

* **Applying Duplex Sequencing to PCR-amplified mtDNA (`SC8`) underestimates heteroplasmy frequency in DCS ($0.53\%$) relative to SSCS ($1.7\%$) due to post-PCR heteroduplex formation: [Well Supported].**  
  In family `SC8` child buccal mtDNA enriched via long-range PCR, site `13,708` shows a DCS MAF of $0.53\%$ (`6/1,138` DCS, strand bias $\text{SB} = 0.01$), whereas all 20 other candidate sites with $\text{MAF} \ge 0.5\%$ exhibit strong strand bias ($\text{SB} = 0.94\text{--}6.08$), confirming site `13,708` as the sole true heteroplasmy. Switching from DCS to SSCS restores the frequency to $1.7\%$ ($\text{SB} = 0.02$, depth `4,548x`), close to the $2.2\%$ original measurement. Because long-range PCR precedes adapter ligation, annealing of wild-type and mutant strands during late PCR cycles forms heteroduplex molecules that receive a single duplex tag and subsequently get converted into IUPAC ambiguity codes (`R` for `G/A`) during DCS reconciliation, dropping out of standard variant counts.

* **Analytical modeling of PCR/sequencing errors within $24\text{-nt}$ barcodes explains singleton family formation and yields an estimated $\sim 0.4\%$ cumulative tag error rate in `SC8`: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Results section (lines 64–67), the manuscript models the fraction of erroneous barcodes $r$ of length $l = 24$ under cumulative error rate $E$ using $r = 1 - (1 - E)^l$ (noting that the display equations themselves were dropped during text formatting at lines 64 and 66). There are three mathematical flaws in how this formula is applied:
  1. **PCR Tree Branching vs. Linear Stage Accumulation:** The authors compute the cumulative per-base error rate across $30$ PCR cycles plus $1$ sequencing step as $E = 1 - (1 - 0.001)^{31} \approx 3.05\%$, yielding $r = 1 - (1 - 0.0305)^{24} = 52.5\%$. However, in PCR, a molecule sequenced at cycle 30 has not undergone 30 serial copying events; under symmetric doubling, the number of replication generations separating a final read from the original adapter-ligated template averages $k/2 = 15$ to $\sim 20$ replication events depending on efficiency, and an error introduced in an early PCR cycle is shared across *multiple* descendant reads (forming a multi-read artifactual family rather than a single-read family).
  2. **Conflation of Read-Level and Family-Level Ratios in `SC8`:** At line 67, the authors divide the number of single-read families (`1,717,170` = `884,295` $\alpha\beta$ + `832,875` $\beta\alpha$ from Table 1) by the total number of read pairs (`17,385,100`) to get $r = 1,717,170 / 17,385,100 = 0.098$, and then invert $r = 1 - (1 - E)^{24}$ to estimate $E \approx 0.4\%$. However, this calculation assumes that *zero* single-read families are genuine unamplified or low-depth template molecules sampled only once by sequencing. In reality, whenever library complexity is high relative to sequencing depth, the Poisson/negative-binomial tail of family sizes naturally produces a large fraction of true $1\text{-read}$ families even when $E = 0$.
  3. **Table 1 Internal Inconsistency in Total DCS Count for `SC8`:** In the main text (line 59), the authors state that the `SC8` dataset was assembled into `82,230 DCSs`. However, Table 1 (line 50) reports `109,999` for *"Unique $\alpha\beta\beta\alpha$ with $\ge 3$ read pairs on both strands"*. Unlike `ABL1`, where the text (`20,746 DCSs`, line 52) matches Table 1 (`20,746`, line 50) exactly, the `27,769`-DCS discrepancy between `109,999` and `82,230` in `SC8` is never explained.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify Heteroduplex Dropout Directly by Counting IUPAC Ambiguity Codes in `SC8` DCSs
* **WHY:** The authors hypothesize that post-PCR heteroduplexes in `SC8` cause `Du Novo` to emit IUPAC ambiguity codes (`R` at site `13,708`) when reconciling complementary SSCSs (`G` on one strand, `A` on the other), thereby reducing the called DCS MAF from $1.7\%$ to $0.53\%$. Because `Du Novo` explicitly writes IUPAC ambiguity characters into the untrimmed DCS output (Methods, line 90), this hypothesis can be verified directly rather than inferred indirectly from SSCS counts.
* **WHAT:** Tabulate the exact number of DCS reads carrying an `R` (or filtered out by `Sequence Content Trimmer`) at position `13,708` in `SC8`.
* **HOW:** Inspect the unfiltered/untrimmed DCS alignment at `chrM:13,708` in `SC8` and count how many of the `109,999` duplex families carry `G/G` (`G`), `A/A` (`A`), and `G/A` or `A/G` (`R`). Under random post-PCR strand re-annealing with true allele frequency $p \approx 0.017$, homoduplex mutant molecules occur at frequency $p^2 \approx 0.0003$ and heteroduplexes at $2p(1-p) \approx 0.033$; showing the exact count of `R` calls at site `13,708` will directly validate the heteroduplex mechanism.

#### 2. Benchmark `Du Novo` vs. Reference-Based Duplex Calling on Indels and Divergent Sequences
* **WHY:** The primary motivation for a reference-free (`MAFFT` + `Smith–Waterman`) consensus caller is that reference alignment prior to consensus building fails or introduces bias around indels and divergent alleles (Background, line 21). Yet all three evaluations (`rCRS` point-mutation simulation, `ABL1` SNV `E279K`, and `SC8` SNV `13,708`) test only isolated point substitutions.
* **WHAT:** Extend the *in silico* mitochondrial simulation to include small insertions, deletions ($1\text{--}20\text{ bp}$), and clustered variants, and run both `Du Novo` and the Kennedy et al. reference-based pipeline on the identical simulated reads.
* **HOW:** Since the wgsim + coalescent PCR simulator (line 92) already introduces polymerase indels ($15\%$ of errors) and the authors already have the Kennedy et al. pipeline installed (line 53), spike $10$ small indels ($1\text{--}15\text{ bp}$) at $\text{MAF} = 10^{-3}\text{--}10^{-4}$ into `mt-mut` and compare recall, precision, and DCS recovery between `Du Novo` and the reference-based pipeline.

#### 3. Correct the Barcode Error Formulation, Restore Missing Equations, and Reconcile Table 1 Numbers
* **WHY:** The mathematical equations at lines 64 and 66 are missing from the text, the `ABL1` unique tag count and `SC8` DCS count disagree between Table 1 and the prose, and the singleton barcode error inversion ignores both coalescent error sharing and natural family-size singletons.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Restore Equations 1 and 2 (Lines 64–66):** Insert the explicit formulas $r = 1 - (1 - E)^l$ (Eq. 1) and the numerical substitution $r = 1 - (1 - 0.0305)^{24} = 0.525$ (Eq. 2).
  2. **Clarify Coalescent vs. Independent Error Assumption:** Note explicitly in the text that an error occurring in PCR cycle $c < 30$ is inherited by multiple daughter reads (creating a multi-read mutant tag family rather than a singleton), so only late-cycle PCR errors and final sequencing errors generate true singleton tag artifacts.
  3. **Reconcile Table 1 vs. Main Text Discrepancies:**
     - Line 35 states `ABL1` contains `1,468,089` unique tag combinations, whereas Table 1 (line 42) lists `1,467,768`.
     - Line 59 states `SC8` contains `2,100,704` unique tags assembled into `82,230` DCSs, whereas Table 1 lists `2,100,705` unique tags and `109,999` unique $\alpha\beta\beta\alpha$ families with $\ge 3$ read pairs on both strands. Explain whether `82,230` is the post-`Sequence Content Trimmer` / mapped DCS count out of `109,999` raw DCSs.
     - In Table 1 (lines 48–49), `SC8` lists `140,486` for *"Unique $\beta\alpha$ configurations with $\ge 3$ read pairs"* and `140,485` for *"Unique $\alpha\beta\beta\alpha$"*—verify that `140,485` is not a copy-paste error from the row above.

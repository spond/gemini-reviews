### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the relationship between infant growth trajectories from birth to age two and the composition of the oral and gut microbiota sampled at two years of age across 236 mother-child dyads from the INSIGHT cohort (retaining 215 maternal oral, 214 child oral, and 189 child stool samples after 16S rRNA V3–V4 sequencing and rarefaction to 100,000 reads). Weight-for-length ratios measured at seven time points (birth, 3–4 w, 16 w, 28 w, 40 w, 1 y, 2 y) are smoothed via PACE and aligned into continuous growth curves using Functional Data Analysis (FDA) alongside binary Conditional Weight Gain (CWG $\ge 0$ vs. $< 0$ from birth to 6 months). Using function-on-scalar regressions (Choi test), Mann-Whitney U tests, `bestglm` covariate selection across 17 clinical/dietary variables, and genus-level sparse/collinear phylogenetic merging followed by FLAME (Functional Linear Adaptive Mixed Estimation) and LEfSe, the authors report that 2-year child oral microbiota $\alpha$-diversity ($p = 0.041$) and Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes (F:B) ratio ($p = 0.0015$), as well as maternal oral $\alpha$-diversity ($p = 0.018$), are associated with 0–2 year growth curves, whereas child gut F:B is not and child gut $\alpha$-diversity is positively associated with growth only after adjusting for age-2 vegetable and fruit intake ($p = 0.020$). Raw reads (SRA PRJNA420339), clinical phenotypes (dbGaP phs001498.v1.p1), R scripts, and workflows are fully shared.

The integration of longitudinal growth curve modeling (PACE, function-on-scalar regression, and FLAME) with well-phenotyped mother-child dyads and open data/code archival is a clear strength. However, the study's bioinformatics, statistical modeling, and causal framing suffer from several major methodological flaws: (1) **temporal inversion in regression specification**: the function-on-scalar regressions model weight-for-length trajectories from *birth to 24 months* (and CWG from *birth to 6 months*) as the **response** variable predicted by microbiota and diet measured at **24 months** (lines 63, 68, 89, 156); (2) **phylum-level $\alpha$-diversity**: computing Inverse Simpson $\alpha$-diversity on *phylum-level* read counts (~6–8 phyla total; lines 23, 91, 140) measures coarse phylum evenness rather than ecological species/OTU/ASV richness and diversity; (3) **`prop.test` unit-of-analysis error**: applying Pearson's $\chi^2$ test (`prop.test`) to continuous compositional read proportions by multiplying the mean phylum proportion by $N$ subjects to invent discrete "success" counts (line 140) violates binomial count assumptions and ignores inter-subject variance; (4) **artificial NNNNN read concatenation into Kraken**: concatenating unmerged V3–V4 paired-end reads with `NNNNN` and classifying individual reads via $k$-mer matching in Kraken against GreenGenes (lines 136–138) biases classification toward short $k$-mer hits for degraded low-quality read pairs; and (5) **post-selection inference (`bestglm`) and uncorrected one-tailed testing**. All of these issues can be resolved computationally by re-running the existing data and R codebase.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Decreased $\alpha$-diversity ($p = 0.041$) and elevated Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes ratio ($p = 0.0015$) in the two-year-old oral microbiota (and lower maternal oral $\alpha$-diversity, $p = 0.018$) are associated with higher 0–2 year weight-for-length trajectories and 0–6 month rapid infant weight gain: [Fragile].**  
  First, the statistical model regresses a historical longitudinal trajectory $Y(t)$ ($t \in [0, 24]\text{ months}$, and CWG over $[0, 6]\text{ months}$) as the dependent response variable on a scalar predictor $X$ measured at $t = 24\text{ months}$ (lines 63, 148). Because an exposure at 24 months cannotretroactively cause weight gain between 0 and 6 months, the estimated coefficient curves $\hat{\beta}(t)$ for $t < 24\text{ months}$ reflect retrospective association (how infant growth predicts age-2 microbiota), not prospective prediction. Second, $\alpha$-diversity was calculated by applying the Inverse Simpson index to *phylum-level* classifications (lines 23, 91, 140). Because human oral and gut samples are dominated by 2–5 phyla (Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Proteobacteria, Actinobacteria, Fusobacteria), "phylum-level Inverse Simpson diversity" is mathematically redundant with the major phylum ratios (such as F:B) and does not measure species- or genus-level microbial diversity. Third, the CWG box-plot comparisons (Figure 3, $p = 0.049$, $0.019$, $0.036$) rely on one-tailed Mann-Whitney U tests without multiple-testing correction across the six comparisons; under standard two-tailed testing with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, $p = 0.049$ ($p_{\text{two-tailed}} = 0.098$) and $p = 0.036$ ($p_{\text{two-tailed}} = 0.072$) are not statistically significant.

* **Significant differences in phylum proportions between child gut, child oral, and maternal oral microbiota ($p = 1.1 \times 10^{-14}$ to $2.2 \times 10^{-5}$) established via `prop.test`: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Methods (line 140), the authors explain how they computed the $p$-values on line 56: *"we used `prop.test` from the base stats package in R (calculates Pearson's chi-squared test). For this test we took the average of all individual proportions (calculated as the proportion of reads assigned to that phyla) multiplied by the number of subjects to obtain the 'probability [sic: count] of successes', and to calculate 'failures' we took the number of individuals less the 'probability of successes'."* Converting a continuous compositional proportion $\bar{p} \in [0,1]$ across $N \approx 200$ subjects into a synthetic binomial count $k = N\bar{p}$ out of $N$ trials treats each human subject as a single Bernoulli trial and completely discards the empirical between-subject variance (overdispersion) of microbiome relative abundances. In addition, child gut and child oral samples come from the same children, and maternal-child samples come from paired dyads, violating the independence assumption of `prop.test`. While gut and oral communities clearly differ in composition (corroborated by Bray-Curtis NMDS), the `prop.test` procedure is statistically invalid.

* **Diet at age two explains 20.8% of variance in child gut F:B ratio ($p = 9.45 \times 10^{-9}$) and 5.9% in gut $\alpha$-diversity ($p = 3.76 \times 10^{-3}$), and controlling for diet uncovers a positive association between gut $\alpha$-diversity and 0–2 year growth curves ($p = 0.020$): [Fragile].**  
  The regression statistics in Table 2 were obtained by running `bestglm` exhaustive subset selection across 17 candidate covariates and then refitting standard ordinary least squares (`lm`) on the selected subset (vegetables, meats, fruit) *on the exact same dataset* without adjusting for post-selection inference (line 154), which inflates $R^2$ and biases $p$-values downward. Moreover, in the subsequent function-on-scalar regression (line 89 & 156), growth curves from *birth to age 2* are regressed on *age-2 diet* and *age-2 gut microbiota*, creating a collider/confounding structure where infant growth trajectory (0–24 m) can influence both parental feeding practices at 24 m and the 24 m microbiome.

* **Two-stage phylogenetic merging of sparse and collinear genera followed by FLAME and LEfSe identifies specific bacterial groups associated with child growth: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Tree-guided aggregation of adjacent low-count ($<5$ counts in $>90\%$ of samples) and highly correlated ($r > 0.7$) genera is a sensible dimensionality-reduction strategy before penalized regression. However, in Stage 1 (line 158) merged nodes are **summed**, whereas in Stage 2 (correlational merging) merged nodes are **averaged**; averaging rather than summing relative abundances alters the total count weight of multi-genus groups when rarefied counts are combined. Furthermore, FLAME and LEfSe identified completely non-overlapping sets of taxonomic groups (Figure 4), indicating substantial sensitivity to model specification and outcome encoding.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Recompute $\alpha$-Diversity at the Genus / OTU / ASV Level Rather Than the Phylum Level
* **WHY:** Computing Inverse Simpson $\alpha$-diversity on phylum-level counts (lines 23, 91, 140) collapses the entire bacterial tree into ~6–8 phyla, measuring phylum dominance (which is largely a mathematical transform of the Firmicutes:Bacteroidetes ratio) rather than true taxonomic richness and evenness.
* **WHAT:** Recompute Shannon and Inverse Simpson $\alpha$-diversity at the finest resolved taxonomic level (genus/species level from Kraken or de novo VSearch OTUs/ASVs) on the 100,000-read rarefied tables.
* **HOW:** Pass the rarefied genus-level (or VSearch OTU) count matrix to `vegan::diversity(x, index = "invsimpson")` and `index = "shannon"`, and re-run the six function-on-scalar regressions (Figure 2), CWG Mann-Whitney comparisons (Figure 3), and diet regressions (Table 2) to verify whether true taxonomic diversity is associated with growth curves.

#### 2. Reverse or Properly Frame the Temporal Direction of the Functional Regressions (Scalar-on-Function vs. Function-on-Scalar)
* **WHY:** Modeling longitudinal growth trajectories from $t = 0$ to $24\text{ months}$ as the dependent response $Y(t)$ predicted by microbiota and diet measured at $t = 24\text{ months}$ reverses chronological order: microbiota at 24 months is an outcome of (or contemporaneous with) the 0–24 month growth trajectory, not a baseline predictor at $t = 0$.
* **WHAT:** Fit **scalar-on-function regressions** where the 24-month microbiota metric (oral/gut diversity, F:B ratio, or taxonomic group abundance) is the scalar outcome $Y_{24\text{m}}$ and the 0–24 month growth trajectory $X(t)$ is the functional predictor (controlling for 24-month diet), or explicitly frame the function-on-scalar curves $\hat{\beta}(t)$ as retrospective trajectory profiles conditioned on 24-month microbiota state.
* **HOW:** Using the `fda` / `refund` R packages (`pfr` or `fRegress`), regress 24-month oral/gut $\alpha$-diversity and $\log(\text{F:B})$ on the aligned growth curves $X(t)$ and 24-month dietary covariates, and compare the functional weight function $\beta(t)$ to see which developmental window (e.g., 0–6 months vs. 12–24 months) drives the 24-month oral microbiome differences.

#### 3. Replace the Invalid `prop.test` Phylum Comparison with Compositional / Non-Parametric Paired Tests
* **WHY:** Multiplying the sample mean proportion $\bar{p}$ by $N$ subjects and passing $k = N\bar{p}$ into `prop.test` (line 140) treats continuous compositional percentages across subjects as a single binomial count of $N$ Bernoulli trials, ignoring between-subject variance and mother-child / within-child pairing.
* **WHAT:** Replace `prop.test` on line 56 and 140 with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (for paired child gut vs. child oral, and paired child oral vs. mother oral) or Dirichlet-multinomial / ANCOM / ALDEx2 compositional tests across individuals.
* **HOW:** For each phylum, apply `wilcox.test(p_gut, p_oral, paired = TRUE)` across the $n$ children with both samples, and `wilcox.test(p_child_oral, p_mom_oral, paired = TRUE)` across mother-child dyads, reporting Bonferroni-adjusted $p$-values.

#### 4. Correct for Post-Selection Inference (`bestglm`), Two-Tailed Testing, and Summed vs. Averaged Tree Merging
* **WHY:** (i) Running `lm` on the variables chosen by `bestglm` on the same dataset (Table 2) understates $p$-values; (ii) Figure 3 uses uncorrected one-tailed Mann-Whitney tests; and (iii) averaging rather than summing counts in Stage 2 of the phylogenetic merger (line 158) breaks count additivity across internal nodes.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Report the full 17-covariate multiple regression alongside Table 2 (or evaluate `bestglm` out-of-sample / via bootstrap selection stability).
  2. Report two-tailed Mann-Whitney U $p$-values and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR $q$-values in Figure 3.
  3. Sum (rather than average) read counts in both Stage 1 and Stage 2 of the phylogenetic tree merger (or use centered log-ratio transformations) so that merged clade abundances represent true clade relative abundances.
  4. Verify whether discarding unmerged paired-end reads (instead of joining them with `NNNNN` in lines 136–138) changes the Kraken taxonomic assignments.

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript audits the analytical transparency and raw data availability of the four initial publications describing the SARS-CoV-2 genome (references 1–4; Table 1) and demonstrates how open-source BioConda tools deployed on public Galaxy instances (across the US, EU, Belgium, and Australia) and Jupyter notebooks can reproduce and critically evaluate early outbreak genomic analyses. Operating on the six publicly available raw sequencing read datasets as of February 19, [YEAR] (S1 Table) alongside early consensus genomes, the authors execute a six-part open workflow archived on GitHub and Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.3685264`): (1) host-read and adapter filtering; (2) de novo assembly using `SPAdes` and `Unicycler` (recovering a 29,781-bp contig with 100% identity to `NC_045512`); (3) root-to-tip regression dating of the SARS-CoV-2 most recent common ancestor (MRCA) to October 24, [YEAR]; (4) intrahost single nucleotide variant (iSNV) calling at a $\ge 5\%$ minor allele frequency threshold (identifying 39 SNVs across the six samples, most notably a 38% allele-frequency A-to-C substitution in `SRR10903401` causing a `Lys921Gln` change in Spike HR1 that disrupts a predicted salt bridge with `Gln1188` in HR2); (5) recombination screening of the *S* gene via `GARD` (showing that the breakpoints proposed by Wu et al. [3] lack statistical support); and (6) branch-site positive selection testing via `aBSREL` along the ancestral branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 (reporting diversifying selection on ~7% of *S* gene sites; Figure 4).

As an open-science advocacy piece and rapid-response bioinformatics resource, the study is exemplary in its transparency, versioned workflow sharing, and concrete demonstration of why raw sequencing reads (rather than consensus FASTA files alone) are essential for outbreak quality control (noting, for example, that reference 4 cited `FreeBayes` as a de novo short-read assembler and that four later SRA uploads contained no usable viral reads). However, several of the specific evolutionary and genomic analyses performed in the paper suffer from methodological shortcuts that weaken their biological conclusions: (1) pooling reads across multiple patient datasets for de novo assembly (line 24) creates chimeric consensus sequences if samples differ; (2) estimating the MRCA via ordinary root-to-tip linear regression on deduplicated early genomes without confidence intervals or evolutionary rate priors (line 26) ignores phylogenetic non-independence; and (3) running `aBSREL` branch-site positive selection on an *S* gene alignment that was not partitioned at recombination breakpoints—across deep branches separating SARS-CoV-2 (`MN988688`), bat CoVs (`ZC45`, `ZXC21`, `Rp3`), and `SARS-Tor2` where synonymous sites ($d_S$) are saturated and recombination in the RBD is well documented—confounds recombination and synonymous saturation with positive diversifying selection along the terminal branch to SARS-CoV-2 (line 43). These analytical caveats can be addressed directly by partitioning the selection analysis and reporting uncertainty intervals on the molecular clock.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: Three of the four foundational SARS-CoV-2 genome publications failed to release raw sequencing reads and none reported software parameters, whereas open BioConda/Galaxy workflows can fully reconstruct the genome (`Unicycler` 29,781 bp, 100% identity to `NC_045512`) and audit data quality: [Well Supported].**  
  Table 1 and the accompanying GitHub/Zenodo workflows provide direct, verifiable evidence of the analytical gaps in references 1–4 (including the misattribution of `FreeBayes` as an assembler in reference 4 and the lack of raw read accessions in references 1, 2, and 4). Note, however, that in line 24 the authors state they preprocessed available read datasets *"and combined the resulting data sets"* prior to running `SPAdes` and `Unicycler`; while pooling reads from the same patient or identical early Wuhan isolates yielded a 29,781-bp contig matching `NC_045512`, pooling distinct patient samples for assembly is generally unsafe and should be distinguished from per-sample assembly.

* **Claim 2: Raw read analysis uncovers 39 intrahost SNVs ($\ge 5\%$ AF) across early samples, including a 38% frequency `Lys921Gln` variant in `SRR10903401` within Spike HR1: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Mapping raw Illumina reads from `SRR10903401` and applying strand-bias and base-quality filters at AF $\ge 5\%$ (lines 30–36, Figures 1–2) clearly detects the A-to-C transversion at 38% allele frequency corresponding to Spike `Lys921Gln` (homologous to SARS-CoV `Lys903`). The structural observation that `Lys921` in HR1 interacts with `Gln1188` in HR2 (note: a Lys–Gln interaction is a hydrogen bond between a charged amine and polar amide rather than a classic ionic salt bridge between oppositely charged residues, though often loosely termed a salt bridge/H-bond in coronavirus HR1–HR2 literature) and that alphacoronaviruses (TGEV, PRCV, CCV, FIPV, PEDV) carry `Gln` at this site is well documented. However, because `SRR10903401` is a metatranscriptomic BALF/clinical library, the authors should verify whether the 38% C allele occurs on amplicon/primer ends or low-complexity/homopolymer contexts, and correct the biochemical terminology ("hydrogen bond / polar interaction" vs. "salt bridge" with `Gln1188`).

* **Claim 3: Recombination breakpoints within the *S* gene previously reported by Wu et al. [3] lack robust statistical support in `GARD` analysis: [Well Supported].**  
  Re-analyzing the *S* gene alignment of SARS-CoV-2, Bat `ZC45`/`ZXC21`, and SARS `Tor2`/`SZ3` with `GARD` (line 38, Figure 3) demonstrates that while sequence similarity shifts across the *S* gene among this restricted 5-taxon panel, the heuristic scan reported by Wu et al. did not establish statistically supported breakpoint placement on that specific taxon set.

* **Claim 4: Branch-site selection analysis (`aBSREL`) provides statistically significant evidence of positive diversifying selection on ~7% of *S* gene sites along the ancestral branch leading to SARS-CoV-2: [Fragile].**  
  In line 43 and Figure 4, the authors run `aBSREL` on an unpartitioned full-length *S* gene codon alignment containing only five divergent lineages (`SARS-CoV-2 MN988688`, `Bat ZC45`, `Bat ZXC21`, `Bat Rp3`, and `SARS-Tor2`) without including closer sarbecovirus relatives (such as `RaTG13`, which was already public by February [YEAR]) and without partitioning for recombination across the S1/RBD vs. S2 subdomains. Across the deep evolutionary divergence separating `ZC45`/`ZXC21` and `SARS-Tor2` from `SARS-CoV-2` in the S1 receptor-binding domain, historical recombination among sarbecoviruses creates discordant gene trees between the RBD and the rest of *S*, and synonymous divergence ($d_S$) approaches saturation. Running a single-tree codon model (`aBSREL`) across a recombining region with a long terminal branch is well known to generate false-positive signals of episodic diversifying selection ($\omega > 1$ on ~7% of sites).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Partition the *S* Gene Codon Alignment and Include Closer Sarbecovirus Outgroups Before Testing Positive Selection
* **WHY:** Running `aBSREL` on a single unpartitioned *S* gene tree containing only `MN988688`, `ZC45`, `ZXC21`, `Rp3`, and `SARS-Tor2` (Figure 4) confounds historical sarbecovirus recombination (where the RBD of `ZC45`/`ZXC21` has a different phylogenetic history and a large deletion compared to `SARS-CoV-2` and `SARS-Tor2`) and long-branch $d_S$ saturation with positive diversifying selection along the `MN988688` branch.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the `aBSREL` analysis on recombination-free partitions of the *S* gene (and/or include `RaTG13` to break the long terminal branch to `MN988688`).
* **HOW:** (1) Split the *S* gene codon alignment into GARD-inferred non-recombinant segments (or separate the S1 NTD, RBD, and S2 domains, which have distinct phylogenetic topologies across `ZC45`/`ZXC21` vs. `Tor2`); (2) infer separate maximum-likelihood trees for each partition; (3) run `aBSREL` per partition both with and without `RaTG13` (`MN996532`); and (4) report whether the 7% positively selected site class on the `MN988688` branch persists or disappears once RBD tree discordance and branch length are controlled.

#### 2. Report Uncertainty Intervals and Substitution Rate Estimates for the Root-to-Tip MRCA Regression
* **WHY:** Line 26 reports a single point estimate for the SARS-CoV-2 MRCA ("October 24, [YEAR]") from root-to-tip regression on deduplicated sequences as of February 16, [YEAR], without reporting the number of unique sequences ($N$), the correlation coefficient ($R^2$), the inferred substitution rate (substitutions/site/year), or the 95% confidence interval around the intercept.
* **WHAT:** Provide the complete statistical parameters and uncertainty bounds for the root-to-tip regression.
* **HOW:** Report in line 26: (1) the exact number of deduplicated genomes analyzed ($N$), (2) the slope (substitution rate in $\text{subs/site/year}$), (3) $R^2$ and permutation $p$-value for temporal signal, and (4) the 95% confidence interval on the October 24, [YEAR] tMRCA estimate.

#### 3. Clarify Per-Sample vs. Pooled De Novo Assembly and Precise Biochemical Terminology for `Lys921–Gln1188`
* **WHY:** Line 24 states that the authors preprocessed the available sequencing read datasets *"and combined the resulting data sets"* before running `SPAdes` and `Unicycler`. Combining reads from different patients before de novo assembly can create artificial chimeras and masks sample-specific assembly failures. Additionally, line 30 describes the interaction between `Lys921` and `Gln1188` as a "salt bridge," whereas glutamine (`Gln`) is uncharged at physiological pH (forming a hydrogen bond, unlike glutamate `Glu` or aspartate `Asp` which form true ionic salt bridges with `Lys`).
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Clarify in line 24 which exact SRA accession(s) from S1 Table were assembled individually versus pooled, and report per-sample contig lengths and identities to `NC_045512`.
  2. In line 30, refine the phrasing to state that `Lys921` forms an inter-helical hydrogen bond / polar bridge (referred to as a salt-bridge-like interaction in [14]) with `Gln1188` in HR2.
